- **Ruling 2 — the `customers` write and the `customers` ADD COLUMN are dropped entirely.** An anonymous form with no possession proof was calling `CustomersRepository.upsert`, whose `existing.name = name` (`customers.py:89`) is justified only because every shipped caller proves possession by OTP first, and whose model class states the invariant in writing (`models/customer.py:11-13`). **Revised again 2026-08-03 after a second verification pass** (5 agents, 21 findings: 1 BLOCKER, 11 MAJOR, 9 MINOR). Two more **user rulings**, and the first one deletes code rather than adding it:

- **Ruling 3 — server-side dedup is removed entirely. There is no dedup key, no lock, no pre-check, no duplicate branch.** The round-2 blocker proved that Ruling 1's compromise was still a presence oracle in the **positive** direction: submitting a phone that *is* in the queue returned a distinguishable answer, **silently, free, and unbounded across days** — no row written, no evidence anywhere. And the argument that made it acceptable ("a write that leaves evidence") was false in **both** directions, because F33 ships **no staff view of the queue at all** — that is F58. Worse, the dedup index freed its key only on a status change or a soft delete, and F33 writes **neither**: one anonymous POST with a known mobile **denied that named woman a queue slot for the rest of the boutique day**, with no remedy anywhere in F33. So the create now **always creates a ticket and always returns it**. One response shape, no branch, nothing about anyone else. **The oracle is closed, not documented.** Deleted: the partial unique index, the advisory lock, the Python pre-check, the `IntegrityError`/lost-race path, the `{"ticket": null}` branch, the `CheckinCreateResponse` envelope, the per-phone create limiter, and every test of all of them. `queue_day` **survives** on new grounds (D4). `sessionStorage` **survives, re-cast** as a client-side courtesy that is explicitly not a security control (D8). A duplicate ticket is now a real, expected outcome, and F58 merges or removes it — that is the cost the ruling accepts.

Recorded as a first-class section («Deployment ordering») and as Risk 3b, the same shape Risk 3 already uses for F20.

The remaining round-2 findings are applied inline. Findings **dissolved** by Ruling 3 are named in the Decisions Log where they used to live rather than quietly dropped: the targeted day-long denial, the positive-presence oracle, the per-phone 429 oracle, the unwritable advisory-lock-namespacing test, and both Ruling-1 duplicate-branch findings. ## Problem

A bride walks through the door. From that second until a consultant happens to notice her, the product knows nothing: `bookings` records only people who booked ahead, and `bookings.checked_in_at` (F34, `0014_booking_check_in.py:37`) records arrival for a booking that already exists. There is no row anywhere for a person standing in a doorway who did not book.

The cost is two-sided and both sides are concrete. For the boutique she is an unrecorded, high-value lead — `e6-instore-realtime.md:19` calls this out as the reason pre-decided #26 exists at all. For her, the entire experience is standing near a counter trying to work out whether anyone has registered that she is there, and how long she is going to be doing it. That question — *am I in the queue, and where* — is the one the product can answer and currently cannot even hear.

F34 shipped the shift board, which converges every staff phone on server state within five seconds (`.planning/specs/shift-board-checkin.md`, D3/D4). It renders **bookings**, and its own D9 says so: "queue tickets are F33's … F33 will have to answer where its non-booking tickets appear" (`shift-board-checkin.md:278`). F33 is that answer's data layer. It ships the ticket and the customer-facing half; F58 ships the staff-facing panel and every action on it.

**This feature collects name and phone from a member of the public, unauthenticated, at a physical door.** That is the only genuinely dangerous thing in it, and it is why D5 (the unbundled opt-in), D13 (the collection notice) and Risk 3 exist. Everything else here is a table and a timer.

## Goal

A printed sign in the window carries one static QR (pre-decided #30) pointing at `https://{slug}.modryn.co.il/checkin`. She scans it, types a name, a phone and whether she is here for a wedding dress or an evening gown, and lands on a page that tells her **her position in today's queue and nothing else about anybody**. That page updates itself every five seconds, can be paused, stops on its own when her ticket closes, and survives a screen lock because her position lives in the URL and not in memory.

**A second submission creates a second ticket, and that is the decision** (Ruling 3). The server keeps **no dedup key of any kind**, so the create's response is **identical whether or not that phone was already in the queue** — it discloses nothing about any other person, and no request from a stranger can take a slot away from a named woman. A duplicate is a real, expected outcome; F58's panel merges or removes it. What keeps the ordinary re-scan from making a pointless second ticket is a **client-side courtesy**, not a server rule: `/checkin` on mount offers "you already checked in from this device — view your position" when this browser tab still holds a pointer from an earlier check-in (D8). That pointer lives in `sessionStorage`, **dies with the tab**, and is not a security control.

Position is **computed on read** and never stored (pre-decided #30, D3). The queue is FIFO by arrival; bride-priority ordering is an open product question and is not here (`e6-instore-realtime.md:74`).

**F33 ships one migration** — a single `CREATE TABLE`, no `ALTER TABLE` anywhere — **one new backend package, one new runtime dependency (`segno`), three new rate-limiter instances, one new error class, one new exception handler, two new storefront routes and one new manage section.** It ships **no** staff action on a ticket, no dispatch, no wall board, no wait-time estimate, **no server-side dedup**, and **no write to any table it does not own**.

**It also ships behind a deployment gate**: F33 merges and is fully tested, but is **not enabled for a live pilot tenant until F58 ships** the waitlist panel that can render and manage the queue (Ruling 4 — see «Deployment ordering» below).

## Design

### D1 — The POST lives on a fourth `/storefront` sibling router, in a new `app/queue/` package

```
app/queue/__init__.py
app/queue/router.py        the two public routes
app/queue/service.py       QueueService and the three limiters (no lock — Ruling 3)
app/queue/schemas.py       request/response models
app/queue/validation.py    the ticket-specific bounds and the throttle error
app/db/repositories/queue_tickets.py
app/models/queue_ticket.py
```

`app/storefront/router.py`'s module docstring makes four promises, and "GET only; HEAD stays a 405, deliberately" is one of them. Nothing in code enforces it — the enforcement is the social convention that mutations go to a sibling, stated in that same docstring, plus the explicit path literal in `tests/test_storefront_api.py:574-599`. F11 honoured it (`notifications/router.py:3-7`) and F13 honoured it (`booking/router.py:4-8`). F33 honours it.

**Both** F33 routes go on the sibling, including the read. That is not obvious — the position read is a GET-shaped question and the read router exists for GET-shaped questions — and D7 is where it is argued. The mechanical consequence is worth stating here because it removes four separate traps at once: because F33 registers **no new GET under `/storefront`**, the six guards in `test_storefront_api.py` that fan out over the derived `ROUTES` list (`:166-195`) are unaffected. Specifically:

- `ROUTES` substitutes only `{dress_id}` (`:186-192`). A new GET path parameter would have been left as a literal `{ticket_id}` in six parametrized URLs, and the first failure a reader would see is `test_no_route_requires_authentication` asserting 200 against a 400 — a cascade of six confusing red tests with one missing `.replace()` behind them.
- `test_the_read_throttle_is_not_inert` (`:1202-1214`) parametrizes over **every** GET under `/storefront`, installs a blocking limiter on `app.state.storefront_rate_limiter`, and asserts 429. A GET on a sibling router that does not depend on that limiter fails it with a 200, and the only fixes are to reuse the read router's budget (D6 says never) or to weaken the guard for the six routes it already protects.
- `_client()` (`:494-513`) stubs exactly one service. A new GET backed by a different service would reach the real one built in `create_app()` — a live session factory inside the fast, no-Docker suite.
- `FORBIDDEN_KEYS` (`:203-232`) is walked recursively over every GET response and contains `status`, `created_at`, `tenant_id`. F33's payload names `status`, which is fine on a POST — `BookingCreateResponse` already ships `status` on a public POST (`booking/router.py:112`) — and would have needed a rename or an argued edit to that frozenset on a GET.

None of the four is the *reason* for D7; they are the dividend. **Declined:** putting the POST on `app/storefront/router.py`. It silently falsifies an eight-paragraph contract that two features went out of their way to preserve, and no test catches it — the shadowing guard checks paths, not which router owns them.

**Registration** goes after `booking_router` in `create_app()` with the same shape of comment the other three carry (`main.py:1044-1057`), and `_register_spas(app)` stays last.

**No router-level throttle.** Unlike the read router, the sibling routers carry only `Depends(_no_store)`, and F33's three budgets are service-held for the reason D6 gives. `_no_store` is a **local three-line copy, not an import** — the shipped convention. **No ordinal**: `staff_router.py:21` calls itself "a third local three-line copy" and `dashboard/router.py:28` "a fourth", while seven files already carry one, so the running count in those comments is already out of step and F33 must not add a wrong number to it. F33 adds two routers, hence two copies.

**CSRF is structurally N/A on two independent grounds**, both of which the sibling routers already state: `CsrfOriginMiddleware` only inspects paths under `/manage`, and these routes read no cookie and carry no ambient credential, so there is nothing a cross-site request could ride. The cookie-blindness test (D-testing) is what keeps the second claim true.

### D2 — `queue_tickets`: every column, every CHECK, the one partial index — and **no unique index anywhere** (Ruling 3)

```sql

    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuid_generate_v4(),
    tenant_id UUID NOT NULL,
    created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
    updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ,

    marketing_opt_in_at TIMESTAMPTZ
```

Column by column, and the ones with no writer in F33 are named as such:

**One index, NON-unique, and it has one job — the position count's access path:**

```sql
CREATE INDEX idx_queue_tickets_tenant_day_active
    ON queue_tickets (tenant_id, queue_day)
    WHERE deleted_at IS NULL
```

**Ruling 3 deleted the unique index.** The old one was `UNIQUE … (tenant_id, queue_day, phone) WHERE deleted_at IS NULL AND status IN ('waiting','in_service')`, and it was the dedup rule. Two round-2 findings killed it and they are worth keeping in the file, because the second is the one a later reader will want to re-litigate:

- **It was a targeted denial-of-service on one named person.** The partial predicate freed its key only on a status change or a soft delete, and **F33 writes neither** (status transitions are F58's, the sweep is F20's). So one anonymous POST carrying a known mobile took that number's slot for the rest of the boutique day, and there was no remedy anywhere in F33 — no staff surface, no self-service, nothing.
- **The refusal it produced was a free, silent presence oracle.** Answering "already in the queue" *is* the answer a prober wants, it cost no row and left no trace, and it was repeatable across every day forever.

The replacement is the plain house shape: `WHERE deleted_at IS NULL`, the same partial predicate `0008_bookings.py:42-49` uses on `customers`. `(tenant_id, queue_day)` is the exact prefix the position count filters on (D3), so the count is an index range scan over one day's rows; the `status = 'waiting'` filter and the `COALESCE` comparison are applied over that handful. It also serves F59's board read and F20's sweep, which is why it is `deleted_at IS NULL` rather than the narrower live-status predicate — a sweep needs to see closed rows, and a second index for that would be one more thing to keep true.

**The pinned literals must be CAPTURED from the server, never transcribed from this document.** DDL of this shape was run on a live PostgreSQL 16.14 and the server stores something else:

```
CHECK ((visit_type = ANY (ARRAY['bride'::text, 'evening'::text])))
CHECK ((status = ANY (ARRAY['waiting'::text, 'in_service'::text, 'done'::text, 'removed'::text])))
CHECK ((skip_count >= 0))
```

`IN (…)` became `= ANY (ARRAY[…])`, every element gained a `::text` cast, the predicate gained parentheses and the table was schema-qualified. The strings above are an **illustration of the shape**, not a value to paste: `test_migrations.py` already reads definitions back through `pg_get_constraintdef` and `pg_indexes.indexdef` and says why at `:395`. Re-capture on the CI server — including `pg_indexes.indexdef` for the one index, which is the row that will fail loudly if someone re-adds `UNIQUE`.

**Declined:** any unique index or unique constraint on this table beyond the primary key. That is Ruling 3, and it is the whole of it. A later reader who wants one must first answer both bullets above: who frees the key when nobody in the shipped product can write a status, and what does the refusal disclose.
**Declined:** an index on `phone` (nothing looks a person up — F20's promotion sweep scans one tenant-day), on `status` alone (a four-value column on a small table), and on `called_at`.
**Declined:** a foreign key to `customers`, and a `customer_id` column. No FK is house rule (`architecture.md:40`). No `customer_id` because **F33 never touches `customers` at all** (Ruling 2) — there is nothing to point at. When F20 promotes a consenting ticket into a customer, it keys on `(tenant_id, phone)` and the ticket carries the same normalised phone, so the join exists without a column. The feature that needs the join adds it.
**Declined:** an index on `marketing_opt_in_at`. Nothing in F33 reads it, and F20's promotion sweep is a per-tenant scan over tens of rows per day. The feature that measures the query buys the index.
**Declined:** an `estimated_wait` column, or any wait arithmetic. Pre-decided #28 forbids it and there is no data to compute it from.

**A queue ticket is not a booking, and that is the point.** It has no `starts_at`, no seat, no terms acceptance, no manage token and no SMS. Folding walk-ins into `bookings` would have meant a nullable `starts_at` on the table whose two partial unique indexes are keyed on `(tenant_id, starts_at, seat_index)` (`0008_bookings.py:88-92`) — the oversell guard would have had to grow a "unless it is a walk-in" clause, which is exactly the quiet trap F34's D1 argues against in the other direction. Declined explicitly, and it is what keeps F33 distinct from F50's walk-in-with-a-booking path.

### D3 — Position is computed on read, ordered by `COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at)`

```sql
SELECT count(*) FROM queue_tickets
 WHERE tenant_id = :tenant AND queue_day = :day
   AND status = 'waiting' AND deleted_at IS NULL
   AND COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at) < COALESCE(:my_requeued_at, :my_created_at)
```

**`:day` is bound to the TICKET'S OWN `queue_day`, never to `today_jerusalem(clock)`.** The earlier draft left it unsaid, and the two readings differ in a way a customer sees: bind it to today and a ticket left `waiting` from an earlier day — which is the *normal* state of things until F58 can close one — counts zero earlier sort keys and renders **position 1**, telling someone who walked out yesterday that she is next. Bound to the ticket's own day, a stale ticket keeps reporting its true position within the queue it actually joined, which is at worst uninteresting and never a lie. The read has the row in hand, so this costs nothing.

`position = count + 1`, and **`position` is `null` whenever the ticket's own status is not `waiting`** — a ticket in service, done or removed has no place in a queue and reporting one would be a number with no referent.

**A duplicate ticket is visible here, and that is the honest consequence of Ruling 3.** Two tickets for one phone occupy two consecutive positions and each reports its own. Whichever page she is looking at tells her the truth about that ticket; F58 merges or removes the other. This is stated rather than smoothed over because it is the thing a reviewer will notice first.

Pre-decided #30 fixes this ("Queue position computed on read (arrival order within the day), never stored") and the reason is worth restating in one sentence because it is the decision most likely to be re-litigated by someone optimising: **a stored position must be renumbered on every insert and every removal.** Fifteen people in the queue and one is served means fourteen UPDATEs — under a lock, or with a race; and the race is not benign, because two writers renumbering concurrently produce duplicate or skipped positions that are visible to a customer as "you are 4th" on two different phones. The count query costs one index range scan over a handful of rows and cannot be wrong.

**`COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at)` rather than `created_at` alone** is what makes F58's skip a one-column write instead of a renumbering pass (`LOOP-STATE.md:220-223`). It ships in F33 even though nothing writes `requeued_at` yet, because the alternative is F58 changing the ordering semantics of a read F33 already published — and the ordering is the contract, not the column.

**Declined:** `ORDER BY created_at` with a separate `sort_key` column maintained by triggers (a stored position wearing a hat). **Declined:** a window function computing every ticket's position in one pass — the position read answers for one ticket and a `count(*)` is the whole answer; F58's panel, which does want the whole day ranked, can add `row_number()` when it has a screen to put it on.

**The ceiling, stated:** the count is O(waiting tickets in the day) per read, and the read runs every five seconds per waiting customer. At twenty waiting customers that is twenty index scans over twenty rows, four times a minute. This is fine at boutique scale and would not be at mall scale; the escape hatch is the same one pre-decided #23 names for the poll itself, and D6's per-ticket read budget is what bounds any one client meanwhile (the aggregate is bounded derivatively: live tickets are capped by the create ceiling, so worst-case read volume is ~6000/min per tenant — see D6).

### D4 — There is **no dedup**. `queue_day` is a stored `DATE` scoping the queue, not a Jerusalem expression over `created_at`

**Ruling 3: the create always creates.** No unique index, no advisory lock, no Python pre-check, no `IntegrityError` path, no duplicate branch on the wire. A second submission of the same phone on the same day mints a second ticket and returns it exactly as the first, so **the response is identical whether or not that phone was already in the queue**. That identity is the whole security property: there is nothing in the response, the status line or the timing that distinguishes the two cases, so a stranger who submits a woman's mobile learns **nothing about her** and **takes nothing from her**. The oracle is closed, not documented.

Three deletions and what each one used to buy:

1. **The per-tenant advisory lock** (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('checkin:' || :tenant_id))`) — deleted. The round-1 revision had already recorded it as load-free ("it buys nothing for correctness — dedup is a single-row uniqueness rule and the index is the whole answer"). With the index gone it has no job at all: the transaction is one INSERT that conflicts with nothing. Deleted with it: the db-marked test asserting the key was namespaced, which was in any case specified without a mechanism and would have hung the suite rather than failed it.
2. **The Python pre-check** (`active_today(tenant_id, phone=…, queue_day=…)`) — deleted, along with the repository method itself. It existed to route the ordinary double-scan away from the error path; there is no error path.
3. **The `except IntegrityError` wrapper and the lost-race outcome** — deleted. Nothing can raise it. The `0015`/`0016` migration therefore ships no unique constraint whose violation anyone must handle, and `app/queue/service.py` has no `try` block at all.

**What replaces dedup, and it is not on the server.** `/checkin` on mount reads a `sessionStorage` pointer written by the last successful create **in this browser tab** and, if present, offers a link straight to that ticket's position page. It costs zero server round-trips and the server has zero knowledge of it. Its reach is small and D8 states it honestly rather than overclaiming: it covers *navigate away and come back in the same tab*, and **not** "she closed the tab" and **not** a QR re-scan, both of which open a fresh top-level browsing context with an empty store. That is the trade Ruling 3 accepts: dedup moved to the one place where it discloses nothing and denies nobody, and it is weaker there.

**A duplicate ticket is a real, expected outcome.** It occupies its own queue position, and **F58 merges or removes it** — the panel that can see the queue is the surface that can fix it. Until F58 ships, nothing can (Ruling 4, «Deployment ordering»).

**Why `queue_day` survives, on new grounds.** It is no longer a key. It is the scope of "today's queue": D3's count filters on it, D2's index is prefixed by it, F59's board will read it and F20's sweep will key its cutoff to it. Without it the count would have to scope by an instant range over `created_at`, which is the same Jerusalem-arithmetic problem one layer down and with no stored answer to test against.

**Why it is a stored column and not `(created_at AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Jerusalem')::date`.** Both forms were probed against a live PostgreSQL 16.14, and the naive one really is illegal while the zone-named one really is not:

| Expression | Volatility | Indexable? |
|---|---|---|
| `created_at::date` — i.e. `date(timestamptz)` | **STABLE** (it resolves through the session `TimeZone` GUC) | **NO** — `ERROR: functions in index expression must be marked IMMUTABLE` |
| `(created_at AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Jerusalem')::date` — `timezone(text,timestamptz)` then `date(timestamp)` | IMMUTABLE ∘ IMMUTABLE | **YES — `CREATE INDEX` verified to succeed** |

Naming the zone explicitly is what removes the session dependency and makes the composition indexable, so **the expression was available**. (Ruling 3 weakened one leg of this argument and it is worth saying which: the index is no longer unique, so a disagreement between the stored index entries and a fresh lookup can no longer *refuse* a woman a ticket. It can still return the wrong rows to a count, which is a wrong number on a customer's screen.) **This is not "we couldn't".** Declined for three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It makes the day the database clock, and the database clock is untestable here.** `created_at` is `server_default now()` (`models/base.py`, the `_STANDARD` block at `0008_bookings.py:19`). Every service in this codebase takes an injectable `Clock` and every db-marked test pins fixed instants. An expression over `created_at` cannot be exercised at a chosen day, cannot be tested across the DST boundary, and can silently disagree with whatever `today_jerusalem(self._clock)` the service computed for the same request.
2. **Jerusalem arithmetic lives in Python in this repo, by rule.** `app/storefront/validation.py:1-9, 86-94` and `app/booking/validation.py:1-7` both say so; `owner.py` converts a calendar day to a half-open UTC pair in Python. An expression index would be the first and only piece of Jerusalem logic in SQL.
3. **`IMMUTABLE` is a marking, not a guarantee.** Postgres marks the conversion immutable so the index is legal; the conversion still depends on the IANA database, which `pyproject.toml:20-27` pins as a hard runtime dependency precisely because it is not ambient. If Israel's DST rules change and `tzdata` updates, already-stored entries were computed under the old rules while fresh lookups use the new ones — silently divergent scoping, no error, and a `REINDEX` nobody knows to run. A stored `DATE` has the same exposure only at write time, where the stored value is a decided fact rather than a recomputed one.

If a later reader reinstates the expression, the pinned literal in the migration test must be captured, not typed: Postgres deparses it as `(((created_at AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Jerusalem'::text))::date)` — extra parens and an injected `::text` cast on the zone literal that the source SQL does not contain. (Observed, not predicted. The same normalisation bites the shipped CHECKs too — see D2.)

**Declined:** re-introducing dedup as a soft rule (return the existing ticket, or refuse). Both spellings are the oracle Ruling 3 closed, in different clothes.
**Declined:** dedup at the client only, enforced by disabling the form when a pointer exists. `sessionStorage` dies with the tab and the offer is a link and not a redirect (D8), so this would be a lock that opens for anyone and closes on the one person it was for.

### D5 — Consent is `queue_tickets.marketing_opt_in_at`. F33 writes **no row in any table it does not own**, and `customers` is not touched at all

**Ruling 2, and it is a security decision before it is a schema decision.** The earlier draft added `customers.marketing_opt_in_at` and, on the ON branch, called `CustomersRepository.upsert(session, tenant_id, phone=…, name=…)` from an **anonymous form with no possession proof of any kind**. Two attacks followed directly, and one of them was written into this spec as a passing test:

- **Forged consent.** Anyone who knows any mobile number could create a `customers` row carrying a `marketing_opt_in_at` the product treats as evidence — the spec's own words were "the only proof of when she agreed" — and which D5 then forbade anyone from ever clearing. A forged timestamp would have been permanent and authoritative.
- **Name overwrite.** `customers.py:89` sets `existing.name = name` on purpose, justified as "she typed it on this booking, so it is the most recent thing she calls herself". That justification holds only because every shipped caller verifies the OTP first (`booking/service.py:200-206`). From this form it means a stranger renames a real customer. **The earlier draft asserted that overwrite as a test** ("ON with an existing customer keeps the first timestamp and updates the name"); that test is **deleted**, because the behaviour is now impossible.

`models/customer.py:11-13` states the invariant on the model class itself: customers rows are "created ONLY after OTP verification proved possession of that number". F33 has no OTP (`e6-instore-realtime.md:74` rules it out of scope, and D7 keeps that ruling), so **F33 has no business writing to `customers`.**

**Where consent lives instead:** `queue_tickets.marketing_opt_in_at TIMESTAMPTZ NULL`, part of D2's `CREATE TABLE`. F33 already owns that table and already stores `(tenant_id, phone, name)` on it, so the consent record costs **one column and no second migration half**. `NULL` means "no marketing consent on record for this visit" and is the only sentinel. No boolean beside it: a timestamp answers both "may we" and "when did she say so", and the second is the half that matters when the Spam Law question is asked in retrospect.

**The behaviour, both branches.** The form carries one checkbox, unchecked, **not** bundled with the submit action and not with the collection notice — Israel's Spam Law requires the marketing opt-in to be separate and unbundled (`architecture.md:20`, pre-decided #26). Its **label copy is gated with the notice** (D13); it is operative privacy-law text and the builder must not invent it.

- **OFF (the default):** the ticket's `marketing_opt_in_at` stays `NULL`. She remains — correctly — a person in a queue rather than a marketing contact. Amendment 13's minimisation duty is the whole reason this is the default.
- **ON:** `marketing_opt_in_at = now` on the ticket being inserted. One column in one INSERT. No second statement, no second table, no read-modify-write, **and after Ruling 3 no lock and no conflict handling either** — the create is literally one `INSERT`, and `app/queue/service.py` contains no `try` block.

**Three arguments the earlier draft needed have dissolved, and saying so is the point.** There is no `upsert` call, so the review's finding that F33 took a *different* advisory-lock key than the one `customers.py:79-82` documents as `upsert`'s precondition — and could therefore interleave with a booking-create on the same customer row and raise `IntegrityError` on `idx_customers_tenant_phone_unique` — has nothing left to apply to (and after Ruling 3 there is no lock at all). There is no read-modify-write, so the `IS NULL` guard is gone and with it the miscited `confirm_attendance` precedent (`bookings.py:278-298`, not `:204-224` which is `set_manage_token_hash`; and it is a guarded `UPDATE` predicate, never the Python read-then-write the draft prescribed). And **"first timestamp wins" now holds by construction**: F33 never updates a ticket, so there is no second write that could move it.

**A queue-ticket consent is UNVERIFIED, and this spec must not call it evidence.** Ruling 2 moved the forged-consent vector off `customers`; it did not close it. The form still has **no possession proof of any kind**, so anyone can tick the box against a stranger's mobile — the row it forges now lands on `queue_tickets` instead of on a permanent customer record, which is a smaller blast radius and nothing more. Under Communications Law s.30A the evidentiary burden is on the **sender**, and a row created by an unauthenticated POST evidences that *someone typed a number*, not that its owner agreed to anything. So: `marketing_opt_in_at` on a queue ticket is a **submission record, not a consent record**, and it **must not become a marketing permission without possession proof at F20's send time** — F20 has a verified phone in hand and is the only place that proof can be taken. The earlier draft called this "a per-visit evidence trail — arguably better evidence than a single mutable `customers` timestamp"; that sentence read as reassurance and is **deleted**, because it invited F20 to trust the relocated record.

**The hand-off to F20, recorded explicitly so nobody builds half of it here.** F20 owns marketing, can verify a number, and is the feature that decides how per-visit submissions become a person's consent: whether the newest tick wins, whether an unticked later visit means anything, what possession proof it requires before the first send, and what the `customers` row looks like. F33 hands it the per-visit rows and the promotion. **`customers.marketing_opt_in_at` is F20's column to add, in the feature that has a verified phone in hand.**

**Declined: adding `customers.marketing_opt_in_at` anyway, and leaving it unwritten.** `LOOP-STATE.md:1137` pre-authorised it, so this needs saying: an empty column on a table F33 never touches is a migration half, a model line, a downgrade branch and a test row, all to make a future feature's diff one line shorter. `architecture.md:52` will stay wrong for one more feature; conflict 7 records that.
**Declined: clearing consent when the box is left unticked on a later visit.** Structurally impossible now — each ticket carries its own stamp and nothing is shared — but the ruling behind it stands and F20 inherits it: an unticked box is not a withdrawal request, the Spam Law's withdrawal mechanism is an opt-out on the message itself, and silently revoking a March permission because she did not re-tick in August destroys the record of what she was actually asked. The opt-out surface is F20's.
**Declined: a `marketing_opt_in` boolean plus a separate date.** Two columns, one invariant, two chances to disagree.
**Declined: any use of the consent inside F33.** Nothing reads `marketing_opt_in_at` in this feature. It is written here because the form is where consent is given; the feature that sends marketing is the one that reads it.

**The three-way return the service needs is not needed here**, and stating that is the point: F33 performs exactly one INSERT — no second table, no guarded `UPDATE … RETURNING`. The `CheckInOutcome`-style outcome enum and the `populate_existing=True` re-read (`shift-board-checkin.md` D5, `bookings.py:403-429`) exist for a caller that must discriminate two causes of zero rows and render the winner's value. F33 has no such caller. **F58 will** — its take-next, push-assign and skip are all guarded conditional UPDATEs — and that is where the pattern belongs.

### D6 — THREE new `FixedWindowRateLimiter` instances, all held by `QueueService`; never a second key on an existing budget

| Instance | Key | Default | Meters | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `create_limiter` | `checkin:{tenant_id}` | 200 / 3600s | every well-formed create attempt | The runaway brake, **and after Ruling 3 the only bound on the create at all**. A pilot boutique sees tens of walk-ins a day, so 200/hour cannot fire on organic traffic and still stops a scripted flood. Keyed on the **tenant** — an operational fact about the boutique, which is the key the codebase says may answer 429 (`notifications/service.py:200-206`). |
| `position_ticket_limiter` | `checkin:position:{tenant_id}:{ticket_id}` | 30 / 60s | every position read for that ticket, hit **or** miss | **The per-client control, and it is the only one that bounds a legitimate caller.** A 5-second poll is 12/60s, so 30 is 2.5× headroom, and one leaked loop stops at its own ceiling instead of at the boutique's. It is the caller's own budget, keyed on a capability they already hold, so a 429 on it discloses nothing. |
| `position_miss_limiter` | `checkin:position:miss:{tenant_id}` | 120 / 60s | position reads that **MISSED** — and nothing else | The anti-walk brake. See below: this is the fix to a real defect, not a rename. |

**The per-phone CREATE limiter is DELETED, and the reasoning is recorded both ways because it is a close call.**

The case for keeping it: with dedup gone, nothing else caps how many tickets one number can create, and 10/hour is the number `booking_create_max_per_phone_window` already uses (`config.py:96-97`).

The case for deleting it, which wins on three counts:

1. **It answers 429, and a 429 on a phone key is a fact about one person disclosed to anyone who submits her number.** The codebase refuses that exact shape *in writing*: `notifications/service.py:200-206` says a tripped **phone** budget "would turn this endpoint into an oracle for 'is this number mid-booking at this boutique'… So it returns the same 204 as a real send and simply sends nothing", while the **tenant** ceiling raises `OtpThrottledError`. F33 cannot borrow the silent-success escape — every create must return a real, pollable ticket id, and a fabricated one would resolve to nothing. So the only two options were "429 and be an oracle" or "delete it". Ruling 3 exists to close an oracle; keeping one on the way out would be incoherent.
2. **It bounds almost nothing against an attacker.** A flooder varies the number for free — well-formed Israeli mobiles are a generated string — and is then bounded solely by the per-tenant 200/hour, which is where he was bounded anyway. The per-phone budget only forces him to type a loop.
3. **It bounds nothing against an accident either.** The realistic accident is a bride re-scanning three times; a 10/hour cap never fires on that. What actually stops the ordinary re-scan is D8's pointer and the re-entrant submit guard.

Net: one fewer instance, two fewer `Settings` fields, one fewer error path, and one fewer person-keyed 429 on an anonymous surface. **`checkin:phone:{tenant_id}:{phone}` appears nowhere in this feature.** The residual — one number can create tickets up to the tenant ceiling — is Risk 1, and it lands inside Ruling 4's deployment gate.

**The per-ticket read budget could not bound a hostile client, and the fix is a metering rule, not a fourth instance.** The earlier draft metered **both** read budgets on every attempt, hit or miss, and claimed that adding the per-ticket one "lets the per-tenant number go back to being the runaway brake rather than the only control". **That claim was false.** `FixedWindowRateLimiter.is_blocked` (`app/auth/rate_limit.py:53-55`) fires on a repeated **key**, and the ticket key comes from the attacker's own request body — a fresh random UUID per request never repeats a key, so the per-ticket limiter never fires on a walk, while every one of those requests charged the tenant key. One host at 50 rps therefore still 429'd every real bride's position page. The fix:

> **Consult and charge the per-tenant brake on MISSES ONLY.** A read whose ticket id resolves to a live row never touches it and rides its own 30/60s. A read that misses charges `checkin:position:miss:{tenant_id}`, and when that budget is spent, **further misses** answer 429 while hits keep answering 200.

A flood of guessed ids then denies only reads that no legitimate client makes. Two consequences, both stated rather than discovered later:

- **The aggregate hit ceiling is now derived, not declared.** Legitimate load is bounded by (live tickets) × 30/60s, and live tickets are bounded by the create ceiling at 200/hour — so the worst case is ~6000 reads/minute per tenant, which is exactly the number `storefront_read_max_per_window` already allows for the catalog (`config.py:152-164`). Recorded so nobody reads the absence of a per-tenant hit ceiling as an omission.
- **A spent miss budget converts a 404 into a 429, which changes the client's terminal decision.** A legitimate client whose ticket was swept normally gets 404 and **stops** (D10); arriving during a miss-flood it gets 429 and backs off instead, retrying at up to 60s until the minute-long window clears and the 404 arrives. Mildly wasteful, never wrong, and the alternative (404 regardless) would make the brake bound nothing.

**Metering the per-ticket budget on a MISS too is deliberate**: it is what bounds a walk that hammers **one** guessed id, which the miss brake alone would let through at the miss budget's rate. Two keys, two shapes of walk, both covered.

All three are **`QueueService` constructor kwargs**, not `app.state`. Eleven of the thirteen shipped limiters are service kwargs; only the two consumed by router-level dependencies live on `app.state` (`main.py:563, 592`). The ticket key needs the parsed body and the miss key needs the lookup result, both of which only the service holds, and putting the create budget beside them keeps the new router at `dependencies=[Depends(_no_store)]` — byte-identical in posture to `notifications/router.py:45` and `booking/router.py:67`.

**Both numbers of all three come from `Settings`** (`checkin_create_max_per_window` / `_window_seconds`, `checkin_position_max_per_ticket_window` / `_ticket_window_seconds`, `checkin_position_max_misses_per_window` / `_miss_window_seconds`), following the `storefront_read_*` and `booking_create_*` precedent, so they can be tightened during an incident without a deploy (`config.py:152-156` says exactly this). **Six** new `Settings` fields.

**Metering order on the create:** validate shape → normalise the phone → check the tenant budget → **record it** → open the transaction → INSERT. Recording before the write, unlike booking-create (`booking/service.py:198-217`, which records only after the OTP proof succeeds), and the difference is the whole reason: booking-create defers because an anonymous caller could otherwise drain a tenant's budget with garbage verification tokens. **F33 has no proof to fail.** Every well-formed check-in is a write, so metering the attempt *is* metering the write. Recording happens after shape validation so a blank-name 400 does not burn the boutique's allowance.

**Metering order on the read:** validate the body (pydantic UUID) → check and record `position_ticket_limiter` → look the ticket up → **on a miss only**, check and record `position_miss_limiter`, then 404. Recording on a miss is the anti-token-walk record, the pattern `booking/manage.py:102-117` establishes and whose docstring names the storefront read throttle as its own precedent.

**Why the read is metered at all.** The brief specifies two limiters, both on the create. The read gets its own pair for one reason specific to this feature: **the position read is the only endpoint in the product an anonymous client is designed to call every five seconds.** F34 declined a server-side limiter on its poll (`shift-board-checkin.md` D3) because "the only reachable victim is the feature it protects" — but that caller holds a live staff session and is CSRF-fenced. This one holds nothing but a UUID. And the client-side discipline that is supposed to bound it is *copied prose*: F34's review found a poll loop that survived unmount and ran forever, one orphan per navigation, and that was the **second** time that bug class shipped in this repo (`LOOP-STATE.md:104-121`).

**Why never a second key on an existing limiter, stated as the failure it causes.** `max_attempts` lives on the **instance**, so one instance is one ceiling however many keys it holds — `main.py` states this rule verbatim four separate times (`:637-639, 655-657, 673-677, 687-693`). This is also why the per-ticket read budget must be its **own instance** and not a second key on `position_miss_limiter`: sharing would give the ticket key the miss ceiling and the two would trip each other. Concretely, if F33 reused:

- **the OTP budgets** (`otp:tenant:{tenant_id}`, 100/3600s), a bride-heavy morning that spends the tenant's OTP ceiling would stop walk-ins from checking in — and the per-phone half of that budget answers a spent allowance with a **silent 204** (`notifications/service.py:218-219`), so the failure would be invisible. This is the exact failure `e6-instore-realtime.md:100` names for staff login, in the other direction.
- **the booking-create budgets** (`booking:create:{tenant_id}`, 300/3600s), a morning of walk-ins would exhaust the ceiling on the endpoint that takes actual bookings — the door queue would close the front door.
- **the storefront read brake** (`storefront:{tenant_id}`, 6000/60s), a leaked position-poll loop would 429 the catalog for every shopper on the site.

Also declined: **a per-IP component**. `storefront/router.py:103-134` argues it in full — per-IP keying needs `trust_forwarded_for`, whose two-hop Cloudflare caveat is unresolved until F21, and behind an untrusted proxy `request.client.host` is the proxy, so an IP key silently collapses to one bucket. Worse here than there: every walk-in in the boutique is on the same guest wifi.

**One new error class, `CheckinThrottledError`, for all three budgets**, mapped by one new `@app.exception_handler` to the shared `TOO_MANY_ATTEMPTS_BODY` verbatim (`main.py:137-139`). Its own class rather than a reuse of `StorefrontThrottledError`, for the reason `storefront/validation.py:45-54` gives about `RateLimitedError`: unrelated budgets, keys and operational meanings, and reparenting them all onto one base is F21's behaviour-neutral cleanup — `main.py:1011-1012` already says so for the ninth. **A tenth spelling of "too many attempts" is declined** — the shared body is the shipped answer for all **nine** existing throttles (`main.py:726, 775, 801, 811, 849, 869, 894, 916, 1015`), and F33 is the tenth handler returning that same literal. **All three of F33's keys are about a boutique or about a ticket the caller already holds; none is about a person**, which is what makes one shared 429 safe here where the OTP surface needed two answers.

**The client copy must not promise a number the body does not carry.** `TOO_MANY_ATTEMPTS_BODY` is `{"error": {"code": "TOO_MANY_ATTEMPTS", "message": "Too many attempts. Try again later."}}` — no duration — and F33 specifies **no `Retry-After` header**, because that would be a new response contract on a surface whose posture is byte-identical-to-the-shared-body. So the error table says *"an error inviting a later retry"*, never *"naming the wait"*: every window here is a `Settings` field precisely so it can change without a deploy, and a hardcoded Hebrew "try again in an hour" would silently contradict the next `.env` edit. **Declined: adding `Retry-After`.** It is the honest alternative and it is one line of contract, one storefront parse, one test and one more thing that can disagree with the body beside it — for copy that reads no better than «נסי שוב עוד מעט».

### D7 — Both public routes are **POSTs** with the ticket id in the body; the id is the capability, issued **once, at creation, to the creating device**; the create has ONE response shape and echoes no PII

```
POST /storefront/checkin            201  → TicketView  { id, status, position, called_at }
POST /storefront/checkin/position   200  → TicketView  { id, status, position, called_at }
```

**This overrules the brief.** The queue entry sketches "a public GET keyed by the ticket UUID" (`LOOP-STATE.md:229-231`) and the codebase rules the other way, in writing, twice: `booking/router.py:119-126` — "POST for a read, deliberately: a GET would put the token in the query string, and from there into every access log, proxy trace and Referer header on the path (D7)" — and `tests/test_storefront_api.py:592-595`, where the shadowing guard's own comment restates it as the reason all three manage routes are POSTs "INCLUDING the read". A path segment leaks into exactly the same places a query string does. Recorded as a program-state conflict, resolved codebase-first.

**The residual, stated honestly rather than argued away.** The ticket id still lands in one access-log line per page load, because the storefront route is `/q/{ticket_id}` (D8) and the SPA shell for that URL is served by the API's catch-all. What the POST buys is that the id lands there **once per page load instead of once every five seconds for the length of her visit** — at a 5s beat over a 30-minute wait that is 1 log line instead of 360, and it keeps the id out of the `Referer` header on every subsequent request the page makes. The same trade F16 already accepted for the manage token, which is in `/b/{token}` for the same unavoidable reason.

**Why the UUID is the capability at all, and what the alternative would cost.** The declined alternative is an OTP on check-in: send her a code, verify it, mint a token. `e6-instore-realtime.md:74` already rules it out of scope — "she is standing at the counter, so possession proof buys nothing and would cost an SMS per walk-in" — and the cost is not hypothetical: it is one SMS segment per walk-in per day, on a metered line (Q15), plus a ~30-second round trip in a doorway, plus a dependency on the sender-ID registration that is still unfiled (`external-applications.md`). A v4 UUID is 122 bits of entropy; guessing one is not a threat model, and a walk is bounded by D6's miss brake anyway.

**The create has ONE response shape, and that is Ruling 3.** Every well-formed create mints a ticket and returns its `TicketView`. There is **no `CheckinCreateResponse` envelope, no `ticket: null`, no `outcome` field and no second branch of any kind** — the earlier draft's `{ "ticket": TicketView | null }` is deleted, envelope included, because with one outcome an envelope is a wrapper around nothing.

**State it plainly, because it is the security property the ruling bought:** the response to a create is **identical in shape, in status code and in content-class whether or not that phone was already in this boutique's queue**. A stranger who submits a woman's mobile receives a ticket of his own and learns **nothing whatsoever about her** — not that she is here, not that she is not, not her position, not her id. There is no distinguisher in the body, none in the status line (201 always), and none in the work done (one INSERT either way). Ruling 1's compromise still had one — the presence answer was free, silent and unbounded across days — and that is what Ruling 3 removed. **The oracle is closed, not documented.** There is consequently no residual-disclosure paragraph here and no corresponding sub-risk; Risk 1 records what remains, which is junk tickets and nothing else.

**And it is what makes "the id is the capability" true instead of self-contradictory.** A capability the server will re-issue to anyone who guesses the phone number that owns it is not a capability. The id is handed out **exactly once, at creation, in the response to the request that created it**, and by no other server path ever. D8's `sessionStorage` pointer is a *client-side* copy of that one issuance, not a second issuance.

**What `TicketView` carries — the complete list, and it is four fields:**

```jsonc
{
  "id": "…uuid…",          // the capability, issued once, at creation, to the creating device only
  "status": "waiting",     // waiting | in_service | done | removed
  "position": 3,           // 1-based among that ticket's own queue_day's WAITING tickets; null unless status == "waiting"
  "called_at": null        // ISO-8601 UTC once a manager calls her forward (F58 writes it)
}
```

**What it deliberately does not carry, each with its reason:** her **name** (she typed it; echoing it back over an anonymous endpoint keyed by a guessable-in-principle id is a PII disclosure for zero product value — the page can show the name she typed from her own form state on the create, and on a reload it simply does not need it); her **phone** (same, worse); her **`visit_type`** (nothing renders it); **`created_at`** (the freshness signal is the client's own last-fetch time, per D12); **any other ticket** (no names, nothing about anybody else); **`tenant_id`, `queue_day`, `skip_count`, `requeued_at`** (operator provenance, the rule `storefront/router.py`'s `public_terms` projection states).

**The queue length is NOT withheld, and the earlier draft claimed it was.** That claim is deleted: `position` on a fresh create *is* the live waiting count plus one, by D3's definition, so anyone with a throwaway number learns a named boutique's waiting count — bounded only by the 200/hour create ceiling. The field stays (it is the entire product), the false non-disclosure goes, so a later reader does not build on a guarantee that never existed. F59's public wall board discloses strictly more, deliberately.

`status` on a public POST response is shipped precedent — `BookingCreateResponse` carries it at `booking/router.py:112`. (`status` is in `FORBIDDEN_KEYS`, but that frozenset is walked over GET responses only (`tests/test_storefront_api.py:669-684`), which is one more dividend of D1/D7 rather than a reason for them.)

**`position` and "ahead count" are one field, not two.** The brief names both (`LOOP-STATE.md:230-231`). They satisfy `ahead = position − 1` by construction, so shipping both is one invariant with two chances to disagree; the copy does the subtraction if the design wants that sentence. Recorded as a deliberate narrowing.

**201 on the create, always.** `booking/router.py:78` declares `status_code=201` on the sibling create. Here there is only one outcome, so there is only one code — which is also, for free, a status code that cannot leak anything.

**What Ruling 3 costs, named.** A woman who checks in twice ends up with two tickets, and only F58 can tidy that. A woman on a second device cannot find her first ticket at all — she gets a *new* one, which at least tells her a true position rather than telling her nothing. Both are real; both are cheaper than a request from a stranger being able to take her slot or confirm her presence. **Declined: an OTP to restore cross-device recovery** — the same SMS-per-walk-in cost `e6-instore-realtime.md:74` already ruled out, now paid on the unhappy path.

**The two mutating-surface postures the sibling routers already assert, F33 asserts too:** anonymous (no auth dependency anywhere), tenant-from-Host and never from client input (`get_current_tenant(request)` as the first statement in each handler, never a `Depends()` — the shipped shape at `notifications/router.py:52` and `booking/router.py:82`), cookie-blind (byte-identical response with a valid owner cookie attached), `no-store` at router level, and GET stays a 405.

### D8 — Two storefront routes on the hand-rolled router: `/checkin` and `/q/{ticket_id}`

`apps/storefront/src/router.tsx` gains, in the four places `RouteName` demands:

```ts
export type RouteName = … | "checkin" | "queuePosition";

export type RouteMatch =
  | …
  // Opaque and NOT validated here — an unknown, expired or mistyped ticket has
  // to reach the page so the page can render its own not-found state.
  | { name: "checkin" }
  | { name: "queuePosition"; ticketId: string };

const DOC_TITLE_KEYS: Record<RouteName, string> = {
  …, checkin: "document.checkin", queuePosition: "document.queuePosition",
};

const QUEUE_PATH = /^\/q\/([^/]+)$/;
```

`/checkin` is an exact `===` match beside `/about` and `/accessibility` (`router.tsx:86-87`). `QUEUE_PATH` is matched **before the unconditional `return { name: "catalog" }`**, and that ordering is load-bearing for the identical reason the `/b/{token}` matcher states at `router.tsx:92-96`: a rotated, swept or mistyped ticket must reach the page's own not-found state, never be silently swallowed into the dress grid. The id goes through `decodeId` (`router.tsx:74-82`) so a stray `%` 404s as a ticket rather than throwing out of render and blanking the page.

**`/q/{ticket_id}`, short, and disjoint from F59's `/queue`.** `^\/q\/([^/]+)$` cannot match `/queue` and the exact `/queue` match F59 adds cannot match `/q/…`; noted here so F59 does not have to rediscover it.

**Why the position lives in the URL, and what `sessionStorage` adds beside it.** The URL is the durable location: she locks her phone, reloads, or comes back to the tab, and `/q/{id}` still resolves. Memory-only state (BookPage's verification token shape) loses her ticket on the first reload, so that was never an option.

**`sessionStorage` holds a same-DEVICE, same-TAB courtesy pointer. It is explicitly NOT a security control and it is not dedup.** After Ruling 3 there is no server dedup at all, so this pointer's only job is to stop an *ordinary* re-scan from producing a pointless second ticket. On a successful create the storefront writes the new ticket id; `/checkin` reads it **on mount, with zero server round-trip**, and if it is present offers a link to `/q/{id}` **above** the form. The form stays fully usable underneath it — nothing is disabled, nothing is redirected.

**State its real reach honestly, because the earlier draft overclaimed it in three places and the Goal was one of them.** `sessionStorage` is scoped to the **top-level browsing context** and is destroyed when that tab closes. Therefore:

| Case | Covered? |
|---|---|
| She navigates away inside the tab (`/about`, back button) and returns to `/checkin` | **yes** — this is the case the pointer exists for |
| She reloads `/checkin` in the same tab | **yes** |
| She **closes the tab** and reopens the site | **no.** The earlier draft called this "the whole recovery mechanism for the case … she closed the tab on her own phone", which is exactly backwards |
| She **re-scans the printed QR** | **no.** An OS or camera QR open creates a NEW browsing context, which starts with an **empty** store rather than inheriting one. This is the *ordinary* re-entry path for this feature, so it is the most important row in the table |
| Any other device | **no** — and by construction, since the pointer is on a device and the server holds nothing |

So the honest summary is: **it covers navigate-away-and-back in the same tab, and nothing else.** Every uncovered case now ends in a *second ticket* rather than in a refusal, which is precisely why Ruling 3 could afford a mechanism this weak. Risk 11 carries the same statement in the customer's words.

Four things about it, and the first is not grep-dodging:

- **`localStorage` is banned by `frontend/scripts/qa-greps.sh:33`** — `check "no favorites" 'favorit|localStorage|heart' "$SRC"`, run through a bare `grep -rnE` over `apps/storefront/src` with **no comment stripping** (`:17`, `:23`) — a bundled ban whose *intent* is favourites-as-`localStorage`, with no exemption mechanism. `sessionStorage` does not match that pattern (verified against the real script), **and it is also the semantically correct scope**: one tab, one visit, gone when the tab closes — which is the honest lifetime of a courtesy pointer. If the ban had been on the *capability* rather than on the literal, this feature would still want session scope. **The new module's own comment must state the positive rule without naming the banned API** — see the Frontend-changes table for the wording; the argument for the choice lives here, in D8, not in the source file.
- **The key is a bare constant, `"checkin:ticket"`.** The earlier draft specified `checkin:{tenant_slug}` and **that is unbuildable**: the storefront has no slug anywhere. `BoutiqueResponse` carries `name` with the explicit comment "The tenant's display name, not the slug" (`apps/storefront/src/api.ts:225-227`), and `grep -rn slug apps/storefront/src` returns only comments and no value (verified). A builder following the old text would substitute the owner-mutable Hebrew display name, so a rename would silently orphan every live pointer. A constant is correct because `sessionStorage` is **already partitioned by origin** and every boutique is its own subdomain — origin partitioning, not the key, is what provides the isolation, which the earlier draft conceded in the same sentence that specified a tenant component.
- **It is an offer, never an auto-redirect, and it is labelled as what it is.** The link copy says **"the last check-in made from this device"**, never "your position". On a shared phone or a door tablet — the mother checking in her daughter, then the next arrival — the slot holds *someone else's* ticket, and a link labelled "your position" would hand a stranger a live capability over that visit while claiming it was hers. A mount-time navigation would be worse: a context change she did not request, into someone else's ticket.
- **Every successful create OVERWRITES the pointer**, so it always names the most recent check-in from this tab. That is also the whole of "clear it when a different phone is submitted": since Ruling 3 makes **every** create succeed, there is no branch in which a submit leaves the previous phone's pointer standing. No phone number is ever stored — the pointer is a ticket id and nothing else.

The terminal states (D10) **remove the key** — a 404, a malformed id, or a `done`/`removed` status means the pointer is spent and must stop being offered.

**The `/checkin` page must bind `useBoutique()`'s loading and error arms, and this is a privacy requirement rather than a polish one.** D13's two counsel-gated strings interpolate `{{boutique}}` from the layout's single fetch, and `StorefrontLayout` renders `{children}` **unconditionally** (`:128-130`) with a context default of `{ boutique: null, loading: true, error: null }` (`:40-45`). So an unguarded page renders the collection notice with a **hole where the data controller's name belongs** on first paint — and permanently, if `/storefront/boutique` fails (a suspended tenant answers 404 `TENANT_NOT_FOUND`; the read router's own budget, `app/storefront/router.py:103-134`, is not one of F33's three and can 429 it independently). Amendment 13 requires the notice **at the moment of collection**, and a notice that does not name the controller is not that notice. The contract, following `AboutPage.tsx:96-104` and `:106-127` verbatim:

- **`loading`** → render the page heading and `Skeleton`s. **The form is NOT rendered**, so there is no collection point and nothing to give notice about.
- **`boutique === null` after loading** → render the heading and the shell's boutique-unavailable shape: a `role="alert"` paragraph through `errorMessageOr(...)` and the retry `Button`. **The form is NOT rendered.** Degrading to a nameless form would be the one failure mode this decision exists to prevent.
- **loaded** → the full form, notice and opt-in, with `{{boutique}}` interpolated.

The named `CheckinPage` test asserts the rendered notice **and** the opt-in label each **contain the fixture's boutique name** — an assertion that fails against a blank, against a literal `{{boutique}}`, and against a form rendered in either degraded arm. The earlier draft's assertions ("visible text… not behind a disclosure… not `aria-hidden`") all hold against the broken sentence, which is why they are not enough.


**The page must not set `document.title`, and must not move focus on mount either.** `router.tsx:258-273` is one effect that does three things: `document.title = t(DOC_TITLE_KEYS[match.name])`, `window.scrollTo(0, 0)`, and `document.getElementById(MAIN_ID)?.focus()`. React flushes a child's passive effects before its parent's, and the Router is the parent (`App.tsx:5-13`, `StorefrontLayout.tsx:128`'s `<main id={MAIN_ID} tabIndex={-1}>`), so **anything either new page writes to the title or focuses on mount is silently overwritten one tick later** — both titles are Hebrew and the Router's focus target is `<main>`, which is the WCAG-correct landing, so the override is invisible and the dead page-level code reads as working. `BookPage` carries the title half of this warning at `router.tsx:54-56`. The focus half matters concretely: the `h2 tabIndex={-1}` shape this spec cites for the **manage** side (`StaffSection.tsx`) must **not** be ported to `QueuePositionPage`'s mount. Page-level focus moves are legitimate only in response to a **later user action** — which is exactly D9(b)'s failure-path effect.

**The ticket id must never appear in `document.title`.** No existing comment states this rule: `router.tsx:58-59` reads "One title for all six manage states. She arrived from a text message, and an outcome ('cancelled') does not belong in the tab strip" — it is about outcome copy, not about the token. **F33 is establishing the rule**, and the router test asserting the id never reaches `document.title` is the only thing that will hold it. Owned honestly rather than borrowed.

**How the two pages PROVE they do not touch `document.title` — the sentinel form, because the earlier draft\'s wording was vacuous.** It said "assert the title is whatever the Router set", and storefront page tests never mount the Router: `ManageBookingPage.test.tsx:83-89` renders `<StorefrontLayout><ManageBookingPage token={TOKEN} /></StorefrontLayout>`, so `router.tsx:258-273` never runs and there is no Router-set value to compare against. A builder who instead renders through `<Router />` to satisfy the wording writes an assertion that **cannot fail**, because the parent effect flushes after the child\'s and overwrites anything the page wrote — the green-but-vacuous class Risk 4 forbids. The correct form is already shipped in this repo at `router.test.tsx:70` (`document.title = ""` in a `beforeEach`): **set `document.title` to a sentinel, render the page in isolation, assert it is unchanged after mount.** The `document.activeElement` half is already non-vacuous when the page is rendered directly and keeps its wording.

**The phone field ships a format hint.** `normalize_israeli_mobile` accepts several spellings and 400s the rest (`notifications/validation.py:25, 31-48`), and the rejection happens to a member of the public standing in a doorway. `checkin.phoneHint` is passed as `Input`'s `help` prop, mirroring `BookPage.tsx:1240-1241` — `Input.tsx:20-24` folds `help` into `aria-describedby`, so it is announced **with** the field rather than sitting beside it. SC 3.3.2 is satisfied by the visible label alone, so this is a quality floor rather than a conformance one; the sibling surface already closed it.

**Both pages carry the page-owned bottom gutter.** `hasBookingBar()` is catalog-and-dress only, so a new route reserves no space for the fixed `A11yMenu` trigger, which stays pinned at the block-end inline-end corner at every width. Both new pages use the identical container class the two existing non-catalog routes use (`BookPage.tsx:50-53`, `ManageBookingPage.tsx:31-36`) so the surfaces read as one product.

**Layout, briefly** (the design pass owns the arrangement; this is the contract it may not shrink):

- `/checkin` — one `h2` under the shell's `h1`, the **courtesy link when the tab's `sessionStorage` pointer exists** (above the form, an offer not a redirect, labelled *the last check-in made from this device*), three fields (`Input` name, `Input` phone with `help={t("checkin.phoneHint")}`, a two-option `visit_type` chooser reusing the storefront-local radio-composite pattern `SizeChips`/`TypePicker` established), the **collection notice** (D13) above the opt-in, the **unbundled opt-in `Checkbox`** below it carrying the gated `checkin.optIn` label (D13), one primary submit at `size="md"` (`min-h-11`; `sm` is `min-h-9` = 36px and under the floor). Validation surfaces **on the forward press only** — never on blur, never on input — all messages set at once, focus to the first failure, no request issued: the shipped house rule at `BookPage.tsx:566-592`. Submit is guarded by a re-entrant boolean, not by `disabled`, because React commits `disabled` asynchronously and a fast double-tap on iOS fires two clicks inside one frame (`BookPage.tsx:769-772`).
- `/q/{ticket_id}` — a discriminated-union view state (`loading | live | notFound | failed`), the `ManageBookingPage.tsx:41-49` shape. The live arm shows the position as a large number, her status in words, the pause control (D11), and the non-announced freshness line (D12). `called_at` set is its own visual state and its own sentence — "go to the counter" is the most valuable thing this screen can say and the only reason `called_at` is read in F33 at all.

**Numerals get `<bdi dir="ltr">`; any owner- or customer-authored text gets a bare `<bdi>`** — `dir="ltr"` on Hebrew is itself a bidi defect. i18next interpolation cannot carry a `<bdi>`, so the copy splits into lead + isolated value + tail rather than one `{{placeholder}}` string, exactly as `ManageBookingPage.tsx:127-138` does.

**No client-side constant mirrors a server bound.** The form reuses the shipped `validation.ts` helpers — `validateName` (`MAX_CUSTOMER_NAME_LENGTH`) and `normalizePhone`/`validatePhone` — so `MIRRORS` in `backend/tests/test_frontend_constant_parity.py` gains **no row** and the parity test is untouched. `visit_type` is a two-value closed set with no length bound. If a later reader adds a client-side numeric bound, it must be `export const NAME_IN_CAPS = <digits>;` **and** a `MIRRORS` row, or it drifts silently in both directions.

### D9 — The storefront poll copies F34's mechanisms by **copying**, and the copy must include both `0c7015a` fixes

F57 **has merged (PR #33, 2026-08-03)** and its extraction is now on `main`: the shared hook is `apps/manage/src/lib/usePoll.ts` (324 lines). That path is inside `apps/manage` and is not reachable from `apps/storefront`; the only shared location that *is* reachable is `packages/ui`, which the storefront already depends on. So the choice is real: promote the hook into the design-system package, or copy it.

**COPY FROM `lib/usePoll.ts`, NOT from `BoardSection.tsx`.** This changed under this spec after F57 merged and it changes where the builder should read. Every *mechanism* D9 lists — the arming site, the backoff, the generation discipline, the `{401,403}` terminal set, the unmount guard — now lives in `usePoll.ts`, already extracted, already carrying the `0c7015a` fixes and the reasoning in comments. What REMAINS in `BoardSection.tsx` is the *presentation* this spec also cites: the freshness-key derivation, the pause control and the announced region. Read both, from the locations re-captured below; do not reverse-engineer an inline loop that no longer exists.

**Copied**, and D13-of-F34 is the rule being applied rather than dodged: an abstraction whose second caller lives in a different app, behind a different API client, is not the second caller that makes an extraction reviewable. Concretely, the two callers share ~60 lines of timer discipline and share **nothing** they are wired to:

- two different `ApiError` classes (`apps/manage/src/api.ts` and `apps/storefront/src/api.ts` declare their own; the storefront's is `credentials: "omit"` by contract),
- two different terminal predicates (`{401, 403}` there; `{404, VALIDATION_ERROR, closed-status}` here — D10),
- two different mutation stories (the board mutates and must suppress ticks; this page never mutates at all, so `mutationsRef` and the whole re-arm-after-mutation rule are dead code here),
- two different copy sets, two different pause semantics (D11 declines the idle stop), and two different i18n bundles.

A hook parameterised over all of that would be longer than both copies and would put React data-fetching logic in a package whose review surface is the design system. **Declined**, and stated so a later reader does not "fix" it.

**What the copy must carry — the six mechanisms, minus the two that have no subject here:**

1. **Schedule-after-settle, one arming site.** `schedule(ms)` clears any pending timer, refuses to arm when stopped or `document.hidden`, and is called only from a request's `.finally()` or a user intent. At most one request in flight per tab **by construction** (`usePoll.ts:147-161`). Not `setInterval` + `AbortController`.
2. **One monotonic `generationRef`,** captured once per load and compared at three points — the success branch, the catch branch, **and the `.finally()` re-arm**. Missing the compare in the `.finally()` lets a superseded load arm a *second* timer and the at-most-one property is gone.
3. **`tickRef` updated on every render with no dependency array,** so the timer always calls the latest closure and reads current state without mirroring every field into a ref (`usePoll.ts:116, 201`).
4. **`document.hidden` guarded twice** — in `schedule()` and in `tick()` — and `visibilitychange` back to visible bumps the generation and fetches **immediately** rather than waiting out an interval. Honest rather than an optimisation: browsers already throttle background timers to ≥1/minute, so an unpaused loop looks live while being a minute stale.
5. **Failure backoff, 5s doubling to a 60s cap, reset on the first success.** Three lines in the `.finally()` that already arms the tick. Unlike F34, there **is** a server-side per-client ceiling here (D6's 30/60s per ticket) — but it is 2.5x a healthy poll, so an outage still means every open ticket page retrying every five seconds against a sick server until it trips. The client backoff is what keeps a sick server from being hammered, and the server budget is the floor under a client that forgets to.
6. **The terminal branch** — D10.

Not copied, because they have no subject: mutation-in-flight suppression and its re-arm (this page never mutates), the pointer-hold skip (there is no list to grow under a finger), the stranded-row rescue, and the scroll-once guard.

**The two `0c7015a` fixes are not optional, and the reason is the same for both: axe cannot see either of them.**

**(a) The unmount guard.** The cleanup must set `runningRef.current = false` **before** `clearTick()` (`usePoll.ts:172-173, 224-233`). `clearTick()` alone cancels only the timer armed right now; the arming sites are `.finally()` callbacks that run *after* the cleanup when a request is in flight, and nothing in `tick → load → finally → schedule` touches React state, so an orphaned loop cannot be broken by unmounting. On the board this leaked one permanent 5-second request loop per navigation away, for the rest of a 12-hour session. **On the storefront it is worse in one specific way**: the board's caller is a staff member on a device that gets closed; this caller is a customer whose phone sits in a bag, and the orphan is an *anonymous* loop against a public endpoint. This one line is also what makes "at most one poll in flight per tab by construction" true rather than aspirational, and it is the reason D6's read budget is a floor and not the primary control.

**(b) Failure-path focus restoration.** Any control that carries `loading={busy}` is `disabled={disabled || loading}` inside `@boutique/ui`'s `Button`, so the browser blurs the tapped control the instant the request starts. The success path is usually compensated; the failure path usually is not, and focus sits on `<body>` with the sentence she needs to read nowhere near it — WCAG 2.4.3, Level A, which pre-decided #38 makes a **legal** bar here. It must be a `useEffect` keyed on the error state, **not** a `.focus()` call in the `catch`: the alert node does not exist yet when `setError` runs, so the call in the catch is a no-op on nothing (`BoardSection.tsx:194-201`, and `BookingDetail.tsx:103-115` fixed the same class before it). This bug class has now shipped **twice** in this repo and axe passed over it both times — axe cannot see a focus move that never happened.

On F33's surfaces this applies to the check-in submit (a rejected create must land focus on the error), and to the pause/resume control (D11), whose name changes under the user's focus.

### D10 — F33's terminal set, derived from its own contract, is **not** F34's `{401, 403}`

The board's terminal set exists because a staff session can be deactivated (→401) or demoted (→403). **The storefront carries no session at all**, so both codes are unreachable here and copying them would ship a branch that can never fire while missing the branches that can. F33's terminals:

| Condition | Terminal? | What the client does |
|---|---|---|
| `404 NOT_FOUND` — unknown, soft-deleted or retention-swept ticket id | **TERMINAL** | Stop the loop; render the not-found state with a link back to `/checkin`. Polling a ticket that does not exist is a request that can never succeed. |
| `400 VALIDATION_ERROR` — the path segment is not a UUID | **TERMINAL** | Same state. A malformed id cannot become well-formed. |
| **200 with `status` in `{done, removed}`** | **TERMINAL** | Stop the loop; render the closed state. **This is F33's real terminal and it has no HTTP analogue at all** — a successful response ends the loop. Without it every served customer leaves a phone polling a finished ticket until the tab closes. |
| `429 TOO_MANY_ATTEMPTS` | not terminal | Back off per D9(5). Every budget here is a fixed window, so a 429 is transient by definition — including the miss brake's, which is the one case where a terminal 404 is briefly deferred (D6). |
| 5xx / network / timeout | not terminal | Back off; keep the last good position on screen and mark it stale rather than blanking. Stale-and-labelled beats empty. |

The success-terminal is the one a reviewer should check hardest, because it is the only place in the product where a 200 stops a loop, and because getting it wrong is invisible: the page keeps working, it just never stops. It gets a named frontend test.

**Nothing in F33 can produce it, and that is a fact about the whole feature, not a note on this table.** F33 writes only the `waiting` default (D2); every status transition is F58's, no staff surface exists, and the sweep is F20's. So **every ticket F33 creates stays `waiting` indefinitely and every open tab polls until it closes** — Risk 6 treats that as the edge case, and until F58 ships it is the *only* case. Two consequences:

- **This is one of the three findings behind Ruling 4** («Deployment ordering»). A feature whose only end condition is unreachable is not a feature you enable for a live pilot tenant.
- **The tests that cover the success-terminal must seed the closed status themselves.** The frontend test already does by construction — it stubs the API client and can answer `{"status": "done"}` on the third tick. Any *backend* or e2e assertion must set the status at the **service or repository layer** rather than driving it through a product path, because no product path exists. No test may be written as "call the API until it goes `done`"; that test would hang.

**Every terminal also clears D8's `sessionStorage` pointer.** A 404, a malformed id and a closed status all mean the stored ticket is spent, and leaving it behind makes `/checkin` keep offering a courtesy link into a dead page. One line in the terminal branch, and its own assertion in the page test.

**Declined: a maximum poll lifetime** (a wall-clock cap after which the page gives up). The closed-status terminal is the honest end condition; a timer would end the loop while she is still waiting, which is the one moment the page must not go quiet.

### D11 — SC 2.2.2: a user-operable pause on the position view. **No idle stop**, and what replaces it

**This is a legal bar, not a preference.** Pre-decided #38 makes IS 5568 / WCAG 2.0 AA a legal requirement (`interview-2026-07-30.md:146`, `design-config.md:22`, `architecture.md:20`), Level A sits inside AA, and **SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide** requires a mechanism *for the user* to pause, stop or hide auto-updating content, or to control its frequency. A page repainting every five seconds is squarely that. **axe has no 2.2.2 rule** — the criterion needs human judgement about what auto-updates — so this ships green in CI and non-conformant in law unless it is specified here and asserted by a named test. F34 hit exactly this and its test file carries an explicit instruction not to delete the pause assertions as redundant with the axe row.

**The mechanism: one button whose accessible NAME changes.** Never two buttons, never `aria-pressed` (a control that changes both its name and its pressed state reads to assistive tech as two contradictory facts). It sits **before** the auto-updating content in the DOM, next to the freshness line, for the reason F34's comment gives: a 2.2.2 mechanism placed after the content it governs is reachable only by walking content that repaints under the walk.

**The 44×44 floor is pinned as `size="md"`, and the test asserts a class, not a measurement.** jsdom has no layout engine and no Tailwind pipeline (`apps/storefront/vitest.config.ts:9`), so `getBoundingClientRect()` is 0 and `getComputedStyle().minHeight` is empty — **any measurement assertion on this screen is vacuous**. The pause control and the submit are both `<Button size="md">` (`min-h-11` = 44px), and the assertion is `toHaveClass("min-h-11")`, matching the working precedent at `apps/manage/src/__tests__/BoardSection.test.tsx:507-512` and the control it tests at `BoardSection.tsx:389-393`. `size="sm"` is `min-h-9` = **36px, under the floor**, and it is the natural pick for a small utility control sitting inline with a `text-sm` freshness line — which is exactly why the prop is pinned here rather than left to the design pass. **`min-h-11` is min-HEIGHT only**: the ×44 width half is carried by the rule below — a **text label, never an icon alone** — and is stated here so nobody reads the class assertion as covering both axes. Visible focus ring (`focus-visible:outline-focus`), asserted in the same test. Pausing stops the loop; **resuming fetches immediately** and then resumes the interval — the `visibilitychange` behaviour reused rather than reinvented — and resets the backoff, because a resume is a fresh user intent and not a continuation of a backed-off retry.

Pause and resume are announced once through the `role="status"` region (D12 admits them: they are user-initiated), and the resume string exists separately because a screen reader does not reliably re-announce the name of an already-focused control that renamed itself.

**Declined: the idle stop.** F34 ships one, and porting it here would be wrong. That board is a staff tool on an unattended counter tablet, where the idle stop closes a real exposure (a self-refreshing list of named brides on a screen nobody is watching) and where inactivity genuinely means nobody is there. **This page is a customer watching her own position while doing nothing** — inactivity is the expected state and the whole use case, and a timer that stops the updates after ten quiet minutes would stop them at precisely the moment she is still waiting and has stopped touching her phone. The three jobs F34's idle stop does are covered here by other means: the unattended-display exposure does not exist (the screen shows one number about her); the sustained-load half is covered by `document.hidden` (locking a phone fires `visibilitychange`) plus D6's per-ticket budget; and the loop's real end is the closed-status terminal (D10), which is a fact about her visit rather than a guess about her attention.

**Declined: a frequency picker.** 2.2.2 is satisfied by any *one* of pause / stop / hide / control-frequency, and a dropdown of intervals is a settings surface and a persisted preference for a criterion one button closes.
**Declined: relying on `document.hidden`.** It is automatic; a mechanism the user cannot operate is not "a mechanism for the user". This is exactly what makes the gap easy to miss — the loop really does pause, and the criterion is still failed.
**Declined: relying on the browser's tab controls.** 2.2.2 asks for a mechanism *in the content*.

**Residual, named:** a phone left face-up on a table with the tab visible and a ticket staff never close polls until the tab closes. `document.hidden` covers the common case and D6's 30/60s per-ticket budget caps each loop — but **nothing in F33 writes `done` or `removed`**, so in this feature every ticket is that residual, not an edge case. F58's writes close it, which is why Ruling 4 gates the pilot on F58. Recorded as Risk 6 and in «Deployment ordering».

### D12 — The poll never writes into `role="status"`; the freshness line is visible, reachable and **not** announced

A `role="status"` written by a five-second poll announces the page to a screen-reader user every five seconds, forever, and passes every automated check. The rule is mechanical: **the announced region carries only user-initiated outcomes** — pause, resume, the create's success or failure, the terminal states — and **never a poll tick**, including a tick whose value is unchanged (assigning an identical string still replaces the Text node, which is why a naive single-tick test passes against the broken version).

The one exception that is not an exception: **a change from `waiting` to called is a user-relevant event, not a poll artefact, and it must be announced.** It is the one thing this page exists to tell her and she may not be looking at the screen. It is announced **once**, on the transition — guarded on the previous value, not written on every tick that observes the same fact.

**The freshness line is visible, in the reading order, and NOT `aria-hidden`.** F34's design review took the same decision (its F-1 ruling, accepted into that spec's a11y floor) for a reason that applies harder here: `aria-hidden` would make the page's only honesty signal sighted-only, so a screen-reader user could never learn the position stopped updating twenty minutes ago. It sits outside every live region. Past tense, the shipped register: «עודכן 14:07» says *this was true at 14:07*, never «בזמן אמת», which a poll cannot keep even for one interval (`apps/manage/src/i18n/he.ts:464-470`).

**Three freshness strings, not one — because one string makes the state legible only by colour, which this same paragraph forbids.** The page has three distinguishable conditions: live, **user-paused** (D11), and **backed-off/stale** (D10's non-terminal branch). The earlier draft shipped a single string and distinguished paused from stale by a class swap, which is "status signalled by colour alone" reproduced inside the rule against it. The shipped precedent distinguishes them with distinct **sentences** and derives the key in one line (`BoardSection.tsx:320`):

```ts
const freshKey = stopped ? "checkin.pausedAt" : stale ? "checkin.staleAt" : "checkin.updatedAt";
```

— the same precedence and the same reason (`BoardSection.tsx:315-320`: a stopped loop cannot fail a tick, so the stop is the cause in force and the resume control is the remedy). The three Hebrew values follow `he.ts:469-470, 484`'s register — «עודכן {{time}}» / «אין עדכון מאז {{time}}» / «מושהה · עודכן {{time}}» — all past tense. **A named test asserts the rendered TEXT differs across the three states**, not just the class; a class-only assertion is the defect it is supposed to catch.

**No shimmer, no pulse, no flash on refresh.** The same rule serves `prefers-reduced-motion` for free.

**The rest of the a11y floor, non-negotiable:** one `h1` (the shell's) and an `h2` for the page · 44×44 on the pause control, on the submit and on the opt-in row · the opt-in is a **native checkbox with its role left alone** (`@boutique/ui`'s `Toggle` renders `role="switch"`, which is the wrong semantic for a consent, and the whole row is the hit target) · a visible focus ring on every control and focus never dropped to `<body>` (D9(b)) · status never signalled by colour alone · `<bdi>` per D8 · 375 / 768 / 1440 with no horizontal scroll · the collection notice is real readable text at the point of collection, not behind a disclosure.

### D13 — The collection notice: a neutral interim Hebrew paragraph, in one named slot, with the swap recorded

`in_run_gates` F33 is **open and stays open** (`LOOP-STATE.md:1094-1099`). It blocks one string, not the feature — the F19 precedent (`:1079-1084`). The gate's own brief for the eventual text: *name the boutique, the purpose (queue position + being called), the retention window* — **and, added by this revision, the exception for opted-in contact detail, which F20 promotes into `customers` and nothing deletes.**

**TWO gated strings, not one, and the second was missing from the earlier draft.** `checkin.notice` is the collection notice; **`checkin.optIn` is the checkbox's own label**, which is the operative consent text under the Communications Law s.30A. The opt-in string names **who sends** (the boutique, interpolated), **by what channel** (SMS), **about what** (promotions, new collections, events) and **how to stop** — the four things that make the unbundling legible in the label itself and not only in the notice beside it. `{{boutique}}` interpolates the name `StorefrontLayout` already fetched once for the whole app and publishes through `useBoutique()` (`StorefrontLayout.tsx:55`) — no second fetch, in either string. **Its null states are not optional and they are D8's**: the layout renders children unconditionally with a `{ boutique: null, loading: true }` default, so `/checkin` must withhold the whole form on `loading` and on `error` rather than render a notice with a hole where the controller's name belongs. (Under Ruling 2 it is at least a promise about **one table F33 owns** on the default path, rather than a promise contradicted by a permanent `customers` row the same form created.) Two things follow, and the second is the actionable one: the clause stays (the gate explicitly asks for a retention window, and a notice that omits retention is worse than one whose retention is enforced late), and **F33 must not be enabled for a live pilot tenant before F20's sweep exists**. That is one of the two deployment gates on this feature — «Deployment ordering» below carries both, and Risk 3 owns this one. Neither blocks this PR.

**Declined: shipping no notice at all until F20 clears.** Amendment 13 requires notice *at the moment of collection*, and this form is the moment. Collecting a name and a phone from a member of the public behind silence is the one thing this feature may not do.
**Declined: inventing check-in-specific privacy wording as final text.** That is privacy-law text, the Q1 user-only class, and the E6 risk register's trip-wire says so in as many words (`e6-instore-realtime.md:102`). The interim is labelled interim, in the spec and in the run report.
**Declined: putting the notice behind a "read more" disclosure.** Notice at the moment of collection means visible at the moment of collection.

### D14 — The printable QR: server-side `segno`, one JSON route on a new `/manage` router, both roles

**One new route, on the EIGHTH `/manage` router, in `app/queue/manage_router.py`:**

```
GET /manage/checkin-qr  →  { "checkin_url": "https://bella.modryn.co.il/checkin", "qr_svg": "<svg …>" }
```

It is the `app/dashboard/router.py` shape verbatim — the smallest shipped `/manage` router: `dependencies=[Depends(_no_store), Depends(require_role(...))]` at router level, a local three-line `_no_store` copy, one service getter off `request.app.state`, one `Annotated` alias, one handler taking `request` and calling `get_current_tenant(request)`. No audit row, no body, no rate limiter.

**Registration, which the earlier draft never stated for this router.** `app.include_router(queue_manage_router)` goes in `create_app()` **after `gateway_router` and before `storefront_router`** (`main.py:1043-1046`), keeping every `/manage` router contiguous and ahead of the anonymous surfaces. It carries the same numbered shadowing comment every sibling carries (`main.py:1020, 1024, 1029, 1034, 1039`) — **"The EIGHTH"**, naming `tests/test_checkin_qr_api.py`'s `ROUTES` table as what keeps the eight-way prefix honest. The count is checkable, and it was wrong before: `main.py`'s own running commentary numbers dashboard "The sixth /manage router" and gateway "The SEVENTH", plus `auth_router` at `prefix="/manage/auth"` — **seven already mount `/manage`**, and F33's is the eighth. (F33's *public* router is separately the **fourth** `/storefront` sibling, which `main.py:1051-1054` numbers the same way; that count was right.)

**The URL is composed on the SERVER, and this is not a preference.** `manage_link()` (`booking/comms_templates.py:74-81` — re-captured; a round-2 finding proposed `:73-80` and that is off by one, `def manage_link` is at 74 and its `return` at 81) is the only place in the backend where a slug and a domain compose into a URL, and F33 adds its exact sibling beside it:

```python
def checkin_link(*, slug: str, base_domain: str) -> str:
    return f"https://{slug}.{base_domain}/checkin"
```

Pure, module-level, keyword-only, unit-testable with no app. `base_domain` is injected into the service at construction from `Settings` (`main.py:645-652` is the shipped shape), never read from a global inside a handler; `slug` comes from `get_current_tenant(request).slug`, which the Host header bound. **The frontend cannot do this**: `slug` appears nowhere in `apps/manage` and `GET /manage/settings` does not return it, so a client-side composition would have to guess from `window.location.origin` — correct today only because the console is same-origin with the storefront, and silently wrong the day it is not.

**JSON carrying the SVG source, not an `image/svg+xml` response.** The console renders it as `<img src={"data:image/svg+xml;utf8," + encodeURIComponent(qr_svg)} alt={…} />`. Reasons, in order:

- `apps/manage/src/api.ts`'s `apiFetch<T>` unconditionally ends in `(await response.json()) as T` — an SVG response cannot go through it, and a bespoke `fetch().text()` beside it is a second HTTP path in a file that has one.
- The page needs the **human-readable URL as text** anyway (a printed QR with no legible URL beside it strands anyone whose camera fails), so an image-only response needs a second request regardless.
- An `image/svg+xml` route would be the **first non-JSON response in the entire API** (`main.py` imports only `FileResponse` and `JSONResponse`), and with `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` stamped on every response by the outermost middleware, getting the media type wrong means the browser refuses to render it — a failure mode with no precedent to copy.
- A `data:` URI in an `<img>` renders the SVG in an image context: no scripts, no external references. Strictly safer than an inline `<svg>` or `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, which is the shape a reviewer would otherwise have to argue about. There is no CSP today (deliberately deferred to F21, `security_headers.py:19-24`), so neither is blocked — the choice is made on merit, not on what the headers allow.

**The dependency: `segno`, added to `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` together.** CI runs `uv sync --locked` as its very first step, so a `pyproject` edit without a regenerated lockfile fails before lint or tests: run `uv add segno` in `Backend/` and commit **both** files. Pure Python, no transitive dependencies, no native build. **It ships `py.typed`** (verified against 1.6.6 in a scratch venv), so **no `[[tool.mypy.overrides]]` block is needed** and the boto3 precedent at `pyproject.toml:66-70` does not apply. **The exact call, because both obvious spellings are wrong and one of them fails silently.** Verified against segno 1.6.6:

## Deployment ordering — F33 merges, but is NOT enabled for a live pilot tenant until F58 ships

**Ruling 4.** This is a first-class constraint on the feature, not a footnote on a risk. It does **not** block this PR: F33 is built, tested and merged normally. What it blocks is turning the QR sign around in a real shop.

**Three findings collapse into it, and each one alone would be enough:**

1. **Nothing in F33 renders a queue ticket to staff.** The waitlist panel is F58's (`/manage/floor`), and F33 explicitly ships "no staff action on a ticket". So a junk ticket — from a script, from a griefer, or from Ruling 3's ordinary duplicate — lands in a table **no shipped surface displays**. Risk 1's original mitigation ("a junk ticket costs nothing but a line on a screen a staffer can close") named a screen that does not exist.
2. **There is no staff remedy for anything.** A duplicate cannot be merged, a no-show cannot be removed, a served customer cannot be closed. Every one of those is an F58 verb.
3. **The poll has no reachable end condition.** D10's success-terminal fires on `status ∈ {done, removed}`, and F33 writes only the `waiting` default. Every ticket stays `waiting` and every open tab polls until it is closed. The load is bounded (D6, `document.hidden`) but the *product* is not finished until something can end a visit.

**Together with the retention gate already recorded at D13/Risk 3, F33 carries TWO deployment preconditions:**

| Gate | Blocks | Discharged by | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| No staff surface, no remedy, no terminal | enabling the QR for a live tenant | **F58** shipping the waitlist panel | team, at F58's merge |
| The notice promises a retention window nothing enforces | enabling the QR for a live tenant | **F20** shipping the retention sweep | team, discharged by F20 |

**What "not enabled" means mechanically**, so this is checkable rather than aspirational: the printed sign is not produced (`/manage/checkin-qr` may be reachable in the console — it renders a URL and a picture — but no poster goes in a window), and no pilot tenant is told the URL. There is no feature flag and F33 does not build one: a flag is a surface, a setting and a migration to enforce a constraint that is discharged by two named merges. **Declined: a `queue_enabled` tenant setting.** The loop's own ordering is the control, and F58 is next in this epic.

## Decisions Log

- **D1 — The POST and the position read both go on a fourth `/storefront` sibling router in a new `app/queue/` package, registered after `booking_router` with `_register_spas` still last.** `app/storefront/router.py` is contractually GET-only and nothing enforces it but the sibling convention F11 and F13 both honoured (`notifications/router.py:3-7`, `booking/router.py:4-8`). Registering no new GET also leaves the six `ROUTES`-derived guards in `test_storefront_api.py` untouched — the `{dress_id}`-only substitution, the throttle-not-inert parametrization, the single-service `_client()`, and the `FORBIDDEN_KEYS` walk are all dividends, not reasons. Declined: adding either route to the read router (falsifies an eight-paragraph contract, and no test catches it).
- **D2 — `queue_tickets` with `queue_day DATE`, `name`, `phone`, `visit_type` (2-value CHECK), `status` (4-value CHECK, default `waiting`), `called_at`, `requeued_at`, `skip_count`, and ONE NON-UNIQUE partial index `(tenant_id, queue_day) WHERE deleted_at IS NULL`.** **Ruling 3 deleted the unique index**, and the reason is kept in the file because it is what a later reader must answer before re-adding one: its partial predicate freed a key only on a status change or a soft delete, and F33 writes **neither**, so one anonymous POST with a known mobile took that number's slot for the whole boutique day with no remedy anywhere in the shipped product — and the refusal it produced was a free, silent, unbounded presence oracle. The replacement is the plain house predicate `0008_bookings.py:42-49` uses, prefixed exactly as D3's count filters, and it also serves F59's board and F20's sweep. `called_at` and `requeued_at` ship with F58 as their writer because F33 **reads** them (the called state, and the ordering); `skip_count` ships because `LOOP-STATE.md:241-243` promises F58 needs no migration. `test_migrations.py` asserts the table carries **no unique index but the primary key**, so re-adding one reddens a test rather than quietly restoring both defects. Declined: any uniqueness, a second index (nothing measures one yet), `customer_id` and any FK (the phone is the join, and no-FK is house rule), an `estimated_wait` column (#28), and folding walk-ins into `bookings` (the oversell guard's two partial unique indexes are keyed on `starts_at`).
- **D3 — Position is `count(*) + 1` over the ticket's OWN `queue_day`'s waiting tickets, ordered by `COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at)`, computed on read, `null` when the ticket is not waiting.** **`:day` binds to the ticket's `queue_day`, never to `today_jerusalem(clock)`** — the earlier draft left it unbound, and with *today* a ticket left `waiting` from an earlier day (the normal state until F58 can close one) counts zero earlier sort keys and renders **position 1**, telling someone who walked out yesterday that she is next. Pre-decided #30. A stored position must be renumbered on every insert and removal, and two concurrent renumberings produce duplicate or skipped positions visible to a customer on two phones. The `COALESCE` is what makes F58's skip a one-column write instead of a renumbering pass, and it ships now because the ordering is F33's published contract. Declined: a maintained `sort_key`, and a window function (F58's panel, which wants the whole day ranked, can add one).
- **D4 — There is NO server-side dedup. `queue_day` is a stored `DATE` scoping the queue, not `(created_at AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Jerusalem')::date`.** **Ruling 3**: no unique index, no advisory lock, no Python pre-check, no `IntegrityError` path, no duplicate branch on the wire — the create is one `INSERT` and `app/queue/service.py` has no `try` block. The create's response is therefore **identical whether or not that phone was already in the queue**, which is the security property: nothing distinguishes the two cases in the body, the status line or the work done, so a stranger learns nothing about a named woman and cannot take her slot. Ruling 1's compromise still leaked in the positive direction — the presence answer was free, silent and unbounded across days, and its "leaves evidence" defence was false in both directions because **F33 ships no staff view at all**. The cost, accepted: a duplicate ticket is a real, expected outcome, and F58 merges or removes it (Ruling 4 gates the pilot on F58 for exactly this). What replaces dedup is a `sessionStorage` courtesy on the device (D8), whose reach is *one tab* and which is explicitly not a security control. **`queue_day` survives on new grounds**: it is the scope of today's queue for D3's count, D2's index, F59's board and F20's sweep. The stored-column-not-expression argument stands on three legs — an expression makes the day the database clock and untestable against the injectable `Clock`; Jerusalem arithmetic lives in Python by rule (`storefront/validation.py:1-9`, `booking/validation.py:1-7`); and `IMMUTABLE` is a marking rather than a guarantee, so a `tzdata` update silently diverges stored entries from fresh lookups. Both forms were run on a live PG 16.14: `created_at::date` really is illegal (`date(timestamptz)` is STABLE), the zone-named form really is not, so **this is a choice and not an inability** — though Ruling 3 weakens leg three, since a non-unique index that disagrees returns a wrong number rather than refusing a woman a ticket. Declined: reintroducing dedup in any spelling (refuse, or return the existing ticket — both are the oracle), and enforcing it client-side by disabling the form (a lock that opens for anyone and closes on the one person it was for).
- **D5 — Consent is `queue_tickets.marketing_opt_in_at TIMESTAMPTZ`, set at INSERT from the unbundled default-OFF checkbox. `customers` is NOT touched, and `customers.marketing_opt_in_at` is NOT added.** **Ruling 2**, and it is a security decision: `CustomersRepository.upsert` from an anonymous form would let anyone who knows a mobile number forge a permanent consent record *and* overwrite that customer's stored name (`customers.py:89`), a write defensible only because every shipped caller verifies an OTP first (`booking/service.py:200-206`) and forbidden in as many words on the model class (`models/customer.py:11-13`). The earlier draft asserted the name overwrite as a **passing test**; that test is deleted along with three others that asserted `customers` behaviour. Consent on the ticket costs one column and no second migration half; "first timestamp wins" holds **by construction** because F33 never updates a ticket, which also removes the `IS NULL` guard and its miscited precedent (`bookings.py:278-298` is `confirm_attendance` and is a guarded UPDATE predicate, not the Python read-modify-write the draft prescribed; `:204-224` is `set_manage_token_hash`). The advisory-lock/`upsert`-precondition conflict dissolves with the call. **Ruling 2 RELOCATED the forged-consent vector; it did not close it** — the form still has no possession proof, so anyone can tick the box against a stranger's mobile and the row simply lands on `queue_tickets` now. So the word **"evidence" is deleted** from this spec: a queue-ticket tick is a **submission record, not a consent record**, and it **must not become a marketing permission without possession proof at F20's send time**. The earlier draft's "arguably better evidence than a single mutable `customers` timestamp" read as reassurance and invited F20 to trust the relocated record. Declined: adding the column anyway and leaving it unwritten (a migration half, a model line, a downgrade branch and a test row to shorten a future diff), clearing consent by omission (now structurally impossible; the ruling passes to F20), a boolean beside the timestamp, and any read of the tick inside F33. **F20 owns the column, the promotion into `customers`, the possession proof, and how per-visit submissions become a person's consent.**
- **D6 — THREE limiter instances, all `QueueService` constructor kwargs, all env-tunable: create per-tenant (200/3600s), position per-TICKET (30/60s), position MISS-brake per-tenant (120/60s).** `max_attempts` is per **instance**, so one instance is one ceiling however many keys it holds (`main.py:637-639` and three restatements). **The per-phone create budget is DELETED**: a 429 on a phone key is a fact about one person handed to anyone who submits her number, which `notifications/service.py:200-206` refuses in writing (a tripped phone budget returns the same 204 as a real send, precisely to avoid that oracle) — and F33 cannot borrow the silent-success escape, because every create must return a real pollable id. It also bounded almost nothing: a flooder varies the number for free and lands back on the tenant ceiling, and a 10/hour cap never fires on a bride re-scanning three times. **The per-ticket read budget could not bound a hostile client and the fix is a metering rule, not a fourth instance**: `is_blocked` fires on a repeated **key** (`auth/rate_limit.py:53-55`) and the ticket key comes from the attacker's own request body, so a fresh UUID per request never repeats and never fires — while every such request charged the tenant key, which is how one host at 50 rps still 429'd every real bride. So the tenant instance is re-purposed: **consult and charge it on MISSES ONLY.** Hits ride their own 30/60s and never touch it; a walk of guessed ids denies only reads no legitimate client makes. Two consequences stated rather than discovered: the aggregate hit ceiling is now *derived* (live tickets × 30/60s, bounded by the create ceiling at ~6000/min, the same number the catalog already allows), and a spent miss brake converts a would-be 404 into a non-terminal 429, deferring a terminal by up to a window. The per-ticket budget is charged on misses too, which is what bounds a walk that hammers one guessed id. Reusing the OTP budget would let a bride-heavy morning silently close the door queue; reusing booking-create's would close the front door; reusing the storefront read brake would let a leaked poll 429 the catalog. Create meters on attempt (unlike booking-create, because there is no proof to fail). One new error class, the shared 429 body verbatim — the **tenth** handler returning it (`main.py:726, 775, 801, 811, 849, 869, 894, 916, 1015`) — zero new codes, and **no `Retry-After`**. All three keys are about a boutique or about a ticket the caller already holds; **none is about a person**, which is what makes one shared 429 safe here. Declined: per-IP keying (`trust_forwarded_for` is unresolved, and every walk-in is on the same guest wifi), a `Retry-After` header, and a per-phone budget in any form.
- **D7 — Both public routes are POSTs; the position read carries the ticket id in the BODY; and the create has ONE RESPONSE SHAPE.** The POST-not-GET half **overrules the brief's GET** on the codebase's own written rule (`booking/router.py:119-126`, restated at `tests/test_storefront_api.py:592-595`): a path segment lands in access logs, proxy traces and `Referer` exactly as a query string does; the residual (one log line per page load, because the SPA route is `/q/{id}`) is stated, and the POST turns 360 log lines per visit into 1. **The one-shape half is Ruling 3.** Every well-formed create mints a ticket and returns its `TicketView`, 201, always — **no `CheckinCreateResponse` envelope, no `ticket: null`, no `outcome` flag, no branch**.
