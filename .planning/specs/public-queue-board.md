## Scope fence

F59 **only reads**. It ships:

- one **read** endpoint on F33's shipped `app/queue/router.py`,
- one repository method and one service method, both read-only,
- one storefront route (`/queue`), one page, one poll loop, one pause control,
- one new `FixedWindowRateLimiter` instance and its two `Settings` fields,
- an **amendment to one shipped interim i18n value** (D13) and new `queueBoard.*` keys in `he.ts` + `ar.ts`.

It ships **NO**:

- **migration** — see D2. `queue_tickets` already carries every column. If a builder finds they need one, that is a significant finding and §D2 says what to do with it.

- **staff surface.** The board is public, anonymous and on the storefront. `/manage` gains nothing.
- **ticket id on the wire.** D7 — this is the single most important omission in the feature.
- **new error code, new exception handler, new `AuditAction`, new dependency, new lockfile churn.**

## Problem

A woman is sitting in the salon. She scanned the QR at the door, typed her name and her phone, and her own phone now says «3» (F33's `/q/{ticket_id}`). That works — for her, on her phone, if her phone has battery and she has not put it in her bag.

What she actually does, in a room, is look up and try to work out whether anything has happened. Today the only way to answer *how many are ahead of me* is to ask a member of staff, which is the interruption the whole floor programme exists to remove: the brief's own framing is that a consultant's attention is the scarce resource in a bridal boutique, and «כמה עוד?» is the cheapest possible way to spend it. Pre-decided #27 deferred the kiosk board as "a small follow-up if the pilot asks for it"; the 2026-07-31 ruling revived it at full scope in the same breath that revived F33 (`rulings_2026_07_31`, "CUSTOMER QUEUE, FULL SCOPE… the public wall board all build NOW").

So: a screen on the wall, showing the day's waiting queue, that she can read from her seat without asking anyone anything.

**The dangerous thing about it is stated up front, because it governs every other decision in this document.** This is the one place in the entire product where a customer's name is shown to a room full of strangers — and, because the ruling fixes the transport as a public unauthenticated GET on a public subdomain, to anyone on the internet who knows the boutique's slug. Everything in D3, D5, D6 and D7 is minimisation against that fact, and Risk 1 states the residual without arguing it away.

## Goal

`https://{slug}.modryn.co.il/queue`, opened once on a browser in kiosk mode on a TV bolted to the salon wall. It shows, in type legible from three to five metres:

- a heading that is true whatever the queue is doing,
- up to **five** rows, each a **position number** and a **first name**, in exactly the order F33's `/q/{ticket_id}` computes positions,
- an overflow line when there are more than five, **in room-legible type** (D8),
- an **empty state** designed on purpose, because that is the state the screen is in for most of the day,
- a freshness line that says, in past tense, when the board was last true,
- and a pause control, because SC 2.2.2 is Level A and Level A is legally required here.

It updates itself every five seconds and it never moves, fades, scrolls or pulses while doing it.

The same URL opened on a phone is the same page. That is not an accident — it is what makes the pause control mean something (D11).

## Design

### D1 — `POST /storefront/queue`, the **third** route on F33's shipped queue router. This overrules the brief's "public GET", and the reason is mechanical

```python
# app/queue/router.py — third route, same router, same _no_store, same tenant-from-Host
@router.post("/queue")
async def queue_board(request: Request, service: Queue) -> QueueBoardView:
    tenant = get_current_tenant(request)
    return await service.board(tenant.id)
```

The LOOP-STATE note says "Public GET on the queue router" (`LOOP-STATE.md:520`). **A GET is not available, and the reason is a shipped test rather than a preference.**

`ROUTES` in `backend/tests/test_storefront_api.py:186-194` is **derived**: `{path for method, path in _registered_routes(create_app(…)) if method == "GET" and path.startswith("/storefront")}`. It is router-agnostic — it walks the whole app. It has **six members** and **five** guards parametrize over it (`:519`, `:544`, `:554`, `:689`, `:1222` — verified by grep, not assumed; the six is the route count, which is a different number), and one of the five is fatal:

> `test_the_read_throttle_is_not_inert` (`:1222-1234`) installs `FixedWindowRateLimiter(max_attempts=0)` on `app.state.storefront_rate_limiter` and asserts **429** on every path in `ROUTES`.

Every GET under `/storefront` today lives on `app/storefront/router.py`, which carries that limiter as a **router-level** dependency (`:103-134`). A GET registered on the queue sibling router does not depend on it, answers **200**, and reddens that test. There are exactly two fixes and both are worse than a POST:

1. **Make the board GET depend on `storefront_rate_limiter` too.** That is a second key on the catalog's 6000/60s budget, which is precisely the failure `main.py:720-722` names in writing: "reusing the storefront read brake would let one leaked position-poll loop 429 the catalog for every shopper on the site." A wall screen polling forever is a *permanent* leaked loop by design.
2. **Weaken the guard** so it skips the new route. That test protects six shipped public reads against a class of defect (`record_failure` deleted → the limiter silently counts nothing) that its own docstring says would otherwise make every one of them answer 200 against a limiter configured to block everything.

Two further dividends, the same four F33's D1 enumerates:

- `_client()` (`:494-513`) stubs exactly **one** service (`storefront_service`). A new GET backed by `QueueService` would reach the real one built in `create_app()` — a live session factory inside the fast, no-Docker suite.
- `FORBIDDEN_KEYS` (`:203-232`) is walked recursively over every **GET** response and contains `status`, `created_at`, `tenant_id`. F59's payload names none of those (D7), so this one is a near miss rather than a hit — recorded because a later reader who adds `status` to a board entry would need to argue with that frozenset.

**So: POST, with no request body at all.** `GET /storefront/queue` stays a 405, which is the same posture F33's two routes assert and which the new test file asserts again.

**This is not the same argument F33 made and it must not be mis-cited as such.** F33's two routes are POSTs because the ticket id is a **capability** and a path segment leaks into access logs, proxy traces and `Referer` (`app/queue/router.py:10-16`). F59's request carries **nothing** — no id, no body, no secret — so the capability argument does not transfer at all. The reason here is the derived-`ROUTES` collision above, and it is recorded as its own conflict (conflict 1) rather than folded into F33's.

**Registration:** none. The route is a decorator on the router `main.py:1212` already includes. `main.py`'s comment at `:1206-1211` ("F33's walk-in check-in and its position read, both POSTs" — `:1203-1205` is the `register_fake_pay` block and is not part of it) becomes false and **this PR rewrites it** — see the shipped-comment table in «Frontend and backend changes».

**No router-level throttle, no new dependency, no CSRF work.** `CsrfOriginMiddleware` inspects only `/manage`; the route reads no cookie and carries no ambient credential. Both claims are already asserted for this router and F59's test file asserts them for the third route.

The access path is `idx_queue_tickets_tenant_day_active (tenant_id, queue_day) WHERE deleted_at IS NULL` (`0018_queue_tickets.py:93-96`) — the exact prefix the board filters on, chosen for that reason by F33's own migration comment (`:88-92`).

**If a builder concludes a migration IS needed, that is a significant finding and it must stop and be recorded, not built.** The only shapes that could justify one are (a) an index on `called_at` — declined: a four-value status filter over one tenant-day is already a range scan over a handful of rows, and F20's sweep would then have two indexes to keep true; (b) a `first_name` column — declined by D5, since storing a derived value means storing it wrong for every row inserted before the rule changed, and the derivation is three lines; (c) anything F58 needs — not F59's to add, and F58 needs no migration either (`qr-walkin-queue.md:20`, citing `LOOP-STATE.md:241-243`; `LOOP-STATE.md:481-482` says the narrower "No new table", which is not the same promise).

**What already guards this.** `backend/tests/test_migrations.py` pins the two CHECK definitions and `idx_queue_tickets_tenant_day_active`'s `pg_indexes.indexdef` **byte-identically, captured from a live server**, and asserts `queue_tickets` carries **no unique index but the primary key**. A column added to this table, or an index, reddens a shipped test. That is a dividend of F33's pinning work and it is the reason F59 needs no new migration test of its own.

### D3 — The read model: which rows, which order, and why the order must equal F33's `position()` exactly

```python
# app/db/repositories/queue_tickets.py — one new method, read-only
async def board(
    self, session: AsyncSession, tenant_id: UUID, queue_day: datetime.date, *, limit: int
) -> tuple[Sequence[Row[tuple[str, datetime.datetime | None]]], int]:
    """`select(QueueTicket.name, QueueTicket.called_at)` — a COLUMN PROJECTION,
    never `select(QueueTicket)`. …"""
```

**It selects two columns, not the entity, and that is the D4 minimisation argument applied one rung further down.** `select(QueueTicket)` would pull six normalised Israeli mobile numbers and six `marketing_opt_in_at` consent timestamps into the process on **every** poll, twelve times a minute, forever, for a view that renders `name` and `called_at is not None`. Nothing would leak — the schema narrows — but the wire is not the only place minimisation belongs, and D4's own argument ("a client-side cap would ship forty names to a browser and render five") is exactly this argument one layer up. Projecting also makes `QueueTicketsRepository`'s class-docstring promise (conflict 7) true in the stronger sense: **the phone never enters the process on this path at all**, rather than merely never being read from the object. One line, and A7's "no field beyond three" becomes true at the repository boundary as well as at the schema.

**Which rows.** Exactly the set `position()` counts over, with the day bound differently and for a stated reason:

| Predicate | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `tenant_id == :tenant` | from the Host header | RLS is the control; the explicit predicate is the house defence-in-depth (`queue_tickets.py:12-14`, citing `CustomersRepository`) |
| `queue_day == :day` | **`today_jerusalem(clock)`** | The wall shows **today's** queue. This is the one place F59 reads a clock. |
| `status == 'waiting'` | `QueueTicketStatus.WAITING.value` | Byte-identical to `position()` (`queue_tickets.py:85`). `in_service` is not waiting; `done`/`removed` are terminal. |
| `deleted_at IS NULL` | — | Matches `position()` (`:86`) and the index predicate. |

**`:day` is TODAY here and the TICKET'S OWN DAY there, and that difference is deliberate.** `position()` binds to `ticket.queue_day` because it has a ticket in hand and because binding to today would tell a woman who walked out yesterday that she is next (`queue_tickets.py:66-70`). The board has no ticket, and "today" is the only meaningful scope for a screen on a wall: a ghost ticket nobody closed overnight — which is the **normal** state of things until F58 ships — must not sit at position 1 on the wall forever. **The consequence is stated rather than hidden: a stale ticket from an earlier day is invisible on the board while its own `/q/{id}` page still reports its own day's position.** That is honest in both places, and it is the only case where the two screens legitimately disagree.

**The day is computed on the SERVER, per request, and that is worth naming because it deletes a whole class of client bug.** F34's board had to recompute the Jerusalem date on every tick and bump the generation when it rolled (`shift-board-checkin.md` D12: "`todayJerusalem()` at mount is wrong for a device left on the counter past midnight"). F59's request carries no date at all, so **the board empties at midnight Jerusalem with zero client-side date logic**, on a screen that is specifically expected to be mounted for months. No `todayJerusalem` import, no date-roll generation bump, no test for it.

**Which order.**

```
ORDER BY COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at) ASC, id ASC
LIMIT :limit
```

plus a second statement, the untruncated count:

```
SELECT count(*) FROM queue_tickets
 WHERE <the same four predicates>
```

**The ordering justification against `COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at)`.** F33 defines the queue's order once, in `position()`, as `count(sort_key < mine) + 1` with `sort_key = COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at)` (`queue_tickets.py:80-89`), and F33's spec states the reason the `COALESCE` ships before anything writes `requeued_at`: "the ordering is the contract, not the column" — F58's skip must be a one-column write rather than a renumbering pass. F59 renders the **same** order as a list. Index `i` (0-based) therefore satisfies `position == i + 1` **iff** it counts strictly-earlier rows over the identical predicate set, which is why the four predicates above are byte-identical to `position()`'s rather than merely equivalent-looking.

**The residual, decided rather than papered over: an exact tie on the sort key.** `created_at` defaults to `now()`, which in Postgres is transaction-start time, and each check-in is its own transaction — so a tie needs two `INSERT`s whose transactions began in the same microsecond. If it happens, `position()` gives **both** tickets the same number (neither is `< mine`) while the board's index gives them 3 and 4. The wall and one phone disagree by one.

- **`, id ASC` is added to the board's ORDER BY** — not to fix that disagreement (it cannot; the count has no second key), but because **without it a tie makes the board's own row order non-deterministic across polls**, which on a wall screen is a visible flicker between two names every five seconds. A stable order is worth one clause.
- **`position()` is NOT edited.** Changing a shipped read's semantics to buy a one-in-a-microsecond cosmetic agreement is not a trade worth making, and the edit would have to be justified against F33's pinned tests. Recorded as an accepted residual with F58 as the eventual owner — its panel wants the whole day ranked and will add `row_number()`, at which point one expression can serve all three readers.

**Two statements, not one.** `count(*)` over the same predicates, issued in the same `tenant_session`, gives `waiting_total`. Declined: a window function returning the count on every row (five copies of one integer, and the count is needed even when `entries` is empty); declined: `len(entries)` as the total (it is capped at five, so the overflow line would always say zero — the failure is silent and the number is on a wall).

**⚠ `count(*)` counts TICKETS, not women, and that is not a rounding error — it is Ruling 3.** F33 deleted server-side dedup by ruling, and `app/models/queue_ticket.py:23-26` states the consequence on the shipped table: *"There is no uniqueness of any kind beyond the primary key: a second ticket for the same phone on the same day is a real, expected outcome, and F58 merges or removes it."* F33's D8 adds that the `sessionStorage` pointer "does not survive a QR re-scan, which opens a fresh browsing context with an empty store and is this feature's ordinary re-entry path" — so a duplicate is the **ordinary outcome of the ordinary re-entry path**, and D10 establishes that nothing removes it before midnight. On the wall, one woman who re-scanned renders at **two positions with the same first name**, and `waiting_total` counts her twice. Three things follow and all three are recorded rather than fixed:

- **The board cannot deduplicate**, and must not try. The only key that would identify her is `phone`, and `QueueTicketsRepository`'s class docstring (`queue_tickets.py:15-18`) promises **no read is keyed on `phone`** and calls that absence "the security property, not an omission". Deduplicating on the public board would be the one read that breaks it, on the one endpoint where breaking it is worst. **F58 owns the merge**, exactly as the model comment says.
- **`waiting_total` is documented as a count of waiting TICKETS**, which under Ruling 3 is `>=` the number of women (D7's field table says so).
- The row and the arithmetic are both correct; the *interpretation* is bounded. The ugly-edges table carries the case and a frontend test asserts two entries with an identical `first_name` both render (the React key is `position`, so they must).

**The ceiling, stated.** Two index range scans over one tenant-day's waiting rows, once per screen per five seconds. At a busy boutique that is tens of rows, twelve times a minute, per screen. Fine at boutique scale; D6 bounds the aggregate.

### D4 — `BOARD_ROW_LIMIT = 5`: a minimisation control first, a layout constant second

The cap lives in `app/queue/validation.py` beside the feature's other bounds and is applied **on the server**, in the `LIMIT` clause. Two independent reasons, in order of weight:

1. **At most five names leave the database, whatever the queue length.** The wire is the disclosure surface (Risk 1). A client-side cap would ship forty names to a browser and render five, which is forty names in a network trace, a devtools panel and any intermediary that ignores `no-store`.
2. **Five is what fits at the legibility floor.** The arithmetic is in D8 and it is derived off the **position number**, which is the taller of the two glyphs in a row — an earlier draft derived it off the name (59px) while the row also renders the number (76px), which over-counted the fit by a full row on the exact hardware this feature targets.

**It does not adapt to the viewport, and that is the point.** A 4K screen gets more whitespace, not more rows. A cap that grew with the screen would make the amount of personal data on the wire a function of the customer's display settings, which is not a property anybody wants to have to reason about.

**The client renders whatever it receives and asserts nothing about the count**, so raising the cap in the pilot is one server constant and no frontend change. A frontend test pins that (rendering 1, 5 and 8 entries all work).

### D5 — The first-name derivation, with every edge case decided

```python
# app/queue/validation.py
BOARD_NAME_MAX = 12

def board_display_name(name: str) -> str:
    """The ONLY personal datum F59 puts on the wire. Exactly ONE
    whitespace-delimited token leaves the database — WHICHEVER token she typed
    first. That is usually the given name and it is not a guarantee: «כהן נועה»
    is ordinary Israeli form-filling and this returns «כהן». No heuristic fixes
    it; D13's collection notice is the remedy."""
    first = name.split()[0]
    return first if len(first) <= BOARD_NAME_MAX else first[: BOARD_NAME_MAX - 1] + "…"
```

**What "first name" means when the stored value is one free-text field.** `queue_tickets.name` is whatever she typed into a single «שם מלא» input, bounded to 80 characters, C0 controls barred (`app/booking/validation.py:73-87`, reached from `app/queue/validation.py:53`). There is no structured given/family split anywhere in the product and F59 does not invent one — see D2's declined `first_name` column. So the rule is **the first whitespace-delimited token**, and `str.split()` with no argument is exactly right: it splits on any run of whitespace and discards empties, so leading spaces, double spaces and a trailing newline all fall out. `name.split()[0]` **cannot raise `IndexError`** on a stored row, because `validate_customer_name` rejects `not name.strip()` before the insert (`booking/validation.py:82-83`) — verified, and asserted anyway in the fast suite so a future loosening of that validator does not blow up a wall screen.

The edge cases, decided:

| Case | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **«נועה כהן»** — the ordinary case | «נועה» | Only the first token leaves the database, so her surname stays in it. |
| **«כהן נועה»** — surname first | «כהן» — **an accepted, unfixable ceiling** | ⚠ **This is why "the surname never leaves the database" is NOT stated anywhere in this spec as an absolute.** `name` is one free-text «שם מלא» field with no structure; writing the family name first is ordinary Israeli form-filling and is how a name appears on an identity document. The derivation is `name.split()[0]` and it has no way to know. **Declined: a name-order heuristic; declined: a surname stop-list** — for the reason this table already gives twice below (no heuristic can classify a token in free text, and every one that tries is wrong on real names). What the code guarantees is exactly this and nothing more: *one whitespace-delimited token leaves the database, whichever token that is.* The remedy is D13's notice, at the moment of collection. |
| **«נועה»** — a one-word name | «נועה», shown in full | There is no surname to drop, so the board discloses **exactly what she typed and not one character more**. Nothing is gained by refusing to show a name a woman chose to give as her whole name, and showing a placeholder instead would make the board useless for her. |
| **A single token longer than 12 characters** | truncated to 11 + «…» | The ellipsis is visible, so the board never silently misrepresents a name. Truncation is **server-side** so the tail never reaches the wire — a client-side `text-ellipsis` would ship the whole token and hide it with CSS. |
| **«גב' כהן»** — an honorific first | «גב'» | No heuristic can tell an honorific from a given name in free text, and every heuristic that tries gets it wrong on real names. It renders a harmless word. Declined: a stop-list. |
| **«נועה-מרים כהן»** — a hyphenated given name | «נועה-מרים» (one token) | Correct by construction; hyphens are not whitespace. |
| **A rare given name that identifies her on its own** | shown | **The derivation cannot fix this and must not pretend to.** The remedy is not technical: she has to be told, at the moment of collection, that her first name will appear on a screen in the salon. That is D13, and it is a build task in this PR. |
| **Two women with the same first name** | both shown, unchanged | The position number disambiguates. Declined: appending a surname initial — it discloses strictly more, on the one surface where less is the whole design. |
| **A token whose 11th character is a combining mark** | accepted ceiling | Python slices code points, so a surrogate pair cannot split, but a combining mark can detach from its base and render as a stray diacritic — on a name already too long to fit. Grapheme-aware truncation needs a dependency (`regex`/`grapheme`) for a cosmetic edge on a truncated name. Recorded as a `ponytail:`-style ceiling in the function's comment, not built. |
| **Bidi-mixed name («Noa כהן»)** | rendered inside a **bare `<bdi>`** | The shipped rule: numerals get `<bdi dir="ltr">`, customer-authored text gets a bare `<bdi>`, because `dir="ltr"` on Hebrew is itself a bidi defect (`QueuePositionPage.tsx:332` for the numeral half). |

**Where it lives, and the shipped comment it makes false.** `app/queue/validation.py`'s module docstring currently reads "The two error classes and the **one** bound F33 owns." F59 adds two constants and one derivation, so **this PR rewrites that sentence**. Leaving it would leave a false count standing on the file that defines the feature's bounds — the same class of defect F36's spec review caught on three floor comments.

### D6 — A **fourth** limiter instance, `board_limiter`, and the arithmetic that says it cannot be any of the other three

| Instance | Key | Default | Meters |
|---|---|---|---|
| `board_limiter` **(new)** | `queue:board:{tenant_id}` | **600 / 60s** | every board read, always |

Two new `Settings` fields, `queue_board_max_per_window` and `queue_board_window_seconds`, following the `checkin_*` precedent at `backend/app/core/config.py:182-196` so both can be tightened during an incident without a deploy. One new `QueueService.__init__` kwarg. One new `FixedWindowRateLimiter(...)` in `main.py`'s existing `QueueService(...)` construction.

**The traffic shape, which is genuinely different from every other public endpoint.** One wall screen at a 5-second beat is a **single device polling forever**: 12 requests/minute, 720/hour, ~8,600 over a twelve-hour shop day, from one browser, at a perfectly even cadence, whether or not anybody is in the shop. Every other anonymous surface in this product is bursty and human-paced.

**Why each existing budget fails, with the numbers:**

- **`create_limiter` — 200 / 3600s, per tenant** (`config.py:182-183`). One wall screen alone is **720/hour, 3.6× the entire check-in create ceiling**. The budget would be spent about **seventeen minutes** into the shop day, and the endpoint it protects is the front door: `POST /storefront/checkin` would start answering 429 to every woman scanning the QR. The wall screen would close the door queue. This is the most vivid of the four and it is why "reuse an existing budget" is not a live option.
- **`position_ticket_limiter` — 30 / 60s** (`config.py:188-189`). Its ceiling is sized for **one client holding one ticket** (the arithmetic is a comment on the **Settings field**, `config.py:185-187`: "A 5-second poll is 12/60s, so 30 is 2.5x headroom" — **not** on `service.py:121-134`, which is the hit/miss asymmetry docstring and says nothing about headroom). The board key is per-**tenant** and aggregates every screen in the shop plus every phone that opens the URL. Two screens = 24/min (fits); **three screens = 36/min and the wall trips itself**; add three curious phones and one screen trips. A ceiling that a boutique can exceed by buying a second TV is not a ceiling, it is a bug with a delay on it.
- **`position_miss_limiter` — 120 / 60s, per tenant** (`config.py:195-196`). **The trap `main.py:743-746` warns about** — "sharing this one with the ticket budget above would give the ticket key the miss ceiling and the two would trip each other… stated for the case where it is least obvious because both keys belong to the same feature." Two keys on one instance share one ceiling: a board flood would spend the miss brake and start converting genuine 404s on `/storefront/checkin/position` into 429s (deferring a legitimate client's terminal by up to a window), and symmetrically a ticket-id walk would blank the wall screen. Two budgets, two operational meanings, one instance each.
- **`storefront_rate_limiter` — 6000 / 60s, catalog** (`config.py:159-171`). `main.py:720-722` states the failure verbatim: a leaked poll loop 429s the catalog for every shopper on the site. A wall screen is a *permanent* poll loop by design.

**Why 600 / 60s, and the arithmetic must count PHONES, not screens.** An earlier draft sized this at 120/60s against screens alone — "5× a two-screen shop, ten screens' worth" — and that number is wrong, because **the key is per tenant and D11(a) deliberately puts every phone in the salon on it.** D11(a) is not a rhetorical flourish: it is the load-bearing half of this feature's SC 2.2.2 defence, which holds *because* «every woman in the salon who opens the same public URL on her own phone» is an ordinary thing to do with a URL that is public by ruling. A budget that assumes those phones do not exist contradicts the argument that makes the pause control genuine.

The concurrent-viewer arithmetic, written out:

| Population | Pollers | req/min at a 5s beat |
|---|---|---|
| Wall screens (salon + fitting area) | 2 | 24 |
| Phones in the room on a busy Sunday | up to 18 | 216 |
| **Design target** | **20 concurrent viewers** | **240** |
| **Ceiling, with 2.5× headroom over the target** | — | **600** |

At 120 the ceiling was **ten** concurrent viewers total. Two screens and eight phones is 120/min **exactly**, and D10 establishes the board only grows all day, so eight-plus women in the room is the ordinary afternoon — the wall would start taking 429s and backing off toward 60s **at the one moment the board matters**. 600/60s is 50 concurrent pollers, comfortably above any bridal salon, and still kills a scripted reader: at 50 rps the budget is gone in **12 seconds** and stays gone.

**It is deliberately NOT the same number as `position_miss_limiter`'s.** The earlier draft's "same shape, different instance, which is the whole lesson" was a didactic argument, not a traffic argument, and a limiter ceiling has to be sized by traffic. The lesson is carried by the `main.py` comment instead, where it belongs.

**What happens when it trips, and this is the part that matters on a wall.** The screen gets a 429, the client backs off (5s → 60s cap), and **the last good board stays on screen with the freshness line switched to the stale sentence** (D12, and it is the shipped `loadedRef` discipline at `QueuePositionPage.tsx:166-170`). The board **never blanks and never lies**: it says «העדכון האחרון היה 14:07» — in **room-legible type**, which is why D8 promotes the freshness line out of the small-text bucket. That promotion is what makes this paragraph true; at 24px it was a claim the room could not read.

**⚠ And the flooder is not hypothetical, because the key is per tenant on an anonymous endpoint.** Anyone on the internet who knows a boutique's slug can spend the whole 600 in 12 seconds at 50 rps and **hold it spent indefinitely**, at which point the wall shows a correct-but-frozen board labelled stale until he stops. The in-process limiter cannot distinguish the wall screen from the flooder — they key identically — and a per-IP component is declined below for reasons that do not change. **This is recorded as Risk 13, with the same upgrade path Risk 1 already carries** (a signed board URL or a per-tenant setting), rather than fixed here. An earlier draft's Risk 9 read the exposure backwards, saying the limiter mattered "for load and, uniquely on this endpoint, for nothing else" — that is right about confidentiality and wrong about availability: this is the one endpoint in the product where a spent budget degrades the feature's **only** surface.

**Metering order:** resolve the tenant (the middleware already 404s an unknown Host before the handler runs) → consult and record `board_limiter` → open the `tenant_session` → two `SELECT`s. There is no miss case — every resolvable tenant has a board, empty or not — so unlike the position read there is no hit/miss asymmetry to reason about. Charged on an empty board too: the cost is the query, not the rows.

**The key is about a boutique, never about a person**, which is what keeps the shared `TOO_MANY_ATTEMPTS` body safe here — the rule `app/queue/validation.py:29-31` already states for the other three.

**Declined:** a per-IP component — `storefront/router.py:103-134` argues it in full (`trust_forwarded_for`'s two-hop Cloudflare caveat is unresolved until F21, and behind an untrusted proxy the key collapses to one bucket), and here it is worse than anywhere else: the wall screen and every phone in the shop are on the same guest wifi, so a per-IP key would put the TV and the customers in one bucket. **Declined:** `Retry-After` — F33's D6 declined it for this same 429 body and the reason is unchanged. **Declined:** exempting the board from limiting because "the data is public anyway" — the budget protects the **server**, not the data (the data is disclosed by design), and an unmetered endpoint that runs two queries per request is a free amplifier.

**Stated honestly:** `FixedWindowRateLimiter` is in-process, resets on deploy and does not aggregate across replicas (`app/auth/rate_limit.py:4-6`). The ceiling is a speed bump. That matters for load and, here, for nothing else — the payload is public by ruling, so there is no confidentiality the limiter was ever going to buy.

### D7 — The wire: three fields per entry, one integer beside them, and **no ticket id**

```jsonc
// QueueBoardView — the WHOLE body, and there is no other route.
{
  "entries": [
    { "position": 1, "first_name": "נועה",  "called": true  },
    { "position": 2, "first_name": "מיכל",  "called": false },
    // 11 characters then the ellipsis — D5's rule is `first[:BOARD_NAME_MAX - 1] + "…"`
    // with BOARD_NAME_MAX = 12. An earlier draft's example truncated at six and
    // would have taught the wrong boundary to whoever writes test_the_first_name_rule.
    { "position": 3, "first_name": "אלכסנדרינה…", "called": false }

```

Field by field, with the privacy argument for each thing that reaches the wire:

| Field | Why it is here | What it discloses |
|---|---|---|
| `position` | The product. It is the number she is looking up to read, and it equals what her own `/q/{id}` page says at the same instant (D3). | Her place in a queue — which is what the screen exists to publish, and which the room can also count. |
| `first_name` | The brief's ruling, and it is what makes the board readable rather than a column of numbers. Derived and truncated **server-side** (D5). | **The one genuinely personal datum on this endpoint.** A given name, capped at 12 characters, of a woman currently in a named bridal boutique. Risk 1 owns the residual. |
| `called` | **A boolean, not `called_at`.** It drives the highlight and nothing else. | That a manager has called her forward. |
| `waiting_total` | The overflow line's arithmetic. Without it, «ועוד N» is either absent or invented. | The live count of waiting **TICKETS**, which under Ruling 3 is `>=` the number of women (D3). **Strictly more than F33 discloses, deliberately** — F33's own spec says so at **`qr-walkin-queue.md:348`** ("F59's public wall board discloses strictly more, deliberately"); `:820` is the out-of-scope bullet and says something else entirely. **And until F58 ships it is the day's ARRIVAL count, not a waiting count** — see below. |

**Why `called` is a boolean and not the timestamp.** `TicketView` carries `called_at` because the *ticket holder* needs it and because her page reads it to say "go to the counter" (`app/queue/schemas.py:68-70`). The wall needs to know **whether**, not **when**. Shipping the instant would let anyone watching time how long a named woman has been standing at the counter, and nothing on the board renders it — an unused timestamp on a public payload is disclosure for zero product value. One boolean.

**No ticket id, and this is the most important line in the spec.**

`app/queue/router.py:18-22` states F33's whole security model on the shipped file: *"The ticket id IS the capability, issued exactly once, at creation, in the response to the request that created it, and by no other server path ever."* `app/queue/schemas.py:61-63` says it again on the field. A board that returned ids would make both of those sentences **false**, and would hand every passer-by — and every crawler, and anyone on the internet with the slug — a live, pollable capability over every woman's visit, retrievable six at a time, refreshed every five seconds, forever. **The board returns no id. There is no `id` field on `QueueBoardEntry` and there must never be one.**

**⚠ But the correlation runs the OTHER way too, and F59 opens it.** The paragraph above analyses board → id and gets the right answer: no id is on the wire. It does not analyse **id → name**, and that channel is new:

1. `POST /storefront/checkin/position` answers `position` for **any** ticket id, anonymously.
2. `POST /storefront/queue` answers the **first name** at positions 1…`BOARD_ROW_LIMIT`, anonymously.

So anyone holding a ticket id that is not theirs reads the holder's first name in **two anonymous requests**, whenever she is in the top five. And the product already names that caller in a shipped comment: `he.ts`'s `checkin.lastFromDevice` says that on a shared phone or a door tablet the courtesy pointer "holds SOMEONE ELSE'S ticket". This is not hypothetical and it must not be discovered later.

**What it falsifies, and what this PR does about it.** `app/queue/schemas.py:52-55` states on the shipped file that `TicketView` omits her NAME because "echoing it back over an anonymous endpoint keyed by a guessable-in-principle id is a PII disclosure for zero product value". F59 restores exactly that disclosure through a second endpoint. **This PR rewrites that docstring paragraph** to say that from F59 onward the first name of a top-`BOARD_ROW_LIMIT` ticket is derivable by combining the position read with the public board, so the omission is now **defence-in-depth rather than a closed channel** — leaving the sentence standing would be a security rationale the product no longer keeps. Recorded as **conflict 8**, alongside the four count-comments, because it is a *rationale* rather than a count.

**The residual, decided.** The correlation is not fixable inside F59 without either withdrawing the ruled feature or adding an id to the board (which D7 forbids absolutely). The cheapest real bound is already applied: **`board_display_name` has run, so what the correlation yields is one token, capped at 12 characters — never a surname, never a phone, never a `visit_type`.** That is the accepted bound. It is the same residual class as Risk 1 and shares its upgrade path.

Two mechanical consequences, both stated so a builder does not "fix" them:

- **React needs a key and there is no id to use.** The key is the entry's `position`. That is correct here rather than merely tolerable: rows are positional by nature, they contain no input state and no focus, so a re-render swaps text inside a stable row instead of moving DOM nodes. Using an index would be identical; **using a name would collide** — on two different women with the same first name **and** on one woman holding two tickets (D3, Ruling 3), which is the ordinary re-entry path rather than an edge case.
- **A test pins the absence.** `QueueBoardEntry.model_fields.keys() == {"position", "first_name", "called"}` in the fast suite — an assertion that fails the moment anyone adds a field, rather than a source grep that passes vacuously.

**What is deliberately absent, each with its reason:** her **surname** (only the first token is derived — D5, and read D5's «כהן נועה» row for what that does and does not guarantee); her **phone**; her **`visit_type`** (bride vs evening is a fact about her life and nothing on the wall renders it); her **`status`** (the four values are an operator vocabulary, and `status` is in `FORBIDDEN_KEYS` for GET responses anyway); **`created_at`** (**nothing renders it, so it is disclosure for zero product value** — the same sentence this list uses for `visit_type`, and the freshness signal is the client's own last-fetch time per D12); **`queue_day`, `requeued_at`, `skip_count`, `tenant_id`, `marketing_opt_in_at`, `deleted_at`** (operator provenance, the rule `app/storefront/router.py`'s `public_terms` projection states); **any count of `done`, `removed` or `in_service`** (how many women a boutique served today is commercial information — see the caveat immediately below, which is load-bearing); **anything about a ticket from another day or another tenant** (D3's predicates and RLS, asserted in the isolation suite).

**⚠ Two of those reasons are one-shot arguments on an endpoint designed to be polled forever, and both are corrected here rather than left overclaiming.**

- **`created_at`'s omission does not stop anyone timing a named woman's wait.** An earlier draft gave that as the reason. It holds against a single reader and fails against the caller this endpoint is *built* for: a poller sampling every 5 seconds records the tick at which «נועה» first appears and reconstructs her arrival to five-second resolution, plus — until F58 — the fact that she never leaves. **The omission is still right**; only the reason changed, to the one it actually delivers.
- **`waiting_total` discloses the served count this list declines to disclose, for the whole pre-F58 period.** D10 establishes that nothing leaves `waiting` until F58 ships, so `waiting_total` **is** the number of women who checked in today, rising monotonically until midnight Jerusalem. The commercial property the bullet above claims to protect is therefore disclosed exactly, by a field on the same payload, to anyone with the slug. After F58 the field means what its name says. **Decided: the field stays** — the overflow line has no other source, and bucketing it (send `overflow = waiting_total - len(entries)` and round above some N) trades a real number on a wall for a hedge, on a screen whose entire value is being literally true. **The disclosure is recorded in Risk 1 rather than argued away**, together with the polling observation above: a 5s poller reconstructs a boutique's arrival sequence and per-minute footfall from the row sequence alone, and **no payload decision available to F59 changes that.**

**Response, always 200, one shape, no branch.** An empty queue is `{"entries": [], "waiting_total": 0}` — not a 404, not a 204. A screen that answers 404 when nobody is waiting would render an error arm for most of the shop day, which is exactly the failure the empty state exists to prevent.

### D8 — Big-type layout for a screen seen from three to five metres. **This is not the responsive sweep**

The viewport is a 1080p or 4K panel across a salon. There is no thumb, no hover, no scroll gesture and nobody standing at it. Two constraints replace the usual breakpoint sweep: **legible at distance**, and **everything on one screenful, because nobody will scroll it.**

**The type scale. The preferred term is `rem + vh`, NOT `vh` alone, and that is a WCAG 1.4.4 requirement rather than a style preference.**

An earlier draft used `clamp(<rem floor>, <vh>, <rem cap>)` with the `vh` term dominant at every realistic viewport. Run it against the product's own shipped accessibility control: `frontend/packages/ui/src/theme.css:170-172` implements the A11yMenu's text-size boost as `:root[data-a11y-text-size] { font-size: 1.2rem; }`. With a `vh`-only preferred term, `clamp(2rem, 5.5vh, 7rem)` at 1080p is `clamp(38.4, 59.4, 134.4)` boosted and `clamp(32, 59.4, 112)` unboosted — **59.4px either way. The control is a complete no-op on every text element of this page**, at 1080p, at 4K, and on a 375×812 phone (5.5vh = 44.7px, boosted floor 38.4px — the floor still loses). Browser zoom does not rescue it either: page zoom shrinks the CSS-pixel viewport and enlarges the pixel, so `vh` text is net-constant under zoom by construction.

That is **SC 1.4.4 (AA, legally required here)** failing on a page whose accessibility argument is otherwise the most carefully made in the feature, and — exactly like SC 2.2.2 — **axe has no rule for it**, so it would ship green. The repo treats the boost as a real contract in three separate test files (`catalog-composites.test.tsx`, `chrome-composites.test.tsx`, `DescriptionClamp.test.tsx:44-49`), and D11(a)'s phone user, whose existence is what makes this page's 2.2.2 conformance genuine, is the exact person the boost exists for.

**So every size on this page puts a `rem` term inside the preferred value.** Fixed pixels are still wrong for the other reason — a 4K panel reporting 3840×2160 CSS pixels at 1× DPR would render a `4rem` cap *physically smaller* than at 1080p — so the shape is `clamp(<rem floor>, <rem> + <vh>, <rem cap>)`: the `vh` half tracks the panel, the `rem` half tracks the user.

- **D11(c) resolves the whole 2.2.2 wall-screen tension on the freshness line.** Its words are that a room-reachable pause would be actively harmful and *"the honest protection against a frozen wall is not a lock — it is the freshness line, which is on the screen, in the room's reading order… Even the frozen state is labelled, on the wall, in Hebrew."* At 24px it is not — a paused, stale or 429-degraded board would read to the room as live, which is precisely the failure D11(c) invokes the line to prevent, and it is what D6's "a flooder can make the wall stale; he cannot make it wrong" depends on. ### D9 — The poll: copied from the **shipped** `QueuePositionPage.tsx`, including both lines that leaked once

`frontend/apps/storefront/src/routes/QueueBoardPage.tsx` copies the loop from `QueuePositionPage.tsx`, which is the shipped, reviewed source in the same app, behind the same `ApiError`, with the same 5s beat. This is the **third** copy of these mechanisms in the repo (`BoardSection.tsx` → `apps/manage/src/lib/usePoll.ts` → `QueuePositionPage.tsx` → here) and D9-of-F33's argument against extraction still holds: `usePoll.ts` lives in `apps/manage` and is unreachable from `apps/storefront`; the only shared home is `packages/ui`, whose review surface is the design system. Risk 5 carries the cost.

**Copy verbatim, all of it:**

1. **Schedule-after-settle, one arming site.** `schedule(ms)` clears any pending timer and refuses to arm when stopped or `document.hidden` (`QueuePositionPage.tsx:105-118`). At most one request in flight per tab **by construction**. Not `setInterval` + `AbortController`.
2. **One monotonic `generationRef`, compared at three points** — success (`:129`), catch (`:151`) **and the `.finally()`** (`:177`). The third is the one that gets dropped; without it a superseded load arms a second timer and the at-most-one property is gone.
3. **`tickRef` updated on every render with NO dependency array** (`:191-193`).
4. **`document.hidden` guarded twice** — in `schedule()` (`:111`) and in `tick()` (`:184`) — and `visibilitychange` back to visible bumps the generation and fetches **immediately** (`:217-237`). On a wall screen this matters in a way it does not on a phone: a kiosk browser that backgrounds the tab (a screensaver, a second tab opened by a staffer) would otherwise be throttled to ≥1/minute by the browser and the board would look live while being a minute stale.
5. **Failure backoff, 5s doubling to a 60s cap, reset on the first success** (`:138`, `:171`, `MAX_BACKOFF_MS` at `:29`).

**⚠ The two lines that leaked once, and neither is optional:**

- **`runningRef.current = true` as the FIRST line of the mount effect** (`:196-201`). The cleanup sets it false and nothing else sets it true except `resume()`, so without this line a setup → cleanup → setup cycle leaves the loop **permanently dead while the UI still shows a pause control**. React requires effects to survive that cycle and StrictMode performs exactly it. This is the bug F57's review found inherited from `BoardSection` on `main` (`LOOP-STATE.md:265-267`). **On a wall screen the symptom is a TV that shows a correct board frozen at the moment of mount, with a working-looking pause button, and nobody in the room to notice** — the worst instance of this bug in the product. It gets its own **named test** (Testing).
- **`runningRef.current = false` BEFORE `clearTick()` in the cleanup** (`:203-213`). `clearTick()` alone cancels only the timer armed right now; the arming site is a `.finally()` that runs *after* cleanup when a request is in flight, and nothing in `tick → load → finally → schedule` touches React state, so the orphan cannot be broken by unmounting. This shipped as a defect twice in this repo (`LOOP-STATE.md:193-202`, `:265-267`).

**⚠ What must NOT be copied: the terminal branch.** `QueuePositionPage.tsx:139-143` and `:158-163` stop the loop on a closed status and on `isNotFound(error)`, and clear the `sessionStorage` pointer. **Every one of those has no subject here and copying them would be a defect:**

- there is no ticket, so no `CLOSED_STATUSES`, no `status` field on the payload at all;
- there is no capability, so no `clearCheckinTicket()` — F59 touches `sessionStorage` **not at all**;
- and **`isNotFound` must not stop this loop.** A 404 on `/storefront/queue` means `TENANT_NOT_FOUND` — a suspended or misconfigured tenant — which is a fact about the *server*, not about a dead link the user holds. F33's page stops because *her ticket is gone and no retry will bring it back*. F59's page has nothing to lose and nobody at the keyboard: **a wall screen that stops permanently needs a human to notice and reload, and there is no human.** So **F59's loop has no terminal at all.** Every failure — 404, 429, 5xx, network — backs off to the 60s cap and keeps trying, at one request a minute, until the server comes back and the board heals itself. That is the whole cost, and it is the right one for an unattended display.

  This is a deliberate divergence from a shipped file a builder will have open beside them, so it is stated here, restated in the Decisions Log, and pinned by a named test (`a 404 does not stop the loop`).

**⚠ "No terminal at all" has exactly ONE exception, and it is `document.hidden`.** The copied `schedule()` (`QueuePositionPage.tsx:105-118`) **returns without arming a timer** whenever `document.hidden` is true, and `tick()` (`:184`) returns as well — so while the tab reports hidden the loop is not slowed, it is **stopped**, and the single path back is the `visibilitychange` listener (`:217-237`). On a phone that is right and self-correcting: she looks at the tab, it wakes. **On a permanently mounted kiosk it is the one state where the board freezes with no automatic recovery and, by D9's own argument, nobody in the room to notice.** Item 4 above discusses `document.hidden` only as a protection; it is both. It is **accepted as a ceiling rather than fixed** — removing the guard would poll a genuinely-hidden tab forever and re-open D6's budget arithmetic — and the mitigation is configuration, not code: **the kiosk requirement is a single full-screen tab with screen-blanking disabled**, which goes on the D10(4) checklist line beside "the TV waits for F58", and the ceiling itself goes to Risk 10, which already owns "a permanently-mounted browser nobody maintains".

**Not copied, no subject:** mutation-in-flight suppression and its re-arm (this page never mutates), the pointer-hold skip, the stranded-row rescue, the scroll-once guard, and the failure-path focus restoration (there is no submit and no row control; the only control is the pause toggle, and D11 covers its focus).

### D10 — `called_at` has no writer, so the board's headline state is unreachable on the day F59 ships. Stated plainly, and the interim designed rather than pretended

**The fact.** `LOOP-STATE.md:501-502` — "CALL: stamps `called_at`, which is what F59's wall board highlights" — belongs to **F58**, which is `status: queued` and not built. `app/models/constants.py:101-104` says the same thing on the enum: "F33 writes only the WAITING default — every transition out of it is F58's, which is why nothing in the shipped product can currently reach a terminal." `app/db/repositories/queue_tickets.py:32-35` says it a third time: `status` and `skip_count` are left to their DB defaults.

**So on ship day, in a real shop:**

- `called_at` is `NULL` on every row. **Nothing is ever highlighted.** `called` is `false` for every entry, always.
- No ticket ever leaves `waiting`. **The board never shrinks except at the midnight `queue_day` roll.** A woman who is served walks away and her row stays on the wall until midnight Jerusalem.
- `waiting_total` therefore counts everyone who checked in today, not everyone still waiting.
- **And because D3 orders by arrival and D4 caps at five on the server, the five rows on the wall are the day's five EARLIEST check-ins — permanently.** At 17:00 on a forty-walk-in day the board displays the first names of five women who arrived at 09:00 and went home at 10:00, plus «ועוד 35 ממתינות». The top of the board freezes at 09:15 and never moves again until midnight. This is the single most visible consequence of the missing writer and an earlier draft did not carry it through from the fact to the cap.

**This is said out loud rather than shipped quietly, because it is what the room would see.** A board whose numbers only ever go up, all day, whose top five names froze mid-morning, is not a queue board — it is an arrivals log with a stale header. Three things follow, and they are the design.

**(1) The board must not claim anything it cannot know.** Specifically:

- **No "next up" label on position 1.** Position 1 means *first in arrival order among today's waiting rows*, which until F58 ships is *the first person who arrived today*, not the next person to be seen. A «הבאה בתור» label would be a lie for most of the day.
- **No wait-time text of any kind.** Pre-decided #28 forbids it independently, and here it would be arithmetic over a number that does not move.
- **The heading is a statement of fact that stays true in both worlds:** «ממתינות בתור» — *waiting in the queue* — which is exactly what the rows are, before and after F58.

**(2) ⚠ THERE IS NO USEFUL INTERIM ON A WALL, and this spec no longer claims one.** An earlier draft claimed the interim answered, for a woman who just scanned at the door, *did my check-in register and how many people were in front of me when I arrived*. **That claim is false on any day with more than five check-ins and it is deleted.** D4 caps the board at five rows on the server, so a woman who scans and lands at position 12 sees five strangers and an overflow line — her own row is not on the board at all, so it can neither confirm her check-in nor show her position. Her `/q/{ticket_id}` page, which is F33's shipped product and which this spec's Problem section opens with, already answers both, better, on the device in her hand. And the count the overflow line offers her is not "people in front of me": under Ruling 3 it counts **tickets**, including her own re-scans, and until F58 it counts **arrivals**, including everyone who went home.

**The two halves of this decision were arguing opposite things and the argument is resolved in one direction: the deployment gate holds.** D10(4) already gates the TV on F58 via F33's Ruling 4, so the interim board **never reaches a room**. What the interim exists for is that the code is written, reviewed, tested and merged — not that a customer reads it. That is the honest position and the brief's instruction to "design the board so it is honest and useful in that interim" is satisfied by honesty, since usefulness is unavailable: **the board must not say anything false, and nothing goes on a wall.**

**One consequence for the copy, decided rather than left implicit.** `queueBoard.overflow` renders «ועוד N ממתינות» — *and N more waiting* — from `waiting_total - entries.length`, a quantity that in the interim is arrivals rather than waiters. **The wording stays**, because it is true in the only world the board is ever deployed into (post-F58, per the gate) and because a second interim string would be a string to un-ship. **Declined: «נרשמו היום» / *registered today***, which is true in both worlds but wrong in the world that ships; **declined: suppressing `waiting_total` until F58**, which would delete the field the overflow line runs on and buy nothing the gate does not already buy.

**(3) The highlight is built and tested anyway, against a stub — the F33 D10 precedent verbatim.** F33 faced the identical problem for its `done`/`removed` terminal and resolved it: "the `done` fixture is seeded by the stubbed API client, which is the only way it can be produced: nothing in F33 writes a terminal status, so there is no product path to drive this through and no backend or e2e assertion may try" (`qr-walkin-queue.md:779`). F59 does the same for `called: true`. The frontend test seeds it; the backend test seeds a row with `called_at` set **directly in the fixture** (which is legitimate — the db suite writes rows, it does not use the product); **no e2e journey may attempt it.**

F59 is downstream of that gate: if the QR sign is not in a window, nobody is in the queue and the board is empty. **F59 adds no second gate and builds no feature flag** — F33 declined a `queue_enabled` tenant setting on the grounds that "the loop's own ordering is the control", and that argument is unchanged.

What F59 adds is **two** lines to the existing gate's checklist:

2. **The kiosk browser runs a single full-screen tab with screen-blanking disabled** — the `document.hidden` ceiling from D9.

**Declined: making the interim useful by bounding the read on row age** (an `AND COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at) > :cutoff` predicate behind a `queue_board_max_row_age_minutes` Settings field). Recorded so a later reader knows the option was weighed.

If F58 flips the status to `in_service` at call time instead, **the called row drops off this board the instant it is called** — the opposite of the feature — because D3's predicate is `status == 'waiting'`, byte-identical to `position()`'s. If F58 needs the status to change at call time, then **`position()` and the board query must change together**, and the ordering-agreement db test (Testing) is what will catch a change to only one. ### D11 — SC 2.2.2 on an unattended wall screen: resolving the tension rather than hand-waving it

**The criterion.** SC 2.2.2 *Pause, Stop, Hide* is **Level A**, Level A is inside AA conformance, and IS 5568 / WCAG 2.0 AA is a **legal** requirement here (pre-decided #38, `architecture.md:20`). It requires that auto-updating information that starts automatically and is presented in parallel with other content have **a mechanism for the user** to pause, stop or hide it, or to control its update frequency. A permanently mounted screen that repaints every five seconds forever is the most literal instance of that criterion in this product. **And axe has no rule for it** — it needs human judgement about what is auto-updating — so this ships green in CI and non-conformant in law unless it is specified and named-tested. F34's D14 established that framing; F33 shipped the control; F59 inherits both.

**The tension, stated properly.** A button on a TV bolted to a wall, with no keyboard, no pointer and nobody standing at it, is a control that satisfies the letter of 2.2.2 and none of its purpose. Pretending otherwise would be the exact "green in CI, non-conformant in law" failure the criterion is meant to catch, reproduced inside the defence against it.

**The resolution, in three parts.**

**(a) The user WCAG addresses is reachable, and it is not the room.** WCAG's "user" is the person operating the user agent. On a wall screen there are two distinct populations and only one of them has a user agent:

- **The room** — women sitting in the salon. They are *viewers*. They have no browser, no keyboard and no pointer, and no web technology can hand them a control. WCAG does not reach them and neither does any implementation.
- **The user** — whoever operates a browser pointed at `https://{slug}.modryn.co.il/queue`. That is the staffer who set the kiosk up, **and every woman in the salon who opens the same public URL on her own phone**, which is a completely ordinary thing to do with a URL that is public by ruling and printed nowhere. For her the page auto-updates under her thumb and the pause control is exactly as operable, exactly as meaningful and exactly as necessary as F33's is on `/q/{ticket_id}`.

So the control ships in the identical shape F33 shipped, and its audience is real rather than notional. **The URL being public — which is the feature's biggest privacy cost — is what makes its 2.2.2 conformance genuine rather than formal.** That is worth noticing, and it is the one place the ruling's cost buys something back.

**(b) For the room, the answer is that the board does not MOVE.** 2.2.2 governs *moving, blinking, scrolling* **and** *auto-updating*. Auto-updating is irreducible — it is the feature. Every other category is optional, and D8 removes all of them: no transition, no crossfade, no flash on refresh, no pulse, no marquee, no auto-scroll, no carousel, no shimmer. A number that changes changes **in place**, at a five-second beat, with no animation. React commits only changed text nodes, so **a board whose data has not changed produces no visual change at all** — the overwhelmingly common case on a boutique floor. The screen is calm to sit next to, which is the substantive protection the room can actually be given, and it discharges SC 2.3.1 and `prefers-reduced-motion` by construction rather than by a media query.

**(c) A pause control the ROOM could reach would be actively harmful, and that is not a dodge.** If a passer-by could freeze the wall board, the next woman to sit down would read a frozen queue as live and act on it. The pause is correct for a user who *knows she pressed it* and can see the paused freshness sentence on her own screen; it would be a trap as a physical control on furniture. The honest protection against a frozen wall is not a lock — it is the **freshness line** (D12), which is on the screen, in the room's reading order, and says «העדכון מושהה. עודכן 14:07» in three distinguishable sentences. **Even the frozen state is labelled, on the wall, in Hebrew.**

**Declined: leaning on 2.2.2's own exception.** The criterion excepts auto-updating that "is part of an activity where it is essential", and a queue board is a plausible candidate. **Do not use it.** The control is cheaper than the argument, F34's spec set the precedent of not leaning on exceptions, and an exception asserted in a spec is not a defence anyone can check.

**Declined: porting F34's idle stop** — for F33's reason, sharpened. F34's board is an unattended *staff* tablet showing named brides' appointments, where inactivity means nobody is there and stopping both saves load and closes an exposure. F59's board is **designed** to be read by people who are not touching it: inactivity is the entire use case, there is no secret to hide by stopping (the content is public by ruling), and a ten-minute timer would freeze a wall screen with nobody in the room to press resume — the exact failure mode (a) is trying to avoid. What the idle stop bought on F34 is bought here by `document.hidden` and D6's budget.

**Declined:** a frequency picker (2.2.2 is satisfied by any one of pause/stop/hide/control-frequency; a dropdown is a settings surface and a persisted preference for a criterion one button discharges). **Declined:** `document.hidden` as the mechanism — it is automatic, and automatic is not "a mechanism for the user"; it is what makes this gap easy to miss. **Declined:** relying on the kiosk browser's own controls — 2.2.2 asks for a mechanism *in the content*.

**The control, mechanically** (copied from `QueuePositionPage.tsx:309-315`):

- **One `<Button variant="ghost" size="md">` whose accessible NAME flips** between `checkin.pause` and `checkin.resume`. **No `aria-pressed`**, and deliberately no `aria-label` variant — an `aria-label` would override the visible text and break the rule that the name **is** the label.
- **Placed BEFORE the auto-updating content in the DOM**, and it is the first control in the section. A 2.2.2 mechanism placed after the content it governs is reachable only by walking content that repaints under the walk.
- **`size="md"` → `min-h-11` = 44px.** `sm` is `min-h-9` = 36px and under the floor, and `sm` is the natural pick beside a freshness line, which is why the prop is pinned and asserted as a **class** (jsdom has no layout engine; a `getBoundingClientRect()` assertion is vacuous — `BoardSection.test.tsx:509-515` is the precedent that spells the trap out, with the `min-h-11` assertion itself at `:513`).
- **Pause** sets `runningRef.current = false`, clears the tick, sets `paused`, writes the paused cue. **Resume** sets running true, **resets the backoff to 5s** (a control that inherited a 60-second gap would look like a control that did not work), bumps the generation and fetches **immediately**.
- **It keeps focus across the press** — the name changes under the user's focus, the node does not.
- **Present in the empty state AND in the error state.** The content auto-updates whether or not there are rows; a control that disappears when the queue empties is a control the user cannot rely on. **The error state gets the same answer and it is decided here rather than left implicit**: F33's page gates the pause row on `live` (`QueuePositionPage.tsx:295`) and that is right *there*, because F33's loop has a terminal and a page that can never poll again must not offer a pause. **F59's loop has no terminal (D9)** — in the error state a request is still going out every 5–60s and will replace the retry arm under the user without her acting. A phone user staring at a retry button that is about to be replaced beneath her thumb is exactly SC 2.2.2's subject. **So the control renders in the error state too**, and the copied `live &&` gate does **not** come across. Asserted in A20.

### D12 — The freshness line is the board's honesty; the poll never writes into `role="status"`

**The freshness line** — visible, in the reading order, **never `aria-hidden`**, and **outside every announced region**. It is the page's only honesty signal, on the one screen in the product that a human will sit and stare at for an hour. Three keys, **reused from F33 rather than re-minted**:

| State | Key | Renders |
|---|---|---|
| live | `checkin.updatedAt` | «עודכן 14:07» |
| stale (a tick failed, data on screen) | `checkin.staleAt` | «העדכון האחרון היה 14:07» |
| paused (user pressed pause) | `checkin.pausedAt` | «העדכון מושהה. עודכן 14:07» |

**Three distinguishable SENTENCES, not one sentence and a colour** — with one string, user-paused and backed-off-stale differ only by a class, which is "status signalled by colour alone" reproduced inside the rule against it. Precedence: **paused beats stale**, because a stopped loop cannot fail a tick (`QueuePositionPage.tsx:270-275` derives exactly this and F59 copies it). The time is formatted through a `timeZone: JERusalem` `Intl.DateTimeFormat` declared multi-line, because `qa-greps.sh` flags a single-line formatter without a zone and a formatter without one reads the **device** clock — on a TV nobody has ever set the clock on.

**Reusing seven `checkin.*` strings rather than duplicating them into `queueBoard.*` is deliberate.** The freshness and pause vocabulary must not diverge between two screens in the same shop, and the values are already approved and already carry their reasoning in comments. `i18n-keys.test.ts` resolves `"checkin.pause"` from `QueueBoardPage.tsx` exactly as it does from `QueuePositionPage.tsx`. New `queueBoard.*` keys exist only for what is genuinely new (D13).

**⚠ There are TWO `role="status"` regions on this page, not one, and A23 must be scoped to the right one.** The page copies both from `QueuePositionPage.tsx`:

| Region | Testid | Lifetime | What may write to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The loading announcement** | `queue-board-loading-status` | A `VisuallyHidden role="status"` carrying `queueBoard.loading`, mounted at first paint and **unmounted on the first settled response** (`QueuePositionPage.tsx:283-285`) | Nothing. It is one-shot: it appears once and goes. Its removal **is** a mutation, and a naive `MutationObserver` scoped to `[role="status"]` observes it on the first tick. |
| **The cue region** | `queue-board-cue` | Persistent for the life of the page (`:375-379`) | The paused and resumed cues, and **nothing else**. |

**A23's assertion — "the poll never writes into `role='status'`" — is about the CUE region and must say so by testid.** Written against `[role="status"]` broadly it goes red on the first tick against *correct* code (the loading region unmounting), gets "fixed" by loosening it, and lands in exactly the vacuous form Risk 4 exists to prevent. The loading region's unmount is named in the test as expected.

**The cue region carries user-initiated outcomes only.** A `role="status"` rewritten every five seconds announces the page forever, passes every automated check, and is unusable. **And the board announces no content at all**, which is a deliberate divergence from `QueuePositionPage.tsx:144-149`: that page announces the `waiting → called` edge because it is *her* ticket and it is the one thing she came for. F59's board is about **other people** — announcing «מיכל, גשי לדלפק» into a stranger's screen reader is both noise and a disclosure she did not ask to broadcast. There is no `called` announcement on this page.

**The "outside the announced region" assertion has a known vacuous form and must not be written that way.** Every live region in this repo is a bare `role="status"` with **no `aria-live` attribute** (`QueuePositionPage.tsx:376`, `BoardSection.tsx:416-420`, `BookPage.tsx:1356`), and `closest()` matches attributes rather than implicit ARIA — so `closest('[aria-live]')` returns `null` **even when the line is nested inside the region**. The assertion is `closest('[role="status"],[role="alert"],[aria-live]')`, **with a negative control** proving the selector matches when the line *is* inside. F33's spec caught this exact vacuity in review; F59 does not re-earn it.

### D13 — The collection notice must say her first name goes on a screen, and F59 is the feature that makes that true

**This is the one privacy-law change F59 forces, and it is a build task in this PR.**

`frontend/apps/storefront/src/i18n/he.ts` `checkin.notice` is shipped, counsel-gated **interim** Hebrew whose header comment says in as many words: *"F20 replaces both VALUES, here and in ar.ts, and that is the whole swap: no component may hardcode any part of either sentence."* Its current value tells her the details are kept «לצורך ניהול התור בלבד — לשמור את מקומך ולקרוא לך כשיגיע תורך» and deleted a few days after the visit.

That was true when F33 shipped. **F59 makes it incomplete**: her first name is now displayed to a room and, because the URL is public and unauthenticated, to anyone who opens it. Amendment 13 requires notice **at the moment of collection**, and the moment of collection is `/checkin`. A notice that does not mention the screen is not that notice.

**⚠ The clause must name the PUBLIC WEB PAGE, not "a screen in the boutique".** An earlier draft specified the amendment as *her first name and her place in the queue are shown on a screen in the boutique, and her surname and phone number are not*, and that wording **reproduces the exact defect D13 exists to correct.** Risk 1 and the ruling establish that the same data is served by an **anonymous endpoint on `https://{slug}.modryn.co.il/queue`, readable by anyone on the internet who knows the slug, refreshed every five seconds**, with `robots.txt` explicitly disclaimed as not a control. A notice describing in-store display when the processing is worldwide publication is not merely incomplete — it is **affirmatively narrower than the truth, at the moment of collection**, which is worse than the current silence, because it becomes an express representation she can rely on. And "her surname… is not shown" is a promise the derivation cannot keep (D5's «כהן נועה» row).

**So F59 amends the interim value of `checkin.notice`** — one string, two files (`he.ts` and `ar.ts`, the latter carrying the approved Hebrew untranslated per Q3), in the slot that exists to be swapped — to add a clause that says, in substance:

- her **place in the queue** and **the first word of the name she typed** are shown on the boutique's **queue board**,
- that board is a **public web page that anyone with the boutique's web address can open** — not only a screen inside the shop,
- and her **phone number** is not shown on it.

**Note what is NOT promised:** the clause says *the first word of the name she typed*, never *her first name and not her surname*. D5 guarantees one token, whichever token she typed first, and the notice must not claim more than the code delivers.

**Constraints on the amendment, which are the same constraints the slot already carries:**

- It is **interim**. The `in_run_gates` F33 entry **stays open** and gains a **fifth counsel item** beside boutique, purpose, retention window and the opted-in-data exception, phrased as the public-URL question so counsel is asked the right one: ***what must the notice say about a first name published on a public, unauthenticated web page?*** — **not** "displayed on a public screen", which invites an answer about signage.
- **No component may hardcode any part of it.** The board page renders no notice text of its own.
- The `CheckinPage` test that asserts the rendered notice **contains the fixture's boutique name** (`CheckinPage.test.tsx:297-306`) is unchanged and still passes — the amendment adds a clause, it does not change the interpolation. **⚠ That test cannot detect whether the amendment happened**: it renders `t("checkin.notice", { boutique })` and compares against the same bundle, so it passes byte-identically against the unamended value. **The amendment therefore needs its own assertion, against the resource bundle rather than through `t()`** — see A32, which an earlier draft wrote as a criterion that could not fail.
- **F59 does not build F20's per-boutique override** and does not invent final privacy wording. That is the Q1 user-only class and this decision declines it exactly as F33 did.

**Declined: shipping the board without touching the notice.** It would leave a shipped sentence that describes a narrower use of her data than the product performs, in the one place the law requires the description to be accurate, on the one surface where the disclosure is to strangers. That is a bigger defect than an interim clause.

**Declined: a notice on the board itself.** The board collects nothing from the people reading it. A privacy notice belongs at the point of collection; putting one on the wall would be reassurance theatre and would spend rows.

## Every state

| State | When | Renders |
|---|---|---|
| **loading** | first paint, before the first response | The heading, a `VisuallyHidden role="status"` (testid `queue-board-loading-status`) with `queueBoard.loading`, and `Skeleton` rows. On a TV this is under a second at boot. **No pause control** — nothing is updating yet. This region **unmounts** on the first settled response, which is a mutation A23 must expect (D12). |
| **default (live)** | `entries.length ≥ 1` | Heading; freshness line + pause control **before** the rows; 1–5 rows, each a `<bdi dir="ltr">` position number and a bare `<bdi>` first name, in a wrapping flex with `min-w-0` + `[overflow-wrap:anywhere]` (D8); the overflow line when `waiting_total > entries.length`. |
| **EMPTY** | `entries.length === 0` and a response has arrived | **Designed, not blank — this is the state the screen is in for most of the day.** Heading; the freshness line **and the pause control, exactly as in the live state**; `queueBoard.empty` («אין כרגע ממתינות») in the same large type as a row, plus `queueBoard.emptyHint` inviting a walk-in to scan the code at the door. **The freshness line is the load-bearing part**: without it an empty board is indistinguishable from a crashed board, and the first thing a staffer does to a blank TV is reboot it. With «עודכן 14:07» ticking, the screen visibly proves it is alive with nothing to show. |
| **error** (nothing ever loaded) | first request failed | Heading; the freshness line **and the pause control** (D11 — the loop is still running, so 2.2.2 still applies here; the shipped `live &&` gate at `QueuePositionPage.tsx:295` is **not** copied); a `role="alert"` paragraph with `queueBoard.loadFailed`; a `secondary` retry `Button`. **The loop keeps running underneath** (D9 — no terminal), so the wall heals itself when the server returns; the retry button exists for the phone user, who is the only one who will ever press it. |
| **stale-data** | something loaded, then a tick failed | **The last good board stays on screen, unchanged**, and the freshness line switches to `checkin.staleAt`. Blanking a correct board because one request failed throws away the only thing the room came to read. This is `loadedRef` at `QueuePositionPage.tsx:166-170`, copied. |
| **paused** | the user pressed pause | Board unchanged; freshness line switches to `checkin.pausedAt`; the control's accessible name flips to `checkin.resume`; `checkin.pausedCue` is announced once through the one `role="status"` region. |

**The ugly edges, each decided:**

| Edge | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **1 person waiting** | One row. No special case, no "you're the only one" copy — same layout, same type scale. A list of one is a list. |
| **40 tickets waiting** | Five rows (positions 1–5) plus «ועוד 35 ממתינות», computed as `waiting_total - entries.length`. **The arithmetic gets its own test** — an off-by-one here is a wrong number on a wall. |
| **A very long first name** | Truncated **server-side** to 11 characters + «…» (D5), so the tail never reaches the wire. The row still carries `min-w-0` + `[overflow-wrap:anywhere]`, because at 200% text-only resize on a 375px phone even 12 characters overflows (D8). |
| **A one-word name** | Shown in full. Exactly what she typed, not one character more. |
| **A name typed surname-first («כהן נועה»)** | «כהן» goes on the wall. **Accepted, unfixable ceiling** (D5) — the field is free text and no heuristic can classify a token. It is why no absolute "the surname never leaves the database" claim survives anywhere in this spec, and why D13's clause says *the first word of the name she typed*. |
| **Two women with the same first name** | Both shown, unchanged. The position number disambiguates. No initial, no suffix. |
| **⚠ ONE woman holding two waiting tickets** | **Both rows render, at two different positions, with the same first name** — and `waiting_total` counts both. This is not an edge case: Ruling 3 deleted server-side dedup (`app/models/queue_ticket.py:23-26`: "a second ticket for the same phone on the same day is a real, expected outcome, and F58 merges or removes it"), F33's D8 records that a QR re-scan opens a fresh context with an empty `sessionStorage` pointer and is "this feature's ordinary re-entry path", and D10 establishes nothing removes it before midnight. **The room cannot tell two women from one woman twice, and the position number cannot disambiguate a woman from herself.** The board **does not and must not deduplicate**: the only key that would is `phone`, and `QueueTicketsRepository`'s class docstring (`queue_tickets.py:15-18`) promises no read is keyed on it. **F58 owns the merge.** A frontend test asserts two entries with an identical `first_name` both render. |
| **A called entry at position 5** (F58 may call out of order) | Highlighted **in place**. **Never reordered** — the number on the wall must equal the number on her phone (D3), and a row that jumps to the top is motion (D8/D11). |
| **Every entry `called: false`, forever, AND the top five frozen** | The interim reality until F58 ships. Nothing is highlighted; the board only grows; and because the order is arrival order and the cap is five, **the five names on the wall are the day's five earliest check-ins and never change until midnight** — women who arrived at 09:00 and left at 10:00 are still on the screen at 17:00. **Which is why D10(4) says the board is not deployable to a wall in the interim at all.** |
| **Midnight Jerusalem, screen mounted for months** | The board empties, because the server computes `today_jerusalem` per request. **Zero client-side date logic** (D3). |
| **A stale `waiting` ticket from an earlier day** | Not on the board (scoped to today) while its own `/q/{id}` page still reports its own day's position. Honest in both places; the only legitimate disagreement between the two screens. |
| **A tie on `COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at)`** | Board order is stable across polls (`, id ASC`); the wall and one phone may disagree by one. Accepted residual, D3. |

- **D1 — `POST /storefront/queue`, the third route on F33's shipped `app/queue/router.py`, overruling the brief's "public GET".** The reason is mechanical, not stylistic: `ROUTES` in `test_storefront_api.py:186-194` is **derived** over every GET under `/storefront`, and `test_the_read_throttle_is_not_inert` (`:1222-1234`) asserts 429 on each against a limiter on `app.state.storefront_rate_limiter`. A GET on the sibling router answers 200 and reddens it; the two available fixes are sharing the catalog's 6000/60s budget (the failure `main.py:720-722` names in writing) or weakening a guard that protects six shipped public reads. `_client()`'s single-service stub and the `FORBIDDEN_KEYS` GET-walk are the same class of dividend. **Explicitly not F33's POST argument** — F33's routes carry a capability in the body; F59's request is empty — so it is recorded as its own conflict. Registering on the existing router buys `_no_store`, the anonymous posture and tenant-from-Host for zero lines. Declined: a new top-level prefix (the storefront dev proxy forwards only `/storefront` and `/health` and has **no** derived guard, so it would break only on a developer's machine).
- **D2 — NO MIGRATION.** `queue_tickets` already carries `tenant_id`, `queue_day`, `name`, `status`, `called_at`, `requeued_at`, `created_at` and `deleted_at` (`models/queue_ticket.py:29-63`), and `idx_queue_tickets_tenant_day_active (tenant_id, queue_day) WHERE deleted_at IS NULL` is already the exact access path (`0018_queue_tickets.py:93-96`, chosen for that reason in its own comment). Stated in bold so a builder does not reflexively generate an empty revision and collide with whatever is in flight. If one seems necessary, that is a **finding** and D2 names the only three shapes it could take and declines all three. The existing guard is F33's pinned CHECK and `pg_indexes.indexdef` literals in `test_migrations.py` — a column or index added here reddens a shipped test, which is why F59 writes no migration test either.
- **D3 — The board reads today's `waiting`, non-deleted tickets of this tenant, ordered `COALESCE(requeued_at, created_at) ASC, id ASC`, capped, plus an untruncated `count(*)`.** The four predicates are **byte-identical to `position()`'s** (`queue_tickets.py:82-88`) so index `i` satisfies `position == i + 1` — that identity is the contract between the wall and her phone, and it is the highest-value db test in the feature. **The day binds to `today_jerusalem(clock)` here and to the ticket's own `queue_day` there**, deliberately: the board must not carry a ghost from an earlier day at position 1 forever, and `position()` must not tell a woman who left yesterday that she is next.
