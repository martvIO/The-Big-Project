## Problem

The boutique owner has no way to touch a booking. F13 shipped the row, F14 shipped the flow that creates it, F16 shipped every SMS the lifecycle sends — and the owner's console has five sections, none of which is bookings. Today the only owner-side booking operation that exists anywhere in the product is a `psql` prompt: `Backend/app/db/repositories/bookings.py` has exactly one status writer (`cancel`, guarded on `status = 'confirmed'`, `bookings.py:168-191`), no writer for `no_show` or `completed`, and no reschedule writer at all. The existing tests that need a no-show booking set the ORM attribute by hand and flush (`test_booking_repositories.py:190-196`) because there is nothing to call.

The consequences compound. `BookingCancelledBy.OWNER` exists with the comment "'owner' has no writer until F15 ships the console" (`app/models/constants.py:54-58`). F16 built four owner comms seams and shipped them with no caller — its own test says so: "F15 has no console yet, so these are unit-level proofs of the seams it will call" (`test_booking_comms_db.py:1044-1069`). A bride whose number was typed wrong gets a control link on a stranger's phone and the boutique's only remedy is to phone her. A slot the owner needs to move can only be moved by cancelling and asking the bride to rebook — which, once E4 lands, means refunding and re-taking a deposit.

None of this is dangerous in the way F13 was. Exactly one part of F15 is: **the phone-correction remedy re-points a live SMS control link at a number nobody verified**, on the owner's word alone. That surface gets the most argument in this document.

## Goal

`apps/manage` gains a sixth section. The owner picks a Jerusalem calendar day, sees every booking on it — cancelled ones included, because a cancelled row is her evidence that the slot re-opened — opens one, and reads the whole thing: customer name and phone, the appointment-type and dress snapshots, her free-text notes, the terms version she accepted and when. From there the owner marks the booking cancelled / no-show / completed / confirmed, corrects a wrong phone number, resends the manage link, or moves the appointment to another slot from a picker fed by the same materializer the storefront uses. Every one of those writes an `audit_log` row inside the same transaction as the change. The cancel and the reschedule fire the customer SMS F16 already built and tested. **F15 ships no migration**, and re-implements no part of the slot grid.

## What already exists to build on (verified against code)

- **The transitions are already legal in the database.** `bookings.status` is `CHECK (status IN ('confirmed','cancelled','no_show','completed'))` (`migrations/versions/0008_bookings.py:65-66`) — the exact four Q6 asks for. `cancelled_by` is `CHECK (cancelled_by IN ('customer','owner'))` (`0010_booking_comms.py:42-45`), and F15 is its first `'owner'` writer. `message_log.kind` already permits `owner_cancel` / `owner_reschedule` (`0007_sms_foundation.py:41-42`).
- **`audit_log` is a real, readable, per-tenant table.** `0003_auth.py:71-79` creates it with `actor_id UUID`, `action TEXT NOT NULL` — **no CHECK** — `entity TEXT`, `details JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'`; `0003_auth.py:83-86` grants full `SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` to `app_user` under FORCE RLS. It is not `platform_audit_log`, which is deliberately INSERT-only (`0004_platform_audit.py:39-40`). `AuditLogRepository.record(session, *, tenant_id, action, actor_id, entity, details)` takes an existing session (`db/repositories/audit_log.py:11-30`), so an audit row joins the caller's transaction. There is no `target_id` column; the auth precedent puts a human-meaningful target string in `entity`.
- **The claim protocol is written down and names F15.** `bookings.py:38-43`: "**Any caller that picks `seat_index` from `active_seats_at` must first hold `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext(tenant_id))`** … F15's owner-side create and reschedule are the next callers." `BookingService.create_booking` is the seven-step reference implementation (`booking/service.py:255-360`).
- **The availability question is already factored for reuse.** `slots.py:1-8`: "the ONE place 'is this time bookable' is decided … F15 offers reschedule targets from it; three implementations of this question would be three chances to disagree." `BookingService._offered_slot` (`service.py:362-399`) is the one-instant wrapper — it re-materializes the grid for the instant's own boutique-calendar date fed with real booked counts, and returns `Slot | None`. `StorefrontService.list_slots` (`storefront/service.py:180-227`) is the window wrapper and returns full `Slot` objects; only the storefront's `public_slots` projection (`storefront/router.py:223-231`) strips `capacity` and `remaining`.
- **Four comms seams, built and callerless.** `comms.py:187-204` `notify_owner_cancel`, `:206-243` `notify_owner_reschedule`, `:245-269` `reissue_manage_token` ("F15's edit-phone remedy" — which D8 ends up **not** calling, for transaction reasons argued there), `:273-316` `reschedule_reminder` ("An UPSERT, never a re-target" — whose body D11 promotes so F15 can run it in its own transaction). All construct `CommsTenant.from_settings(tenant_id, slug, name, settings)` from the same `TenantContext` the manage routers already resolve.
- **The `/manage` posture is established.** Three routers mount `prefix="/manage"` (`auth/router.py:13`, `boutique/router.py:28`, `catalog/router.py:56`) and are included in a fixed order with an explicit shadowing warning (`main.py:552-556`). `Depends(get_current_staff)` is the codebase's one and only owner-only marker (`auth/dependencies.py:18-27`; `storefront/router.py:20-23` states that its *absence* is what makes a route public). `CsrfOriginMiddleware` protects mutating methods under `/manage` and nowhere else (`csrf.py:15-16, 48`).
- **The house shapes.** Paginated lists answer `{items, total, offset, limit}` with a `*_DEFAULT_LIMIT` / `*_MAX_LIMIT` pair (`catalog/router.py:139-141`, `catalog/schemas.py:160-165`) and an `MAX_LIST_OFFSET` clamp below the router (`catalog/service.py:73-81`, `storefront/service.py:61-62`). Request models subclass `ForbidExtraModel` (`app/schemas.py:12-16`). `DomainNotFoundError` / `DomainValidationError` (`app/errors.py`) give a new module the house 404 and 400 with no `main.py` change, because the handlers are bound to the bases (`main.py:401-413`).
- **The console frontend.** `apps/manage` has no router — sections are a `useState<SectionKey>` plus a `nav` array (`App.tsx:13, 19, 50-56, 68-74`). List→detail is an in-component state swap that patches the row from the mutation response rather than refetching (`CatalogSection.tsx:39-40, 73-107`, and the comment at `:78-80` explaining why). `api.ts` is a hand-written typed fetch client speaking the backend's snake_case verbatim with `credentials: "include"` and an `ApiError {status, code, message}` (`apps/manage/src/api.ts:1-18, 40-63`).

## Design

### No migration (D1)

F15 adds no table, no column, no index, no CHECK widening. Every fact above checks out: the four statuses are already pinned, `'owner'` is already a legal `cancelled_by`, both owner `message_log` kinds are already allowed, and `audit_log.action` is unconstrained TEXT. New `AuditAction` members are therefore StrEnum additions only — the same fact `PlatformAuditAction.BOOKING_LINKS_BACKFILLED` already leans on in a comment (`constants.py:88-91`: "platform_audit_log.action is plain TEXT with no CHECK (0004), so this needs no migration").

This is a design **constraint**, stated at Gate 1 so that any field which would break it is a deliberate decision here rather than a surprise at build time. Two were considered and declined: a `rescheduled_from TIMESTAMPTZ` column (the audit row's `details` carries the old instant; a column would be the same evidence in a place nothing queries) and an `owner_attested_phone BOOLEAN` (the audit row already records the attestation, and a flag on `customers` would be a permanent mark on a row that may later be verified by a real OTP in E5's client login).

`test_every_tenant_id_table_has_forced_rls` staying green is the assertion that F15 did not sneak a table in.

### The audit log is `audit_log` (D2)

The epic brief demands "status transitions with audit log" (`e3-booking-and-comms.md:60`). It gets `audit_log`, not a new `booking_status_events` table.

The case for a dedicated table rests on one premise — that the operator audit log is INSERT-only and therefore unreadable by the app — and that premise names the wrong table. `platform_audit_log` is the INSERT-only one (`0004_platform_audit.py:39-40` revokes then grants INSERT alone). Tenant-scoped `audit_log` has full CRUD under FORCE RLS (`0003_auth.py:83-86`) and already carries exactly the five fields a transition needs: `tenant_id`, `actor_id`, `action`, `entity`, `details JSONB`. A parallel table would be a second audit surface with its own RLS policy, its own grants, its own retention rule under F20 and its own migration, so that F15 could store `{from, to}` in typed columns instead of JSONB.

**New `AuditAction` members** (`app/models/constants.py`, no migration):

| Member | Value | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| `BOOKING_CONFIRMED` | `booking_confirmed` | the undo of a mis-tapped no-show/completed |
| `BOOKING_CANCELLED` | `booking_cancelled` | owner cancel |
| `BOOKING_NO_SHOW` | `booking_no_show` | mark no-show |
| `BOOKING_COMPLETED` | `booking_completed` | mark completed |
| `BOOKING_RESCHEDULED` | `booking_rescheduled` | reschedule |
| `BOOKING_PHONE_CORRECTED` | `booking_phone_corrected` | phone correction |
| `BOOKING_LINK_RESENT` | `booking_link_resent` | resend the manage link |

Declined: one `booking_status_changed` action carrying the transition in `details`. Per-action values cost nothing (they are enum members), make a future filtered read one `WHERE action = …` instead of a JSONB predicate, and read correctly in `AuditLogRepository.list_actions`.

Every row is `actor_id=staff.id` (from `StaffContext`, `auth/service.py:19-25`), `entity=str(booking.id)` — the auth precedent puts a human-meaningful target string in `entity` — and `details` carrying the payload:

| Action | `details` |
|---|---|
| status transitions | `{"from": "confirmed", "to": "no_show"}` |
| reschedule | `{"old_starts_at": "...Z", "new_starts_at": "...Z", "old_seat_index": 3, "new_seat_index": 1}` |
| phone correction | `{"old_phone_last4": "4567", "new_phone_last4": "4576", "attested": true, "old_customer_id": "…", "new_customer_id": "…", "repointed": false, "rotated_booking_ids": ["…", "…"]}` |
| link resent | `{"customer_id": "…"}` |

**Full phone numbers are deliberately not in `details`.** `audit_log` is retained on the audit clock, not the booking clock, and a full number in JSONB is a second uncontrolled copy of the one PII field this feature exists to edit.

**But last4 alone cannot reconstruct the correction**, and a reviewer was right about it: on the non-collision path `set_phone` overwrites `customers.phone` in place and `customers` has no history table, so the number the link was pointed *away from* would survive nowhere. Hence the customer ids above, on **both** paths and on the resend: ids are not PII on their own, the customer rows are the join, and `old_customer_id` + `rotated_booking_ids` are what let an Amendment-13 complaint three months later be answered at all — "which number was this link pointed away from, and which other links moved with it". That is the whole reason the audit row exists on this surface (Risk 2).

**Nothing reads these rows in v1.** The detail response already carries the current status plus `cancelled_at` / `cancelled_by`, which is what an owner asks the screen. A history endpoint over a table that already grants SELECT is a follow-up, not scope — and pretending otherwise would put a list nobody asked for in front of a 720px content cap. Recorded as Risk 7 so a reader of the epic brief finds a ruling rather than a gap.

### The transition graph (D3)

Nothing in the codebase enforces a transition graph today; the two existing writers guard by SQL predicate (`bookings.py:158-159` `confirm_attendance`, `:183-184` `cancel`). F15 introduces the first graph.

**Correction (review).** The Gate-1 draft said the graph stays "in the UPDATE predicate, not in a Python pre-check" and cited the house pattern. Both halves were wrong and the rule was unbuildable:

- A predicate-guarded UPDATE returns zero rows for an illegal transition **and** for a legal repeat, so nothing in the statement result separates the 409 from the 200 the very next paragraph promises.
- The cited precedent is Python. `ManageBookingService.cancel` does `if booking.status == CANCELLED: return await self._render(...)` (`manage.py:158-161`) and `if booking.starts_at <= now: raise BookingAlreadyStartedError` (`manage.py:163-164`) — read-then-check, in Python, before the write. `bookings.py:149-152` is about idempotent *timestamp* writing, not a transition graph.

**The rule, written down.** The house shape is read → compare → return-or-raise → guarded write, and F15 adopts it openly:

1. Load the booking inside the transaction. Missing → 404.
2. `booking.status == target` → **200, render unchanged, write no audit row.** A no-op is not a transition, so there is nothing to record; `{from: "no_show", to: "no_show"}` rows would be noise in the one trail this feature has.
3. The pair or the clock is illegal per the table below → **409 `BOOKING_TRANSITION_INVALID`**, carrying the refused pair in the message. Nothing written.
4. Otherwise the guarded UPDATE runs, carrying the same predicate as belt to the Python braces (the predicate is what makes the write safe under a concurrent writer; the Python check is what makes the *answer* honest). A zero-row result from that UPDATE means another request moved the row between step 1 and step 4 → **409 `BOOKING_TRANSITION_INVALID`**, transaction rolled back, no audit row.
5. Audit row, same transaction, before commit.

**The writers, named** — the Gate-1 draft enumerated `reschedule`, `list_day` and `set_phone` and left the feature's headline capability with no writer at all. `no_show`, `completed` and the confirm-undo all go through **one** new method:

```python
async def set_status(
    self, session, tenant_id, booking_id, *,
    to: str, allowed_from: tuple[str, ...],
    not_before: datetime | None = None,   # requires starts_at > not_before
    not_after: datetime | None = None,    # requires starts_at <= not_after
) -> Booking | None
```

`None` means the predicate matched nothing. Per endpoint: `/no-show` → `to='no_show', allowed_from=('confirmed','completed'), not_after=now`; `/complete` → `to='completed', allowed_from=('confirmed','no_show'), not_after=now`; `/confirm` → `to='confirmed', allowed_from=('no_show','completed')` (no clock bound — the undo of a mis-tap is legal whenever the mis-tap is noticed).

**Cancel keeps the shipped writer.** `BookingsRepository.cancel` (`bookings.py:168-191`) writes `cancelled_at` / `cancelled_by` as well as the status, and it is **shared with the customer path** (`manage.py:165-171`). Its predicate has no clock component, and widening it would silently invert F16's contract: `cancel()` always returns a row through its trailing `by_id`, and `ManageBookingService.cancel` renders `updated if updated is not None else booking` (`manage.py:178`) — so a customer cancelling in the seconds around `starts_at` would get a 200 rendering an *un*-cancelled booking instead of the 409 `BOOKING_ALREADY_STARTED` her page's states are built on. **Ruling: `cancel` gains one optional keyword, `not_before: datetime | None = None`, adding `starts_at > not_before` to the predicate only when passed.** F15 passes `now`; `ManageBookingService.cancel` does not pass it and is not touched, so its `BookingAlreadyStartedError` contract is unchanged by construction. Declined: a second `cancel_future` method (two writers for one statement, and the customer path's Python clock check would still be the one that matters).

`/confirm` writes `status` **only**. It never touches `attendance_confirmed_at`, which is F16's reminder-link column (`bookings.py:146-167`) and means something different — the bride said she is coming. Undoing a mis-tapped `no_show` leaves whatever `attendance_confirmed_at` already held, set or NULL: the owner is correcting her own record of the outcome, not speaking for the bride. Declined: renaming the path to `/reopen` to avoid sharing a verb with the customer page's confirm-attendance action. The console's four buttons are named for the four statuses and `confirmed` is one of them; a fifth vocabulary would be the larger confusion. Recorded so the collision reads as a ruling.

| From | To | Legal? | Guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| `confirmed` | `cancelled` | ✅ | `status='confirmed' AND starts_at > now` |
| `confirmed` | `no_show` | ✅ | `status='confirmed' AND starts_at <= now` |
| `confirmed` | `completed` | ✅ | `status='confirmed' AND starts_at <= now` |
| `no_show` | `completed` | ✅ | `status IN ('no_show','completed')` |
| `completed` | `no_show` | ✅ | same |
| `no_show` / `completed` | `confirmed` | ✅ | same — the undo of a mis-tap |
| `no_show` / `completed` | `cancelled` | ❌ | — |
| `cancelled` | anything | ❌ | — |

**The `starts_at` boundary is the whole shape.** Cancelling is a thing you do to a *future* appointment — it frees a seat somebody else can take and it sends the customer an SMS. Marking no-show or completed is a thing you do to a *past* one; both are attendance records. The split falls exactly at `now`, so a past confirmed booking's only moves are no-show and completed, and a future one's only move is cancel. A booking sitting `confirmed` two days after its time is not an error state — it is the owner not having got to it yet.

**`no_show` and `completed` are mutually reversible, and both revert to `confirmed`.** Neither frees a seat (`bookings.py:93-106`: "`status <> 'cancelled'` mirrors the unique index predicate exactly … only a cancellation frees it"), neither touches money, and mis-tapping two adjacent buttons on a phone is the likeliest owner error in the whole feature. Reversal is seat-neutral and index-neutral, so it costs one predicate.

**`cancelled` is terminal.** Reviving a cancelled booking re-enters both partial unique indexes — `idx_bookings_slot_seat_unique` (`0008:88-92`) and `idx_bookings_tenant_customer_starts_unique` (`0009:32-36`) — against a seat that may since have been sold to someone else, so the UPDATE would fail with an `IntegrityError` in the common case and silently double-book in the race. Declined: an un-cancel that re-runs the seat pick. That is a reschedule to the original instant with extra steps, and it would need the whole D5 protocol to be correct. The remedy for a mis-tapped cancel is a rebook, which is why cancel gets a confirm Modal and why the customer's cancelled-state page already carries a rebook link (pre-decided #7). Recorded as Risk 1: owner-created bookings are out of scope, so the rebook must come from the customer or from the storefront on her behalf.

**`no_show` / `completed` → `cancelled` is forbidden** for a different reason: E4 #19 evaluates refund-due versus forfeit from `cancelled_at`, `starts_at` and the accepted terms version (`models/booking.py:49-53`), and writing `cancelled_at` on a booking the bride actually attended would make that evaluation lie.

**A repeat of the same transition is a 200, not an error, and writes no audit row** — step 2 above, the `manage.py:158-161` shape.

**The late-recorded cancellation has no in-product answer, deliberately.** A bride phones on Sunday to cancel Monday's 10:00 and the owner does not open the console until Monday 11:00: `confirmed → cancelled` is now refused by the clock, and the graph offers only `no_show` — which asserts something false about a bride who did cancel, on a field E4 #19 will read. Considered and declined: allowing a past `confirmed → cancelled` with the seat release and the SMS suppressed. The seat is worthless once the instant has gone and `notify_owner_cancel` for a past appointment is noise, so the suppression is cheap — but it puts a second, quieter cancel path into the one graph this feature exists to make legible, and it writes `cancelled_at` *after* `starts_at`, a shape E4's refund arithmetic has never been asked to read. **The pilot answer is that the owner marks nothing**: D3 already rules that a booking sitting `confirmed` after its time is not an error state, so "confirmed and past, never marked" is a perfectly readable record of "she told us she wasn't coming". Recorded as Risk 8 rather than served, because the honest fix is a `cancelled` reason field that E4 will need anyway.

### Reschedule (D4, D5) — the hard part

**Reschedule is an in-place UPDATE of `(starts_at, seat_index)` on the same row.** Declined: cancel-and-reinsert. It would (i) write `cancelled_at` / `cancelled_by='owner'` on a booking that was never cancelled, which is exactly the E4 lie above; (ii) break the customer's manage link and orphan the `scheduled_messages` row, which is keyed on `booking_id`; (iii) mint a new id the audit trail would have to bridge; (iv) force a re-snapshot of `terms_version_accepted` / `terms_accepted_at`, destroying the evidence `bookings` exists to hold; and (v) break the deposit carry-over the epic promises in the same sentence that asks for reschedule ("deposit … carries over, no re-payment round-trip", `e3-booking-and-comms.md:60`) — a deposit is attached to a row, and keeping the row is what makes the carry-over free rather than a transfer E4 has to implement. The only thing an in-place UPDATE loses is the record of the old time, which is precisely what the audit row the same epic line demands already carries.

The protocol, in **one** `tenant_session` (`db/tenant.py:25-30` — a real committing transaction with transaction-local RLS context):

0. **Horizon guard before any arithmetic.** `if not now < new_starts_at <= now + BOOKABLE_HORIZON: raise SlotUnavailableError`. Copied verbatim from `service.py:180-186`, for its reason: `AwareDatetime` accepts the whole datetime range and `.astimezone()` on a year-9999 instant raises `OverflowError` — an unhandled 500. Comparison itself cannot overflow, so the guard is total.
1. **`SELECT pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext(:tenant_id))` — before any read of the booking.** The obligation `bookings.py:38-43` records by name. Skipping it does not oversell — the index is the backstop — but it races the read and hands the owner a spurious 409 every time a public create lands on the same instant.

   **Correction (review): the Gate-1 draft read the booking first and locked third, and that ordering is a bug.** `create_booking` takes the lock *before* every read it depends on and says why in the code (`service.py:283-286`: "The read cannot race: every claim for this tenant takes the advisory lock above before running it"). With the read outside the lock, two submissions of the same reschedule dialog both read `starts_at = T0`; A moves the booking to T2 and commits; B wakes holding a stale row, compares its target T2 against the stale T0, misses the no-op short-circuit, and step 5's `active_at(customer_id, T2)` then finds **the booking itself** and answers 409 `CUSTOMER_ALREADY_BOOKED` — the exact self-collision step 3 exists to prevent (at capacity 1 it is a 409 `SLOT_UNAVAILABLE` against its own seat instead). The audit row is wrong too: it would record `T0 → T2` for a move that was `T1 → T2`, on the feature whose epic line demands an audit log. Every value below — the status check, the short-circuit comparison, `old_starts_at`, `old_seat_index` — comes from the **post-lock** read.
2. **Load and check.** `bookings.by_id`; missing → 404. `status != 'confirmed'` → 409 `BOOKING_TRANSITION_INVALID`. **`booking.starts_at <= now` → 409 `BOOKING_TRANSITION_INVALID` as well.** Only a confirmed *future* booking moves: a cancelled one is terminal, and a past one is an attendance question. (Review caught the draft guarding only on status, which let a Tuesday no-show be silently rewritten into next week's appointment — overwriting the very `starts_at`/attendance record D3 forbids lying about, and texting the bride a confirmation for the appointment she missed. The errors section already said "reschedule … on a booking that is not confirmed-and-future"; the protocol now agrees with it.)
3. **Short-circuit `new_starts_at == booking.starts_at`.** Return the booking unchanged; no audit row, no SMS. This is not a nicety — `active_at` and `active_seats_at` have no booking-id exclusion (`bookings.py:74-106`), so a no-op move would find *itself* and 409 a capacity-1 booking against its own seat. Declined: adding an `exclude_booking_id` parameter to both repository methods. Those predicates mirror the two unique indexes exactly and that mirroring is what makes them trustworthy; a self-exclusion that no index has would break the correspondence to fix a case one `if` handles.
4. **`_offered_slot(session, tenant_id, starts_at=new, now=now)` → `None` ⇒ 409 `SLOT_UNAVAILABLE`.** This one call buys every availability rule at once: past instants are dropped (`slots.py:140`), off-grid times never materialize, closed days and exception days resolve correctly, nonexistent DST wall times are dropped and ambiguous ones pinned to `fold=0` (`slots.py:81-104`), and **full slots are dropped rather than marked** (`slots.py:150-152`), so capacity at the target is enforced by the slot simply not being there.
5. **Per-customer collision check.** `active_at(customer_id=booking.customer_id, starts_at=new)` → hit ⇒ 409 `CUSTOMER_ALREADY_BOOKED`. `idx_bookings_tenant_customer_starts_unique` (`0009:32-36`) means "one person cannot be at two appointments at one instant, whatever their types", and this is a genuinely different failure from a full slot: the target can have free seats and still be unmovable-into for *this* bride. Step 3 already excluded the row itself, so a hit here is always another booking.
6. **Pick the lowest free seat at the target.** `seats = active_seats_at(new)`; `next((i for i in range(1, slot.capacity + 1) if i not in seats), None)`; `None` ⇒ 409 `SLOT_UNAVAILABLE`. Identical to `service.py:312-319`. **Carrying the old `seat_index` across would be a silent oversell**: seat 3 moved into a capacity-1 target satisfies the `CHECK (seat_index >= 1 AND seat_index <= 1000)` (`0008:65`) *and* the slot-seat unique index, because nothing in the database bounds a seat by its slot's capacity — capacity is enforced in Python and nowhere else.
7. **One guarded UPDATE.** New `BookingsRepository.reschedule(session, tenant_id, booking_id, *, starts_at, seat_index, not_before)` setting both columns, `WHERE status = 'confirmed' AND starts_at > :not_before AND deleted_at IS NULL`, `RETURNING id`. An `IntegrityError` from `idx_bookings_slot_seat_unique` becomes `SlotUnavailableError` — the same backstop mapping `create_booking` uses (`service.py:342-345`). The move needs no extra write to release the source seat: the index is re-evaluated at statement time over the row's new values.

   **Zero rows is not silence.** The per-tenant lock serializes this against public creates but **not** against the four owner status endpoints, which take no lock (they touch one row and need none) — so a concurrent owner cancel between step 2's read and this UPDATE flips the row to `cancelled` and the statement writes nothing. A zero-row result therefore raises `BookingTransitionInvalidError` (409) and the transaction rolls back **before** the audit row, rather than committing evidence for a move that did not happen and rendering the booking at its old time as though it had.
8. **The reminder upsert and the audit row**, in the same transaction, before commit — see D11 for why the reminder write moved inside.

**Losing the race.** With the lock held, a concurrent public create at the target blocks until this transaction commits; if it committed first, step 6 sees the seat taken and answers 409 through the normal path. A writer that skipped the lock is caught by step 7's `IntegrityError` and produces the same 409. Either way the owner sees "that time was just taken" and picks another — never a double-booked room. This is the same guarantee `create_booking` gives, from the same two mechanisms.

**No owner override of published availability.** An off-grid placement has no capacity to pick a seat against — the grid is the only thing that produces one, and `DEFAULT_SLOT_CAPACITY = 1` is a fallback for exception days, not a general answer (`slots.py:75`, `validation.py:28`). An override would bypass the oversell arithmetic entirely and create a booking `/storefront/slots` never offered. An owner who needs an unusual hour opens it as an availability exception in the שעות פעילות section (F7) and then reschedules. Declined explicitly so this reads as a ruling, not an omission.

**`_offered_slot` moves.** Two services now need it, and it is a private method on `BookingService` (`service.py:362`). It is promoted to a module-level coroutine in `app/booking/slots_io.py` — `offered_slot(session, *, tenant_id, starts_at, now, rules, exceptions, bookings)` — taking the three repositories it already uses. **`_offered_slot` is deleted, not kept as a delegate**: the Gate-1 draft justified the delegate with "so F13's tests keep their entry point", and no test calls it — the only references in the repo are the definition (`service.py:362`), its single call site (`service.py:300`) and a docstring mention (`test_booking_service.py:1050`). `create_booking` calls `offered_slot(...)` directly. Declined: making the owner reschedule a method on `BookingService` purely for access (it would put owner-only, staff-authenticated logic behind the anonymous booking service's constructor), and duplicating the twelve lines (`slots.py:6-8` names three-implementations-disagreeing as the failure this module exists to prevent).

### `GET /manage/slots` — a sibling, not a reuse (D6)

The reschedule picker needs slots. `GET /storefront/slots` already returns them and would work on the wire. It is still the wrong endpoint, for three reasons that are each sufficient:

1. **It spends the wrong budget.** The storefront router carries a router-level `Depends(_throttle)` keyed `f"storefront:{tenant.id}"` (`storefront/router.py:125-137`) — a per-tenant *anonymous read* budget. An owner opening the reschedule dialog three times would eat the ceiling that exists to stop a scraper, and degrade the customer-facing endpoint from inside the console.
2. **It is contractually blind to exactly what the owner needs.** `public_slots` ships start times only: "Start times only — neither `capacity` NOR `remaining` reaches the wire" (`storefront/router.py:223-231`), and `availability-slot-engine.md:79` argues at length why `remaining` was removed rather than kept. `capacity` is in the storefront tests' `FORBIDDEN_KEYS` and the wire-absence walk (`test_storefront_api.py:203-232`) actively asserts it off that surface. The owner picking a reschedule target legitimately wants to know a target has two seats left rather than one. Reusing the route would mean either shipping the owner a fenced field on the fenced surface, or shipping her a picker that hides the fact she is about to take the last place.
3. **The console cannot reach it in dev.** `apps/manage/vite.config.ts:9-19` proxies `/manage` and `/health` only, with `changeOrigin: false` to preserve the tenant Host header. Reuse means a proxy entry too, so "reuse" is not even the smaller diff.

The sibling costs almost nothing, because the computation is already factored. `StorefrontService.list_slots` returns full `Slot` objects (`storefront/service.py:180-227`) — capacity and remaining are only lost in the storefront's projection — so `GET /manage/slots` is that same call plus an owner projection. No second materializer, which is the one thing `slots.py:1-8` forbids.

**It inherits `SlotWindowError`, and that belongs in the error table.** `list_slots` runs `slot_window`, which raises on `to < from` (`storefront/service.py:305-306`). `SlotWindowError` subclasses `DomainValidationError` (`storefront/validation.py:57`), so the app-wide handler bound to the base already answers it as a 400 `VALIDATION_ERROR` and F15 adds no handler — but the row has to be in the table below, or the pinned `SPEC_ERROR_CODES` literal is wrong on day one and the guard is vacuous for the one case it would have caught.

**No throttle on the owner route.** It is session-authed, CSRF-fenced and reachable only from a console the operator provisioned. A per-tenant read budget there is a self-DoS with no attacker on the other side of it.

**No `booking_id` parameter.** The engine drops full slots, so a capacity-1 target that the booking itself occupies never appears — meaning "reschedule, then change your mind" would find no way back to the original time. That is a client rendering problem and it gets a client answer: the dialog renders the booking's current `starts_at` (which the detail response already carries) as a pre-selected, always-present option, and step 2's no-op short-circuit makes re-submitting it free. Declined: a server parameter that re-injects the occupied slot into the grid — a second code path through the materializer to solve a display default.

#### Phone correction (D8) — the dangerous surface

Interview Q6 puts "correct a wrong phone number" in scope and puts owner-*created* bookings out of it, on the grounds that "the SMS control link would target an unverified number". Correcting a phone targets an unverified number too. The difference is real but narrow, and it is the whole argument for shipping this at all: the booking already exists with an OTP-verified acceptance behind it and a terms version pinned to it; the correction is a **remedy for a number that demonstrably does not work**, not the creation of a new relationship. Requiring an OTP round trip would require the bride to be reachable at the broken number — or the owner to read a code to her over the phone she is already holding, which is an OTP that proves nothing except that a phone call happened.

So: **the corrected number is owner-attested. No OTP.** The epic brief authorises exactly this choice — "edit customer phone (**re-verify or owner-attested**) + resend confirmation/token SMS" (`e3-booking-and-comms.md:60`) — and F15 picks the second branch.

**It nevertheless narrows an invariant E3 wrote down three times, and the spec must say so rather than only file it as a risk** (Interview line 7 asks specs to record conflicts with shipped reality). The three sentences:

- `e3-booking-and-comms.md:18` — why OTP exists at all: "the tokenized SMS link is the customer's only control surface in v1; an unverified number strands paid customers and creates Spam-Law liability."
- `e3-booking-and-comms.md:54` (F13 brief) — "Customer record created/attached by (tenant, phone) **only after OTP verification proves possession of the number**."
- `booking-core.md:10` restates it.

After D8 a `customers.phone` can be an owner-typed value with no possession proof behind it, and F15 texts that number immediately. **F15 narrows the invariant to: verified at creation, owner-attested thereafter, with an audit row naming the actor, both customer ids and every booking whose link moved.** Recorded as Risk 2 — and Risk 2's owner moves from *team* to **user**, its trigger from "F21 audit" to **"Gate 1 — one-line acknowledgement before build"**, the same routing Risk 3 already gets for a smaller call. Q1's enumeration keeps F15 self-approving overall; this is one flagged line, not a gate stop.

Four mechanics make it as safe as an attested edit can be:

1. **Normalize first.** Through `normalize_israeli_mobile` (the same function the booking claim uses, `booking/service.py:49, 176`), so the stored value stays E.164 like every other phone in the product. A malformed number is a 400 `VALIDATION_ERROR` before anything is written.
2. **The collision is a re-point, not a merge and not a refusal.** `idx_customers_tenant_phone_unique` pins `(tenant_id, phone)` for live rows (`0008:45-48`), and `CustomersRepository` today has no phone writer at all — only `by_phone` / `by_id` / `upsert`, and `upsert` keys on phone, so calling it with the corrected number would create a *second* customer and leave the booking pointing at the first. F15 adds `set_phone(session, tenant_id, customer_id, *, phone)`, one UPDATE. When the corrected number already belongs to another live customer of this tenant, the UPDATE would raise; instead the service **re-points `bookings.customer_id` at the existing customer row** and leaves both customer records intact. That is one statement, it is what the owner means (the number identifies a person, and that person already has a record with her own history), and it destroys nothing. Declined: merging the two customer rows (needs a policy for the name, and a reattachment pass over every other booking — a data-migration problem F15 has no business inventing) and refusing with a 409 (the owner's only remaining remedy would be to cancel and rebook, and rebooking is out of scope).
   The original customer row keeps the wrong number. Deliberately: it may be a real other person's number, and soft-deleting on a guess is worse than leaving a row nobody looks at.

   **The re-point can violate 0009's index, and that is a 409, not a 500** (review). Moving `bookings.customer_id` touches a second unique index the draft never considered: `idx_bookings_tenant_customer_starts_unique ON bookings (tenant_id, customer_id, starts_at) WHERE deleted_at IS NULL AND status <> 'cancelled'` (`0009:32-36`). Two sisters in one capacity-2 slot at 14:00, the owner corrects the first one's number to the second one's: the re-point puts two live rows on `(tenant, customer B, 14:00)` and the flush raises. With no error-registry fallback (`main.py:96`) that escapes as a bare 500 outside the house error shape. **Ruling:** before the re-point, run `active_at(customer_id=<target customer>, starts_at=booking.starts_at)` and raise `CustomerAlreadyBookedError` on a hit — 409 `CUSTOMER_ALREADY_BOOKED`, a code this feature already defines — with the flush's `IntegrityError` mapped to the same error as the backstop, the way `create_booking` treats its own race (`service.py:342-345`). A boutique where a party books together makes this ordinary, not exotic.
3. **The correction rotates the link — inside the same transaction as the phone write, on every booking the write affects.** The draft made two mistakes here that review caught, and they compound:

   **(a) It was two transactions.** The draft wrote the phone in transaction 1 and called `reissue_manage_token` after commit — which opens its own `tenant_session` (`comms.py:255`), returns `None` on any miss (`:257-258`), discards `set_manage_token_hash`'s `None` (`:260-262`), and is awaited-and-discarded by the router pattern (`router.py:95-107`). So "unconditionally" was false: a redeploy, a dropped pooled connection or a raise between the two leaves the booking carrying the **corrected phone and the original `manage_token_hash`** — the stranger's link still resolves through `by_manage_token_hash` (`bookings.py:108-124`) and still cancels the bride's appointment at `POST /storefront/booking/cancel`, which has no phone check (`manage.py:180-188`). Nothing sweeps for it and nothing tells the owner. Worse, it made the status guard below a TOCTOU: guard in tx1, rotate in tx2, and the stranger's cancel in between leaves tx2 minting a **fresh live control token on a cancelled booking** and texting a confirmation for an appointment that no longer exists — verbatim the outcome the guard claims to prevent.

   **(b) It rotated one booking while rewriting the phone on all of them.** `customers` *is* the phone identity, so `set_phone` moves every booking that customer holds onto the new number — but `manage_token_hash` is per-row (`0010:37`). A bride holding two live bookings against the same mistyped number keeps a working stranger-held cancel link on the second one, and her reminders start going to her while the stranger's link keeps working, because `_customer_phone` reads the customer row at send time (`comms.py:437-454`). That is exactly the harm this rotation exists to prevent.

   **Ruling — the rotation is part of the write, not an after-effect:**

   | | in the transaction | after commit |
   |---|---|---|
   | non-collision (`set_phone`) | phone UPDATE; then for **every live booking of that customer** (`status='confirmed' AND starts_at > now`, one query over `idx_bookings_tenant_customer`, `0008:101-104`): mint a token, `set_manage_token_hash`, re-point that booking's pending reminder; audit | `send_confirmation` for the **edited** booking only, carrying the token minted for it |
   | collision (re-point) | 0009 pre-check; `bookings.customer_id` UPDATE; rotate **this booking only**; audit | same |

   **The "every live booking of that customer" read needs a name, and did not have one (C2, amended at plan time).** `BookingsRepository` ships nothing that answers it: `active_at` is one instant, `list_confirmed_without_manage_token` filters on `manage_token_hash IS NULL`, and `count_by_start` is a count. F15 adds **`BookingsRepository.list_live_for_customer(session, tenant_id, *, customer_id, after) -> list[Booking]`**, predicate `customer_id = :c AND status = 'confirmed' AND starts_at > :after AND deleted_at IS NULL`, ordered by `starts_at`, riding `idx_bookings_tenant_customer` (`0008:101-104`). It is exactly the query the parenthesis above describes; the Gate-1 draft simply never gave it one, and the D5 writer list did not carry it.

   `set_manage_token_hash` already takes a session (`bookings.py:126-144`) and so does `ScheduledMessagesRepository.pending_for_booking`, so this is same-database work in the transaction that owns it — the rule F16 wrote down for itself (`service.py:169-173`: "same-database work belongs in the transaction"). It also gains the guard predicate directly (`status='confirmed' AND starts_at > now`), per D3's rule that the write carries the predicate. **A `None` return from any rotation is a hard failure that rolls the transaction back**, never a discarded result.

   Consequence for F16's seam: **`reissue_manage_token` is not called by F15 after all.** It bundles mint + rotate + re-point + send across its own transaction, and the mint-rotate-repoint half must be inside ours. F15 calls the public `send_confirmation(tenant, booking=…, manage_token=…)` post-commit with the token it already minted, which is the same message. `reissue_manage_token` therefore stays a tested seam with no caller; its docstring's "F15's edit-phone remedy" is re-pointed (D12).

   **Why only this booking's SMS.** Rotating a sibling's token silently is the safety half and must be unconditional; texting the bride N confirmations for N bookings is spend and noise. A sibling with a pending reminder carries its new token on that reminder (re-pointed above), so her link keeps working. A sibling whose reminder already fired has no live link until the owner taps resend on it — recorded as Risk 9, with the owner-visible cue being that the detail screen already shows `manage_link_issued`.

   **Why the re-point branch rotates only this booking.** On that branch `customers.phone` is never corrected — the digits were not wrong, the *identity* was. The original customer row may be a real other person whose bookings are genuinely hers, so rotating her other bookings' links would revoke credentials nobody proved were misdirected. Each of them is correctable from its own detail screen. Stated as a ruling rather than left as a gap.
4. **The guard, evaluated before anything is written.** Phone correction and resend-link are refused on any booking that is not `confirmed` with `starts_at > now` — 409 `BOOKING_TRANSITION_INVALID` — and, per mechanic 3, the same predicate rides the rotation UPDATE so it cannot go stale mid-operation.

**Rate-limit ordering (review).** The D10 limiter is consulted **before the transaction opens**; `is_blocked` raises `OwnerResendThrottledError` before any write, so a throttled correction changes nothing at all. The draft left this unstated, and the natural reading — meter the send, where the SMS actually is — is the dangerous one: it would commit the corrected number and skip the rotation, leaving a live link pointing at the number the owner just proved is wrong. That state is reachable with no attacker, at 21 taps in an hour.

**Where `record_failure` fires (C4, amended at plan time).** The Gate-1 text said "after the post-commit seam returns", and D10 said "on the success path" — but D11 and `booking/router.py:18-23` put every send **in the router**, so as written the limiter would have to be spent by the router, threading the limiter or a `spend()` hook out of `OwnerBookingService` and into `owner_router.py` for three of the ten routes. **Ruling: the service spends the budget immediately after its own transaction commits, before returning to the router.** Nothing is lost — `_deliver` swallows both provider exceptions and returns `False` (`comms.py:403-435`), so "after the send returns" and "after the commit" differ only in a value nobody branches on, and the thing D10 meters is the owner tap that *caused* a real SMS attempt, which is exactly the committed mutation. D10's substance is untouched: own instance, key `booking:owner_sms:{tenant_id}`, checked **before** the transaction opens so a 429 writes nothing and sends nothing; resend, phone and reschedule metered; cancel not. Declined: a `spend()` call in the router (limiter plumbing in three handlers to move one line).

#### Resend is a rotation (D9)

Same mechanic as D8's rotation (in-transaction mint → `set_manage_token_hash` under the guard predicate → re-point the pending reminder → audit → commit → `send_confirmation`), no phone edit. Resend is not a re-send — it invalidates the old link. Say so in the Hebrew: the only two reasons to resend are a lost message and a wrong number, and both want the old link dead. A plain resend is in any case impossible for a booking whose reminder has already fired: `bookings` stores only the sha256, and `cancel_pending` clears the raw token off the terminal `scheduled_messages` row (`db/repositories/scheduled_messages.py:75-81, 128`), so nothing on the platform can reproduce a sent link. Rotation is the only behaviour available in every case, which makes it the only honest one to offer.

**Rejected finding — no compare-and-swap on the rotation.** Review asked for either an advisory lock around rotate-and-send or an expected-hash predicate on `set_manage_token_hash`, because two rotations seconds apart (a double-tapped resend; a resend racing a correction) interleave into "the bride holds two texts and the older one 404s". That outcome is **the specified behaviour, not a defect**: D9's whole ruling is that every rotation kills the previous link, so two sequential resends produce one live link and one dead one whether they overlap or not, and the row lock on `bookings` already orders the writes so the surviving hash is a real one. The only genuinely bad shape — an *older* rotation's hash landing after a newer one's — is not reachable, because the hash write is what serializes; only the two provider calls can invert, and the bride's newest message then carries whichever token won the write, which is by definition the live one. A CAS would buy a "someone else rotated, do not send" branch that needs its own owner-facing copy for a case whose bad version does not exist. Declined; the client-side mitigation is the one the console already uses everywhere — the button is disabled while its request is in flight. Recorded here so the disagreement survives.

#### One new rate limiter (D10)

Resend, phone-correction **and reschedule** all spend real SMS credit on an owner tap. They share one new `FixedWindowRateLimiter` **instance**, keyed `f"booking:owner_sms:{tenant_id}"`, constructed in `create_app()` from two new `Settings` fields:

| Setting | Default |
|---|---|
| `booking_owner_sms_max_per_tenant_window` | 20 |
| `booking_owner_sms_window_seconds` | 3600 |

**Reschedule joins after review, and cancel deliberately does not.** The draft's own argument — "a stuck button and an impatient owner" spending real credit — applies verbatim to reschedule, which fires `notify_owner_reschedule` on every hop and is **unbounded**: a booking can be walked A↔B↔A between two legitimately offered slots forever, each hop a real non-noop reschedule passing every D5 guard, each hop one SMS to one bride's phone plus a reminder rewrite plus another `manage_token_hash` churn that kills the previous link. Owner cancel stays off the limiter because it is bounded by construction: `cancelled` is terminal (D3), so it is at most one SMS per booking and the ceiling is the number of bookings the boutique has. The limiter is consulted **before the transaction opens** on all three (D8), so a 429 writes nothing and sends nothing.

Its own instance, never a second key on an existing limiter — `max_attempts` lives on the limiter, so a shared budget could never trip first. The rule is stated three times in the codebase (`main.py:346-353`, `booking/manage.py:89-92`, `rate_limit.py:37-39`) and F15 does not become the exception. `record_failure` is called on the **success** path: the class counts only what the caller explicitly records (`app/auth/rate_limit.py:20-26`), so a send-metering caller that records on failure is inert. **It fires in the service, immediately after that service's transaction commits (C4)** — not after the post-commit send returns, because the send belongs to the router (D11) and reaching back into it would put limiter plumbing in three handlers to move one line. A failed send is not a refund: `_deliver` swallows provider exceptions and returns `False`, so there is no post-send value to branch on.

A new `OwnerResendThrottledError` maps to the existing `TOO_MANY_ATTEMPTS_BODY` at 429 — no new error code. The realistic failure here is a stuck button and an impatient owner, not an attacker; 20/hour is a runaway brake far above a boutique's real volume.

**Every send is post-commit, in the router, awaited, and discarded.** The pattern and its reasoning are at `booking/router.py:18-23` and `:95-107`: post-commit because `NotificationService.send_sms` structurally opens its own sessions and a provider hang inside the transaction would block commits; awaited rather than backgrounded so the send happens inside the request's own lifetime; fire-and-forget because turning a committed mutation into a 503 is a lie. No `BackgroundTasks`, no `asyncio.create_task`.

| Action | In the transaction | After commit |
|---|---|---|
| cancel | `cancel(by='owner', not_before=now)` + `scheduled.cancel_pending(REMINDER)` + audit | `notify_owner_cancel(t, booking=updated)` |
| no-show / complete / confirm | `set_status(...)` + audit | **nothing** |
| reschedule | the D5 protocol + `upsert_reminder(session, …)` + audit | `notify_owner_reschedule(t, booking=refreshed)` |

**Owner cancel must cancel the pending reminder itself.** `notify_owner_cancel` does not touch `scheduled_messages` (`comms.py:187-204`), so without this step the customer gets a cancellation SMS and then a reminder for the cancelled appointment. `ManageBookingService.cancel` already does exactly this for the customer path (`manage.py:172-177`) and F15 mirrors it. The worker's claim-time re-check would in fact catch the stray row (`comms.py:342-352`), but relying on the poller's belt when the braces are one line away is the wrong default.

**The reminder rewrite moved into the reschedule transaction (review).** The draft left it post-commit and declined to inline it, on the grounds that inlining "would make `reschedule_reminder` dead code and re-implement its cancel-then-insert upsert". That is a refactoring cost, not a correctness argument, and it contradicts the ruling F16 recorded when it faced the identical choice for the *first* reminder: "Leaving the schedule to a post-commit block would let a crash between commit and block lose the reminder permanently, with nothing sweeping for the gap — same-database work belongs in the transaction" (`service.py:169-173`). Post-commit, two failures are live: a crash after commit leaves the OLD `send_after` on a pending row (the worker then fires a 24h reminder days early, since `drain_due` re-reads the booking for the body but not for the schedule, `comms.py:374-378`) or, in the day-of case, leaves **no reminder at all, permanently**; and `drain_due` can claim the stale pending row with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (`scheduled_messages.py:83-113`) and `mark()` it, which clears `manage_token` (`:128`) — after which the post-commit rewrite mints a *new* token and kills the link in the reminder the bride just received.

**Ruling: the upsert is promoted the same way `_offered_slot` is (D5).** `BookingComms.reschedule_reminder`'s body becomes a module-level `upsert_reminder(session, *, tenant_id, booking_id, starts_at, now, bookings, scheduled) -> datetime | None`, and `reschedule_reminder` becomes the one-line wrapper that opens its own `tenant_session` around it — so F16's tests keep their entry point and the seam keeps its caller, while F15's reschedule calls `upsert_reminder` on the transaction it already holds. No duplicated cancel-then-insert, no second subtlety to keep in sync, and the reschedule is atomic: the pending row with its live token commits with the new `starts_at` or neither exists.

**The ordering rule survives, structurally.** `notify_owner_reschedule` resolves its link through `_live_token`, which reads the pending reminder's `manage_token` (`comms.py:222, 456-461`); when there is nothing pending it falls back to `_rotate`, which mints a token and rotates the booking's hash *without creating a pending row* (`comms.py:463-469`). Independently, the upsert mints its own token and rotates the hash whenever nothing pending was there to inherit from (`comms.py:299-307`). With the upsert now committed before the post-commit send, `_live_token` always finds the row the same transaction wrote — one rotation, and the ordering is enforced by the transaction boundary rather than by two calls in the right sequence. The failing case the draft described is what that boundary prevents; here it is, for the record, in the day-of reschedule where the prior reminder has already fired so nothing is pending:

- **Wrong order** (notify before the upsert): `_rotate` mints token A, the hash becomes A, the customer receives an SMS carrying A. The upsert then finds nothing pending, mints token B, rotates the hash to B. The link in the message she is reading right now is dead — `by_manage_token_hash` no longer matches it (`test_booking_comms_db.py:750-755` proves a rotated token 404s).
- **Correct order** (upsert in the transaction, notify after commit): the pending row carrying a live token is already there, `_live_token` finds exactly that token, and the SMS carries the same link the future reminder will send. One rotation total.
- **Suppression case** (`starts_at` inside the <2h band): the upsert returns `None` having left nothing pending, `_live_token` returns `None`, `_rotate` mints once. One rotation, live link. The correct order works in both branches; the wrong one only works in the branch the tests would reach first.

A `None` from the upsert is **not an error** and must not surface as one — it means the moved appointment is inside two hours and legitimately gets no reminder (D3, pre-decided #6).

**`drain_due` is a third concurrent actor and the ordering proof must say so** (review — the draft's proof modelled only the two calls). A reschedule whose new reminder is due immediately (Q4's "<24h out, send now") commits a pending row the worker can claim, send and `mark()` — clearing `manage_token` (`scheduled_messages.py:128`) — in the milliseconds before `notify_owner_reschedule` runs. `_live_token` then returns `None`, `_rotate` mints again, and the reminder the bride received seconds ago has a dead link. Accepted, not fixed: she is holding two messages sent seconds apart and the **newer** one — the reschedule notice, the one that states the correct time — carries the live link, which is the message she needs. Closing the window would mean holding the send inside the transaction (the one thing `router.py:18-23` forbids) or threading a token through `notify_owner_reschedule`'s shipped signature. Recorded as Risk 10 and covered by a db-marked test asserting the *outcome* (newest link live), not the absence of the race.

**No-show, completed and confirmed send nothing to anyone (D13).** There is no template — `comms_templates.py` holds exactly four bodies, all approved and segment-budgeted. Adding a fifth means a new copy row, the Amendment-40 non-promotional posture and counsel sign-off before a real provider goes live (`sms-foundation.md:161`, Interview Q5). And texting a bride «you did not show up» is a product decision several orders of magnitude larger than a status field. Ruled silent, not deferred. F15 also leaves any pending reminder alone on these transitions: they are guarded on `starts_at <= now`, so the reminder has already fired or the worker's claim-time re-check will flip it.

**A failed send never fails the mutation.** `_deliver` swallows both `SmsSendError` and `SmsNotConfiguredError` and returns `False` (`comms.py:403-435`); the unconfigured case is checked up front and leaves no `message_log` row at all, because `NotificationService` raises before its insert (`notifications/service.py:104-105`, a deliberate F11 ruling). **The console therefore cannot honestly claim an SMS was sent**, and its copy must not — Risk 3.

*(b) The pattern is line-based and every correct formatter in the repo is multi-line.* `BookPage.tsx:82, 88, 132, 136`, `ManageBookingPage.tsx:18, 24` and `packages/ui/src/lib/hours.ts:86` all open `Intl.DateTimeFormat(` on one line and put `timeZone:` on the next.
